## THE WAY GOD WANTS {#cuaderno.10.1}

Jesus Christ instituted the sacraments as visible signs of his living and redeeming presence amongst men. As our Father said, they are his footprints, traces of his divine sojourn on this earth.1 The sacraments are channels through which the grace merited by our Lord in his Passion, Death and Resurrection reach us in superabundance. In each sacrament God comes to meet us in a specific, supernatural way, to give us his life, or to increase it in our souls. When we receive them with the proper dispositions, “we are formed in the likeness of Christ,”2 and his life is reproduced in our lives, in a mysterious yet real way.3 With him we die to sin and rise again to grace. In him we are filled with the Holy Spirit and can call God our Father. Through him our actions, even the most ordinary ones, take on redemptive force. God, always attentive to our needs, wants these divine gifts to accompany us from the first moment of our life until the last. He brings us to new birth through Baptism. He strengthens us through Confirmation and feeds us with the Blessed Eucharist. When we fall, wounded by sin, he forgives us and gives us new strength through Penance, the Sacrament of Reconciliation. And when the moment comes for us to render an account to God, he prepares us for our definitive meeting with him, our Creator, by the Anointing of the Sick.

As well as these five sacraments, there is the sacrament of Holy Orders which seals a special call to be a mediator between God and men, and the sacrament of Matrimony which enables Christian spouses to “signify and share in the mystery of unity and fruitful love between Christ and his Church (cf. Eph 5:32), helping each other to reach holiness in married life and in the procreation, upbringing and education of their children.”4 From the very beginning, the Church’s teaching on marriage has fostered holiness in countless souls and has cooperated decisively in shaping civil society in harmony with the deepest demands of human dignity. So it should not surprise us to see that God’s enemies, who want to uproot every trace of Christianity from society, aim to destroy marriage and the family. Therefore the Church, “aware that the wellbeing of society and her own good are intimately tied to the good of the family (cf. Gaudium et Spes, 47), perceives in a more urgent and compelling way her mission of proclaiming to all people God’s plan for marriage and the family.”5

So marriage was “not instituted or restored by man’s decree, but by God’s.”9 Holy Scripture, the constant teaching of the Church, and the correct use of human reason all recognize in the institution of marriage “a sacred character which is not accidental, but innate; it has not been given by men, but is founded in nature itself.”10 Leo XIII observes that this sacred character was undisputed until “Enlightenment” teachings sought to sever the bond between man and God.11 During the last few centuries a view of marriage with no reference to God has been propagated, in which matrimony is seen as pertaining exclusively to civil legislation. Many even regard it as a private matter between husband and wife, based on a relationship whose content and duration they fix as they please. This idea, so widespread today, is the reason why in many places the dignity of marriage has been obscured by “the plague of divorce, so-called free love, and other disfigurements. In addition,” the Second Vatican Council declares, “married love is too often profaned by selfishness, hedonism and illicit practices against human generation.”12 These evils find their deepest root in the spread of a secularist view of life, which has caused and is still causing grave harm to innumerable souls and leads to civil and moral decline. Every man and woman is free to marry or not, for God obliges no one to get married. But this freedom does not extend to the nature of marriage itself. Human love The Church, in the Second Vatican Council, teaches that “the intimate partnership of life and love,”14 which is marriage, “is rooted in the conjugal covenant, that is, in their irrevocable personal consent.”15 Therefore an essential element of marriage is the consent given and received by each of the spouses, which is an expression of love understood at its truest and deepest level. Chaste and fruitful love Created in the image and likeness of God, man exists at a higher level than other material creatures. His spiritual soul informs and governs his body, with which it forms a substantial unity. And even though God’s image is found chiefly in the soul, the human body also shares in that dignity. Therefore, “sexuality, by means of which man and woman give themselves to one another through the acts proper and exclusive to spouses, is by no means something purely biological, but concerns the innermost being of the human person as such. This is the context, the background, in which the Christian doctrine on sexuality is situated. Marriage, a sacrament of the New Law It is a perennial teaching of the Church, solemnly defined by the Council of Trent2, that “the marriage covenant, by which a man and a woman establish between themselves a partnership of their whole life, and which of its very nature is ordered to the well-being of the spouses and to the procreation and upbringing of children, has, between the baptized, been raised by Christ the Lord to the dignity of a sacrament.”3 Although we do not know the exact moment when Christ instituted the sacrament of marriage, Gospel passages show him restoring marriage to its original dignity4 and sanctifying it with his presence at the wedding feast in Cana.5 In commenting on the latter, a Church Father writes: “In this example we ought to recognize that he is the author of legitimate marriage. The Son of God goes to the wedding to sanctify with the blessing of his presence what he had instituted from of old with his power.”6 The principal testimony in Sacred Scripture on the elevation of marriage to the dignity of a sacrament is found in the Letter to the Ephesians. Here St Paul relates Christian marriage closely to Christ and the Church; the Apostle calls it sacramentum magnum,7 thus emphasizing the sublime nature of the marriage bond contracted between the baptized. As St John Paul II taught: “By means of baptism, man and woman are definitively placed within the new and eternal covenant, in the spousal covenant of Christ with his Church. And it is because of this indestructible insertion that the intimate community of conjugal life and love, founded by the Creator, is elevated and assumed into the

Characteristics of Christian Marriage The sacramental character of Christian marriage does not change the basic elements of the natural institution, but reaffirms, strengthens and elevates them to a higher plane, the supernatural order. Christian marriage is both a contract and a sacrament: the union of one man with one woman, for ever, and a source of grace instituted by Jesus Christ.13 Besides ensuring the continuity of the human race, procreation acquires a higher purpose in the sacrament of marriage, namely, increasing the number of God’s children. Moreover, “The fruitfulness of conjugal love is not restricted solely to the procreation of children, even understood in its specifically human dimension: it is enlarged and enriched by all those fruits of moral, spiritual and supernatural life which the father and mother are called to hand on to their children, and through their children to the Church and to the world.”14 The unity and indissolubility of natural marriage become, thanks to the sacrament, a reflection of the intimate union between Christ and the Church: “By virtue of the sacramentality of their marriage, (Christian) spouses are bound to each other in the most profoundly indissoluble

Effects of the Sacrament of Marriage Like every sacrament of the New Law, marriage is a “memorial, actualization and prophecy”27 of Christ’s redemptive sacrifice on the Cross, and it causes its effects in those who receive it worthily in all these three ways. “As a memorial, the sacrament gives spouses the grace and duty to commemorate the great works of God and bear witness to them before their children. As an actualization, it gives them the grace and duty to put into practice, with one another and with their children, the demands of a love that forgives and redeems. ## ONE AND INDISSOLUBLE {#cuaderno.10.4}

The Christian doctrine on marriage contains a splendid treasury of wisdom and happiness. It could not be otherwise, since “God himself is the author of marriage; he has endowed it with various benefits and ends: all of these have a very important bearing on the continuation of the human race, on the personal development and eternal destiny of every member of the family, on the dignity, stability, peace and prosperity of the family and of the whole human race.”1 God’s loving and inviolable design for spouses establishes that the matrimonial covenant is one and indissoluble: between just one man and just one woman, and for their whole lives. The Church, in an unbroken tradition, teaches that these qualities of a true marriage are of divine and natural law, and a gift of grace given to Christians.2 She also affirms that it is an ideal attainable by all called to this path, although on occasions it requires true heroism. Instituted by the Creator at the beginning of human history, marriage, after the catastrophe of sin, was not only restored by Christ to its original dignity but also raised to the dignity of a sacrament so that married love should be a reflection of God’s love for his creatures and, in the case of Christians, an image of the love wherewith the Incarnate Word loves his Church.3

A Christian model for the family Sometimes it might seem hard for spouses to evaluate their real intentions in the plans they have to build a family. ## GOD’S DESIGN {#cuaderno.10.7}

Crowning the work of creation on the sixth day, the Lord God formed man of dust from the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living being.1 While God was pleased with all his works, he rejoiced in the creation of man. Scripture says that he saw his creation was very good,2 as though to stress God’s special action in creating man in his own image and likeness through the gift of a spiritual and immortal soul. From then on, every single member of the human race has been the object of God’s special love.

## INITIATION IN THE SACRAMENTS {#cuaderno.10.8}

Militia est vita hominis super terram,1 man’s life on earth is a warfare. This struggle means that a constant effort is required to make progress along the way of virtue. Mankind, however, is not left alone in the fight. In his loving Providence, God offers the help of grace which heals and ennobles human nature, enabling it to overcome all obstacles. The sacraments, administered by the Church in Christ’s name and with his authority, are the normal channels of this supernatural power. ## FAMILY AND SOCIETY {#cuaderno.10.18}
