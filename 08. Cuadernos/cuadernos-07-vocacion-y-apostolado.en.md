More than fifty years have gone by since the Work was founded. For an institution that must last as long as there are men on earth, this doesn’t amount to much time. Throughout the world so many people know and love Opus Dei’s spirit. Thanks to the Work’s warmth, persons of all ages and walks of life have spiritually come of age. They partake of the good wine of its spirit and help out in the Work’s apostolic undertakings. ## WHEN GOD CALLS {#cuaderno.7.1}

This is the key to open the door and enter the kingdom of heaven: qui facit voluntatem Patris mei qui in coelis est, ipse intrabit in regnum coelorum; he who does the will of my Father, he shall enter! 1 God has a definite plan for each of us. He does not leave us to a blind fate or set us on an uncharted course. Waiting for us is a path and destiny that will fill all our desires and noblest ambitions.

Called to be saints Our goal is holiness, the fullness of Love. How our Founder emphasized this in the years since 1928! Once again, I set myself this goal and I also remind you and all mankind: this is God’s will for us, that we be saints ... We are capable – sadly capable – of rejecting the goal God has set for us. We can turn away from what will make us happy and instead seek what leads us far from the sanctity of our Father God. ## A REASON FOR SAYING ‘YES’ {#cuaderno.7.2}

If you see your way clearly, follow it. Why don’t you shake off the cowardice that holds you back?1 God’s call requires complete commitment. One’s entire life is affected by that supreme moment when man hears God’s voice and answers ‘yes’. Such a decision, of course, is not to be taken lightly; one would want to see clearly what lies ahead. Only then can the leap of dedication be made for the sake of love and freely, because you want to, as our Founder used to say, which is the most supernatural of reasons. But how can you know for certain that God is calling? In what particular way is a vocation made known to each person? What is it that thousands of people the world over have seen? How could so many have given themselves, risking everything on one card alone? These are the questions posed by a person who finds himself on the threshold of a decision that will change his life into a wonderful, divine, risk-filled adventure. But these same questions can be used to escape from giving oneself completely. Consequently, whoever asks them should examine his conscience thoroughly to see if his doubts are really sincere or not.

## THE FEAR OF SAYING ‘YES’ {#cuaderno.7.3}

When the subject of vocation comes up, it is common for people to ask ‘how do you feel’ the divine calling. Often it is necessary to begin by explaining that vocation is not a special state of mind, more or less intense, urging a person to give himself to God. So much is this the case that if someone were to feel a great enthusiasm, a strong attraction towards giving himself, it might be better to wait a while before making a definitive decision. This is why our Founder used to say that it’s up to the Directors of the Work to bar the doors, to make things hard, so that whoever draws close to the Work ponders with serenity the objective signs of his possible calling. It is not a question of feeling, my children, our Father said in a gettogether. I do not push anyone to come to Opus Dei – on the contrary. Throughout my life I have tried to pose difficulties, not to come with a hook. It’s our Lord who does the fishing, not me.1 Although the divine call does not depend on feelings, it would be surprising if it did not provoke any number of emotions. Our Founder used to list some of these: fear of saying ‘yes’, sadness when hiding from one’s vocation, joy arising from giving oneself.

## A SINGLE CALLING {#cuaderno.7.7}

Opus Dei has come to remind people that God calls everyone to holiness. No longer can holiness be viewed as reserved only to certain groups. Therefore, a person can receive the specific vocation to follow Christ within Opus Dei in the middle of the world, in all kinds of circumstances. Jesus passes by when one is young or in the prime of life; when endless possibilities are opening up, or when one has already become settled in an occupation, in society, in a family. If God calls, one can come to Opus Dei being single, married, widowed or a priest. One can come being rich or poor. One can come being learned or uneducated. One can come being healthy or sick. ## WITH CHARITY AND PATIENCE {#cuaderno.7.21}

In his Gospel, St John has recorded a wonderful phrase of our Lady. At the wedding of Cana, Mary turned to the waiters and said: Do whatever he tells you (John 2:5). That’s what it is all about – getting people to face Jesus and to ask him: Domine, quid me vis facere? Lord, what do you want me to do? (Acts 9:6).1 Above all an apostle must bring his friends to Jesus Christ, so that they may get to know him intimately and fall in love with him. The journey to Christ is long: just how long depends on each person. In any event one has to travel at God’s pace. The Gospel shows us time and again how patient Jesus is with the Apostles. Though he knows their weaknesses and defects, he asks them to follow him. Because he is almighty, he could have filled them with holiness and virtues in a moment, effortlessly. Instead, he forms them little by little. He corrects them. He instructs them. He allows time to make them suitable for the mission he will give them. Finally, on going to Heaven, he sends them the Holy Spirit to complete the work. God acts the same way with those who want to follow him closely. Our Father said that souls, like good wine, improve with time.2 Sacred Scripture teaches that for everything there is a season, and a time for every matter under heaven.3 In his infinite Providence, God foresees

An inclined plane How does one bend iron? asks our Father. It can’t be worked cold. It has to be heated in the furnace until it glows red hot. Then it’s hammered and forged to the desired shape and becomes a graceful filigree. My children, that’s how to handle souls: with the fire of charity and the forge of fortitude.5 The way to Heaven is long. Though God could certainly do so, he does not usually grant graces to bring about holiness immediately and definitively. The gift of grace takes human nature into account and respects it. Just as natural virtues are not cemented by a single action or even several, but rather by many deeds done over time, similarly, to be intimately united to God, one must go little by little, as it were, up an inclined plane. “Therefore, those who are being introduced to a life of virtue must place their feet upon the first steps and climb one step at a time, until by gradual progress they have ascended to a considerable height.”6 Don’t forget, our Father says, that sometimes we must help souls walk little by little. We encourage them to advance slowly, so that they hardly realize they are moving, though in fact they are making progress.7

It is understandable that there will be resistance. Owing to original sin and personal sins, souls find it difficult to respond to what God wants. We must not insist on getting rid of all the difficulties at once. It is Jesus Christ who compels hearts with his grace.8 “All we can do is to pray and help those we have brought close to the Work to remove all the obstacles impeding the action of the Holy Spirit in their souls.”9 Defects and difficulties have to be removed gradually, with gentleness and determination, but controlling one’s impatience. Any tendency to haste must be corrected with supernatural outlook, with the conviction that it is Christ who will act in the most effective way. On our part, there has to be lots of prayer and mortification, a greater effort to foster friendship and trust, and to understand and forgive others from the heart.

Patience and persistence Be patient, therefore, brethren, says the Apostle James, until the coming of the Lord. Behold, the farmer waits for the precious fruit of the earth, being patient until it receives the early and the late rain. You also be patient; strengthen your hearts.10 An apostle must be patient, but with no negligence or laziness. Patience makes us tenaciously persevering, until the desired goal is reached. Our Father wrote: I have learned how to wait, which is a high degree of knowledge. God uses the passage of time to form souls. They cannot be forced, any more than a plant is normally forced to bloom more quickly.11 “Make the effort,” Don Alvaro recommends, “to stoop down with affection, following the progress of souls more closely and patiently.

Nothing is lost When we act with a right intention in the apostolate we have this great assurance: our efforts will never be sterile. No effort we make, provided we act trusting in God, will be fruitless. Nothing we poor mortals do is wasted, when we are really seeking to serve our Lord. If we keep trying constantly to be faithful and to help souls, we will hear the voice of Providence reassuring us: “Do not be afraid; I will give you the increase.” If you have worked generously, if you have really prayed, that promise cannot go unfulfilled, even though we may not see the result immediately in the apostolate.19 Don Alvaro has frequently told us: “If it is God’s will, we will harvest the fruit ourselves. If not, others will. But fruit will always come. In any event, everything is to the good. When we personally reap the fruit, it is good because the fruit is for God. And if we do not reap, it is good too because our work and our sowing is only for God, so we gladly give up the joy of seeing the harvest.”20 With this assurance, we continue to urge our friends on. Ours is true supernatural prudence, which is far from an overly human prudence. We therefore take into account different personal circumstances; we act with great charity and understanding so our friends know we are interested only in their own good. With God’s help, this unselfish attitude will end up moving them, and will enable us to enter more deeply into their lives, overcoming any resistance and helping them to go ahead. To a young person concerned about attracting his friends to the Work, our Founder gave this advice: Think how many people you know of, as do I, who persist in their attempts to pervert persons of your young age, or those who are older, or adolescents.
