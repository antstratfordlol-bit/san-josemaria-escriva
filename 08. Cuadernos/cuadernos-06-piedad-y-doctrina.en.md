## INTRODUCTION {#cuaderno.6.0}

In the Church’s doctrine there are central, basic truths which all Christians should know and practise because they form the basis of solid Christian life. They are what our beloved Founder encouraged us to make into food for our soul, through frequent, devout meditation. One cannot attain interior life, he often pointed out to us, without spending several years striving to make many acts of love for God, offering many mortifications, praying many aspirations. And an effective help to achieve this, as I used to tell your first brothers, can be to dedicate each day of the week to a solid devotion: the Blessed Trinity, the Eucharist, the Passion, Our Lady, St Joseph, the Guardian Angels, the Holy Souls in Purgatory.1 These basic devotions, which the Church has always spread among all kinds of people, will enable us to achieve true piety, which is nurtured by Catholic doctrine, and not by feelings or emotions sought out for their own sake. It is rooted in knowledge of the truths of the Christian faith; knowledge that is as complete as possible, depending on each person’s circumstances. This is why our Father encouraged us to get the most out of all the aspects of our spirit. To get devotion out of them, but theologically-based devotion, founded on the Faith.2 And it is good to pray about the theology we know, so that as a consequence of the obscure light, or luminous darkness, that we find in so many aspects of the faith, our hearts and lips are inspired with affections, acts of 1 St Josemaría, Notes from a get-together, 12 January 1955, in Crónica, August 1958, pp. 7-8 2 St Josemaría, Crónica, 1971, p. 363 8 Piety and Doctrine

hope, the confession of what we believe and what we want others to come to believe. We know that faith, hope and love are not the product of our own actions, but infused virtues freely given by God. 3 This volume of “Notebooks” aims to make this task easier and to help everyone follow faithfully in the footsteps of our Father, who throughout his life always tried to pour out into our souls what God had given him. After making that effort, we find ourselves conversing with God at every moment of the day: we live as contemplative souls.4 All these deeply Christian devotions lead to that as their ultimate goal. Through the intercession of our dearest Father, we ask Our Blessed Lady and St Joseph, principal patrons of the Work, to show us how we can relate to Jesus with piety and doctrine, and to obtain for us the grace to love the Blessed Trinity, Father, Son and Holy Spirit, more each day.

## THE BLESSED TRINITY {#cuaderno.6.1}

Having raised us to the supernatural order, God has chosen to reveal himself to us and also manifest the mystery of his Will,1 which embraces the purpose and content of his life in us, as well as the means for us to attain it. Our Lord spoke, and we received the priceless gift of faith, a true sharing in God’s knowledge, which we would never have reached through our own efforts. In fact, “man could by no effort attain to such wisdom”.2 In many and various ways God spoke of old to our fathers by the prophets; but in these last days he has spoken to us by a Son, Jesus Christ.3 With the coming of Christ we received the fullness of faith and the revelation of the highest mystery, the source and origin of the others: namely, the mystery of the Blessed Trinity, the unity of God in three distinct Persons, the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit.

Substance of the New Testament Under the Old Covenant, this mystery had not been explicitly revealed. In the books inspired by God before the coming of Christ, there are no more than veiled hints, which can only be truly understood in the light of the New Testament. The mystery of the Blessed Trinity is so central to the time of grace inaugurated by Christ that it has been

It was like entering into God’s own intimacy, and seeing the inner richness of God’s life – like a foretaste of Heaven. The whole liturgy began to be filled with this mystery and became a continual act of praise and adoration of the Blessed Trinity. Christians shared in this supernatural, divine knowledge with joy. “The Church understands it, the synagogue does not believe it, philosophy 8 exclaimed St Hilary with holy pride. does not know it,” At the heart of the Creeds and in the decisions of the Councils, the

On the first Sunday after Pentecost, when the divine wonders of creation, the Incarnation, the Redemption and sanctification have all been celebrated, the Church holds a special feast to honour the mystery of the Blessed Trinity.

For this is the fullness of joy, than which there is none greater: to rejoice in God the Trinity, in whose image we were made.”10 The contemplation of the Trinity is the goal and the substance of our life in Christ. Meditating on and loving this mystery, here on earth, while we are travellers, is an anticipation of Heaven. It is our best occupation, since it is not just a means to attain our objective, but also enables us in some way to reach the objective itself. The full, certain assent of faith – through which our understanding firmly and constantly adheres to God who reveals his mysteries to us – fulfils in our souls Our Lord’s astonishing promise: To you it has been

This process begins with our mind, which, guided by the hand of God, by the supernatural virtues and by the gifts of the Holy Spirit, surpasses its natural limitations. But we reach that point only when doctrine and piety go hand in hand, helping one another, when knowledge is guided by love, when our meditation is sustained by our interior life, when our soul moves from intellectual curiosity into prayer, and our piety is solidly based on doctrine.

Unity of Essence and Trinity of Persons “For in one divine nature there are three Persons – the Father, begotten of none; the Son, begotten of the Father before all ages; the Holy Spirit, proceeding from the Father and the Son, likewise, from all eternity. These Three Persons, since it would be impiety to assert that they are unlike or unequal in any thing, are understood to be distinct only in their respective properties. This is what the Apostles received from the lips of the divine Master and transmitted to us. This is what all Christians believed right from the first moment. This is what the Magisterium of the Church has always taught. If God himself had not told us so, we could never have worked it out. Our faith is the light that shows us this truth about God’s inner life, though not by means of step-by-step argument. But afterwards, with the light of faith, reason is able to make things clearer and more precise. That is the task of theology, under the guidance of the Magisterium of the Church, and likewise the task of our formation in religious doctrine, devoutly imparted and devoutly received. Applying our reason But prayer itself ordinarily presupposes doctrine. It means applying our reason to the formulas in which the Church presents the things we should believe, because they can be understood by everyone, even though they require attentive meditation. Anyone can understand why the term “person” can be applied to God. It designates a spiritual being endowed with intelligence and free will. We can understand that this is a perfection that is also appropriate to God, who is the highest Perfection. Since Scripture states that there are three distinct Persons in God, three subjects of attribution, we can understand the formula “three divine Persons”: the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit. We could even understand by the use of reason alone, that the names of Father and Son express opposite relations. The Father is Father because of his relation to the Son, and the Son likewise is the Son in relation to the Father. We can further understand that if there were three divine natures there would be three gods, and therefore there can be only one divine nature. God the Father cannot have anything of his own which is incommunicable – something that the other two Persons do not have – except his fatherhood, which is therefore what makes him a distinct Person. Likewise with the Son and the Holy Spirit, so that the whole personhood of the God the Father consists in fatherhood, that of the Son consists in filiation – being the Son – and that of the Holy Spirit consists in “spiration” – proceeding forth, like breath. We can equally understand that good tends to spread, and that the nobler it is, the more it gives of itself. Thus a living being engenders another living being of the same species. The wise man imparts to

14 St Teresa of Avila, Life, 39, 25 15 The Blessed Trinity

others the wisdom that constitutes his intellectual life, making others as wise as he is, with his same wisdom. And in the same way, at the top of this analogical scale, the Father, by eternally generating the Son, gives him the fullness of the divine nature. The Father and the Son give it to the Holy Spirit. From another angle, we can see that the higher the level of being, the less is lost of what is given, and therefore the resulting union is closer, as many share in the same thing. In this way, when people give money, they lose possession of what they give away. Teachers do not lose their knowledge when they give it to others, but a union of minds is established between them and their students. And at the top of this scale of progressive union, in the Blessed Trinity, the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit are one single God.

Doctrinal piety The mystery of the Blessed Trinity is wholesome food for doctrinal piety. Christianity is not only a way of life, but it is also a doctrine, and sacred doctrine is precisely the rule of our exterior and interior life. A great Saint and doctor of the Church, St Teresa of Avila, said: “I should prefer spirituality to be unaccompanied by prayer than not to be founded upon the truth. Learning is a great thing, for it teaches those of us who have little knowledge, and gives us light, so that, when we are faced with the truth of Holy Scripture, we act as we should. From foolish devotions may God deliver us!”15 Doctrinal, theological formation is doubly necessary for those who have an apostolic mission. As St Paul taught, we have to hold firm to the sure word as taught, so that [we] may be able to give instruction in sound doctrine and also to confute those who contradict it.16 When we

15 St Teresa of Avila, Life, 13, 9 16 Tit 1:9 16 Piety and Doctrine

have to teach others, we need to understand as much as possible of what we propose to explain. We need to learn more and more about what we teach if we want to teach properly, and if we wish to bring what we know within our hearers’ grasp. There is no good apostolate when there is no sure doctrine. The centre and peak of the doctrinal edifice of the Church, and the centre of all Christian life, is the theology of the Blessed Trinity, even though unfortunately many Christians never even bring it to mind, but live and think as if it had not been revealed to us. As apostles with a divine mission, we have to be contemplatives as part of our very vocation. Our activity has to draw its strength from a life of prayer. And in our contemplation, the sublime mystery of the Most Holy Trinity should come first, since this truth is the goal and the substance of our supernatural life. If we give God everything – our name, our life, our love – in a holocaust sacrifice, it is right and necessary for us to try to get to know as much as possible about God, the object of our love,17 our Father told us. And he always recommended us to have the faith of children and the doctrine of theologians.18 The mystery of the Blessed Trinity is like a beginning of eternal life. It lights up the other revealed mysteries from above, and shows us their whole content. It is necessary for our wayfaring souls, because it is the supreme model for supernatural life, which is to share the intimate life of God. It is the rule and measure of our adoptive divine filiation, on which our spirituality is based. It is the model of perfect self-surrender, of self-giving, and of Christian unity, since Jesus besought the Father that we should all be one, as the three Persons are one in God.19

To take another angle, love demands knowledge. I have called you friends, for all that I have heard from my Father I have made known to you.20 A friend rejoices in the life and the happiness of his divine Friend. Finally, learning about this mystery is essential in order to think and to write devoutly about the things of God. Without such contemplation, people would write books on doctrine without any piety, or books on piety without any doctrine. It is also essential in order to raise our lives on earth up to their true supernatural level, since the human objectives we pursue, even though necessary, are insignificant by comparison. What is of fundamental importance is our union with God.

To go deeper in the Faith In the light of faith, guided by the Magisterium of the Church, and nourished by sincere, solid piety, human reason is capable of reaching a progressively deeper and more fruitful knowledge of this inexpressible truth. Of course, however deeply we go into it we will never be able to understand this great mystery properly. Sometimes, our Father used to say, we get a bit of light; but then comes a great deal of darkness. That makes me very happy, my children; because if I could understand the mystery of the Blessed Trinity merely by my own efforts, how small God would be! So I am quite content to wait for the moment of the Beatific Vision, when this faith, this Love, and this hope, that our Lord has given me, will come to their full fruition.21 A lack of humility often leads to concentrating excessively on the darkness of the faith, without noticing the glorious light which that darkness emphasizes. Faith is something very positive. It is not just a test, a demand for submission. Furthermore, part of what is usually

called the darkness of faith is simply the darkness of human ignorance, and we should do all we can to dispel it. God will remove the remainder of the darkness in Heaven. Faith is the basis of our hope as well as our love. Some Fathers and Doctors of the Church coined an analogy, based on certain passages of Sacred Scripture, that perhaps helps us to understand the mystery of the Blessed Trinity as much as it can be understood here on earth; but always in an analogous way, that is, similar and dissimilar at the same time. It is the analogy with the operations of the human soul. Because they are spiritual, our souls, and also the Angels, have two vital operations: knowing and loving. Since God is the most perfect spiritual Being, he must also have these two operations, to the most perfect degree, by his essence. By the act of knowing that is his very essence, God knows; he knows himself. In that knowledge, the Father engenders a concept from all eternity, expresses the Word – the idea, the logos, the inner word. This is the procession of the Word conceived by God, the Son begotten by the unbegotten Father. That is why the Word is called the unblemished Mirror, the Image of the invisible God, the Splendour of his glory, the Figure of his substance. And this Word can truly be called Son because he is conceived by the Father in equality of nature, because only by truly being God can he properly – perfectly – express the knowledge God has of himself. Love follows on from knowledge. When we know something, if it is something good, it awakens love in us in proportion to its goodness. This love that proceeds from our will is an impulse toward the beloved being, a force, a living breath which connects us to the other, which unites us, binds us, captivates us. Similarly, following on from that infinite and eternal act of knowledge by which the Son is engendered as the Word, the Father and the Son love the infinitely perfect divinity, and from this act of love, as from a single principle, there proceeds, from 19 The Blessed Trinity

all eternity, this divine breath, the impulse of love, or “spirated” love, which is the Holy Spirit, the Third Person of the Blessed Trinity, the bond of God with God – bonding together the Father and the Son in one common spiration – who closes, as it were, the cycle of the intratrinitarian operations, in a unity that is perfect, infinitely active and complete, fruitful and joyous.

This solid doctrinal devotion, divine light and love, was always in our Father’s soul, and forms part of the spirit of Opus Dei. It could not be otherwise, because it is not just another devotion: it is the very foundation of all devotions and practices of Christian piety Learn to praise the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit. Learn to be especially devoted to the Blessed Trinity. I believe in God the Father, I believe in God the Son, I believe in God the Holy Spirit: I believe in the Blessed Trinity. I love God the Father, I love God the Son, I love God the Holy Spirit: I love the Blessed Trinity. ## OUR FATHER GOD {#cuaderno.6.2}

Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who has blessed us in Christ with every spiritual blessing in the heavenly places, even as he chose us in him before the foundation of the world, that we should be holy and blameless before him. He destined us in love to be his sons through Jesus Christ.1 In these solemn terms, St Paul revealed to Christians the mystery of their adoptive divine filiation. This reality, hidden in God’s from all eternity, was revealed in the fullness of time, when God sent forth his Son, born of woman, born under the law, to redeem those who were under the law, so that we might receive adoption as sons.2 As our Father taught us, The God we believe in is not a distant being, looking down with indifference upon the fate of men – their endeavours, their struggles, their griefs. He is a Father who loves his children so much that he sends the Word, the Second Person of the Blessed Trinity, so that, by taking on the nature of man, he may die for us and redeem us. He is the same loving Father who now attracts us gently towards himself, through the action of the Holy Spirit who dwells in our hearts.3

The Mystery of the Word The Holy Spirit inspired Christ’s Beloved Disciple with words which summarize the visible mission of the Word Incarnate on earth. This saving mystery, hidden for centuries, is now a light to guide us. He was in the world, and the world was made through him, yet the world knew him not. He came to his own home, and his own people received him not. But to all who received him, who believed in his name, he gave power to become children of God; who were born, not of blood nor of the will of the flesh nor of the will of man, but of God. And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, full of grace and truth; we have beheld his glory, glory as of the only Son from the Father.4 Neither the speculations of the wise nor the power of the mighty can do anything to snatch from us this truth that nourishes our joy and our hope. Only the little ones, the truly poor, the weak and humble of this world, were able to make room in their souls for this astonishing revelation by God. God revealed to them the great love he had for them; how, through the redeeming mission of the Word, he had decided to renew and strengthen the great mystery of adoptive filiation. Children of God in Jesus Christ Christ revealed to his own the treasures of our adoptive filiation. See what love the Father has given us, exclaims St John, that we should be called children of God; and so we are.8 This is God the Father’s plan, this is the dignity to which he has chosen to raise us: that we should feel the joy, the holy pride of bearing the name of God’s children. Still more than that: it was that we were to be God’s children in fact, that a change would be worked in us, that we might really be made partakers of the divine nature.9 And so the name “children” expresses a real quality of our souls. “Divine adoption exceeds human adoption, in that God, by bestowing his grace, makes the man whom he adopts worthy to receive the heavenly inheritance; whereas man does not make him whom he adopts worthy; but rather in adopting him he chooses one who is already worthy.”10 Our divine filiation is called adoptive in order to distinguish it from the natural divine filiation that belongs to the Word alone. “At the Last Supper on the night when he was betrayed, our Saviour instituted the Eucharistic Sacrifice of his Body and Blood. ## THE GUARDIAN ANGEL {#cuaderno.6.10}

The universe created by God had a wonderful unity right from the start. In the divine plan, from the first moment, angels, men and irrational creatures, each with their specific nature, formed a harmonious whole that sprang from the Love of God and was directed to his glory. The same unity in the natural order exists in the supernatural order to which God gratuitously raised angels and men. This integral harmony of the universe has its foundation in the unity of God, in whose perfection all creatures participate in some way according to a hierarchy of dignities: angels, men and irrational creatures. It is an order that reflects the fullness of God, whose inaccessible unity is imitated by a harmoniously ordered multitude.

Angels, ministers of God God ordinarily uses secondary causes in his care and government of all creatures, leading them in an orderly fashion to their proper end. As a result, there is an active and effective relationship between all orders of being. Since angels are pure spirits, immaterial and incorporeal, they are the most perfect creatures in all creation. On the one hand, their intelligence acts with a simplicity and acuteness of which man is incapable; and their will is more perfect than man’s. On the other hand, already raised to the beatific vision, they are glorified creatures who see God face to face. This greater excellence in nature and in grace is what makes the angels God’s ordinary ministers, capable, in a certain way, of 136 Piety and Doctrine

influencing men and lesser beings. Man first witnessed this orderly plan willed by God for the execution of his designs when the angel drove Adam and Eve out of Paradise.1 At the most crucial moments of human history, an angel, appearing in bodily form, has been God’s minister before men, in order to announce a divine plan, to point out a pathway, or to transmit God’s will. In Sacred Scripture there are so many instances that we could say that almost the entire path of history up to the coming of Christ is traced by the hand of angels. The name angel, meaning “messenger”, expresses their function of ministering, as God’s messengers to men. The Chosen People always venerated and respected angels, as created beings blessed by God, and they were reverenced with a true cult known as dulia. Together with the angels’ clear intervention in events which have affected the destiny of a kingdom, an army, a people, or the whole of mankind, there is another quiet and continuous intervention in the personal lives of men. ## HOLY MOTHER CHURCH {#cuaderno.6.11}

From all eternity, God the Father, wishing to have mercy on mankind, “determined to call together in a holy Church those who should believe in Christ. Already present in figure at the beginning of the world, this Church was prepared in marvellous fashion in the history of the people of Israel and in the old Alliance. Established in this last age of the world, and made manifest in the outpouring of the Spirit, it will be brought to glorious completion at the end of time.”1 To fulfil this eternal decree of the Father, with the cooperation of the Holy Spirit, the Word became flesh and dwelt among us.2 Christ reconciled earth with Heaven, accomplishing the work of our redemption and sanctification: he pardoned sins, taught the doctrine of life and gave himself up to death for love of us. ## LITURGICAL LIFE {#cuaderno.6.12}

By his life, his preaching, and especially by his Passion, Death and glorious Resurrection, Jesus Christ carried out his work “of redeeming mankind and giving perfect glory to God”.1 When Christ ascended into Heaven, he entrusted to his Church the task of applying the merits he had acquired for us, to each individual throughout all time. To accomplish this mission, he sent the Apostles (and their successors) all over the world, “that they might preach the gospel to every creature (cf. Mark 16:15) and proclaim that the Son of God by his death and resurrection had freed us from the power of Satan (cf. Acts 26:28) and from death, and brought us into the Kingdom of his Father. But he also willed that the work of our salvation which they preached should be set in train through the sacrifice and Sacraments, around which the entire liturgical life revolves.”2 Right from the beginning, the liturgy has been at the centre of the Church’s life. Of course, it is by no means all that the Church does, since for people to be brought to the liturgical life, they must first be converted and have faith. Nevertheless “the liturgy is the summit toward which the activity of the Church is directed; it is also the fount from 3 which all her strength flows.”

## THE HOLY SOULS IN PURGATORY {#cuaderno.6.15}

While still journeying in this world, the Church always looks forward to the definitive, perfect union with God which will only take place in Heaven. And as a loving Mother, she watches over all her children. Anxious for all souls to achieve eternal happiness, the Church assists them with the means of salvation she received from Jesus Christ, especially the Sacraments. But the Church does not forget those sons and daughters of hers who, having rendered their souls to God, are still in Purgatory, undergoing purification before being admitted into Heaven. “In full consciousness of this communion of the whole Mystical Body of Jesus Christ, the Church in her pilgrim members, from the earliest days of the Christian religion, has honoured with great respect the memory of the dead; and, because it is a holy and a wholesome thought to pray for the dead that they may be loosed from their sins (2 Macc 12:46), she offers suffrages for them.”1

Suffrages for the dead The souls in Purgatory cannot do anything themselves to get to Heaven sooner. They cannot earn merits, receive Sacraments, gain indulgences, or offer satisfaction. Our good friends The state of the Holy Souls in Purgatory is very different from the state they had here on earth while still united to their bodies. Hence their way of acting is different from the natural way of acting on earth. For example, they do not carry out operations of the senses, they do not have sense-powers (memory or imagination of perceptible things), which necessarily involve the body. Nevertheless, they still possess their purely spiritual faculties: intelligence and will, as well as acquired knowledge and the other virtues which pertain to their spiritual powers. As many theologians teach, following the doctrine of St Thomas Aquinas, the Holy Souls in Purgatory know not only what is universal, but also particular realities, thanks to the ideas God infuses into them.28 Hence, even in Purgatory, they remember their loved ones back on earth, and they pray for us.
