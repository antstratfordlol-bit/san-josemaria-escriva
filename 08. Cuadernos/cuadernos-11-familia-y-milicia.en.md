## INTRODUCTION

To struggle, my son, is to be continually in battle.1 Through a specific, personal vocation the Lord has called us to Opus Dei. He has placed us in the frontline, where we fight a continual battle: you against yourself, I against myself,2 a battle in which we never fight alone. We feel protected at all times by the covering fire of a powerful weapon: the prayer of the entire Work. Through the Communion of Saints we are part of a very compact army. We feel the strength of other souls who live with the same aspirations and desires as ours, who fight day after day with the same objective, and who, over and beyond differences of culture, place or time, also rely on our supernatural support. The Work, at the same time as it is an army, is a family united by bonds stronger than blood-ties, with a union of hearts, of wills, of desires…3 From the first moment, those who came close to our Father experienced the affection of a fatherly and motherly heart. Then, in the Grandmother’s house, they found the same atmosphere of a Christian home that now breathes in our Centres and wherever there is a member of Opus Dei, raising the spiritual temperature of those who surround him.4

## FROM WHOM ALL FATHERHOOD PROCEEDS {#cuaderno.11.1}

By our birth to supernatural life as children of God through baptism, we have been received into the Church, “God’s family”.1 This very large family, “a multitude gathered together in the unity of the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit,”2 has a common father on earth, the Pope. Within the Church and in service to the Church, God our Lord has wanted Opus Dei to be a small family that is very united, even though we may be spread out everywhere.3 And he has wanted our spiritual life to be firmly rooted in the sense of our divine filiation, a specific manifestation of which is our filiation to our Founder and to his successors, to the Father. “Thanks to the very special fatherhood that God granted Blessed Josemaría in order to found Opus Dei, the Prelature of the Holy Cross and Opus Dei is a true family with supernatural bonds. Grounded on this fatherhood, which all our Father’s successors will share in until the end of time, with God’s grace the Work will always have this family spirit that is consubstantial to it.”4 Filiation to the Father is for us a sure path for being good children of God in the Church. Filiation and fraternity We read in the Gospels how on various occasions Jesus greets his apostles and disciples affectionately as children, little children.24 He who is the Person of the Son reveals to us that I and the Father are one,25 showing us the paternal sentiments in his human heart. We, too, who have been introduced into this mystery of Unity in Trinity as adopted sons in the Son, must have the same mind which was in Christ Jesus.26 Knowing oneself to be a child of God in Christ, sharing in his Filiation, entails a life, so to speak, immersed in the heart of the Father, totally imbued with his fatherhood. Therefore, together with affection for, confidence in and obedience to the Father and the Directors, so appropriate to the children of God in his Work, this same spirit will move us always to look at those around us, especially our brothers, sisters, with understanding, merciful and attentive eyes, as a reflection of God’s fatherly gaze. Don’t forget, our Founder wrote, that each one of you, as well as being a sheep in this fold, is also in some way the Good Shepherd.27 The heart of a child of God also has to be, in some way, the heart of a father. To put it another way, while knowing ourselves to be small children of God, we must act as older brothers, sisters, who share in their parents’ responsibility. Christ, with whom we seek to identify ourselves, is the Only-Begotten Son of the Father and the first-born among many brethren.28 The fraternity that we live in Opus Dei has all these nuances proper to adoptive divine filiation, which in turn is grounded in the mystery of the Blessed Trinity and in the missions of the divine Persons.

## THE CENTRE OF OUR HOME {#cuaderno.11.2}

Our beloved Father’s great faith and love for Jesus Christ really present in the Eucharist is a precious inheritance for all his children. It is a legacy that must bear more and more fruit in our lives, and in the lives of those we know. Now that the truth of Christ’s presence in the sacramental species is under so much attack, I will not tire of repeating it. Jesus Christ, Son of Holy Mary ever Virgin, is in the Tabernacle. He who was conceived in her womb; who worked quietly in Nazareth, after his humble birth in Bethlehem; who preached and suffered the Passion and Death on the Cross; who rose again and ascended into heaven.1 Our whole faith comes into play, our Father affirmed, when we believe in Jesus, in his real presence under the appearances of bread and wine.2 And he added: If our Lord had remained on earth physically and we could now see him hiding his divinity but not his humanity, we would be overcome with wonder. Well, his love has been even greater: he has wished to stay here in such a way that he hides not only his divinity but his humanity as well. He is in the Eucharist like a thing, and does so to our advantage.3

Eucharistic heart The heart of the spirit of the Work is eminently Eucharistic.4 This is why we can never be satisfied with our signs of affection for Jesus, enclosed in the tabernacle. In one of his letters as Father, Don Javier told us: “We all have to make sure that our Eucharistic piety becomes ever more intense: he awaits our response! Let’s remember the popular saying, ‘A well brought up person is always grateful.’ As we look at Jesus in the Sacred Host, let us pause to consider how defenceless he is, waiting for us… Seeing such a wonderful outpouring of love, surely we are going to respond with all possible affection and tenderness?”5 One of our Founder’s great joys was establishing many tabernacles so that in them Jesus might be… contemplated. This is the right word, because someone contemplated is someone loved...6 With what love our Father prepared everything for the first tabernacle we had in Opus Dei! What care he took so that our Lord would find himself at home among us! Then down the years his joy was renewed over and over again, “whenever he learned that somewhere in the world a new tabernacle had just been installed. For years he counted the Centres of the Work by tabernacles. More than the physical fact of another Centre, what mattered to him was that Jesus would be sacramentally present and worshipped in a new tabernacle.”7 This is why, in spite of the financial straits that accompany our apostolic work, “following a blessed tradition, we have always given Him the best room in the house (…); the best we had, and even what we did not have, we have always devoted to his worship.”8

## RESEMBLING OUR FATHER {#cuaderno.11.3}

To be Opus Dei means carrying out God’s plan, who chose us in him before the foundation of the world, that we should be holy and without blemish in his sight,1 by following this “way to holiness through daily work and the ordinary duties of a Christian”2 where he himself has put us. Our vocation, my children, requires us to work intensely and cheerfully all our life. And with God’s grace, we are all prepared to be heroic, whenever the service of God and souls so requires. Now his successor continues being the shadow of our Father, the visible instrument with which he continues watching over the Work from heaven. “Blessed Josemaría is and will always be by antonomasia the Father in Opus Dei: the one who engendered us in Christ for this specific vocation in the heart of the Church, with his most faithful response, with his exemplary dedication to God’s plans: he is the model of total identification with Jesus Christ which the Blessed Trinity wants for his children in Opus Dei. This is why we can listen to those words of the Apostle, as if they came from his lips, directed to each one of us: Be imitators of me as I am of Christ (1 Cor 11:1).”16 It is a confirmation of that graphic lesson Blessed Alvaro gave us, when our Father pointed out to him some tiny matter in which it wasn’t necessary to imitate, reminding him that he only had to imitate Christ: “Yes, Father, but by the regulatory channel.” During the nineteen years that he governed the Work, his first successor continued being the “regulatory channel” through which our Father made us hear his shepherd’s calls to us, to point out the way to the freshest and most tender pastures. The Father has now taken on the baton and, from the very day of his election, he has frequently repeated a consideration to awaken in us a greater sense of responsibility and the desire to be ever more closely united to our Founder in heaven: “Turn over and over in your mind, every day, the thought that the Work is in our hands and that we have to transmit it to others with the same force and holiness with which God made our Father see it and carry it out, and Blessed Alvaro after him.”17 With his words, with his indications of government, with his physical presence at our side whenever possible, he straightens our paths so that we can serve God and the Church better, spurring on our

A marvellous symphony There are dates when the sense of filiation becomes particularly alive in all the sons and daughters of the Father: dates like his birthday, the anniversaries of his First Communion, his “whistling”... “My sons,” Blessed Alvaro told us on the third anniversary of his election, “more than your Auguri (Best wishes) – which I deeply appreciate – I need you to say to me: Father, we are praying for you a lot, but from now on we are going to pray more… I am in absolute need of your prayers so as to be God’s good instrument.”19 Now too the Father asks insistently for the support of our prayer. Omnia bona pariter cum illa The spiritual growth of each of us is also nourished by this affection and prayer for the one who is at the head of the Work. Our Lord has arranged for many graces to reach us through his words, his indications, the advice he gives us. That is what Blessed Alvaro told us: “If I am to be the aqueduct, the channel for the specific spirit of Opus Dei to reach you – in the sense of conserving it and transmitting it such as I have received it – it is clear that I must not be an obstacle. If the aqueduct were to get blocked (…) that would be disastrous.”26 The spirit of filiation ought to move us to pray continuously for the intentions of the Father, who places so much trust in our support: “I don’t mind sounding repetitive and insistent in my request.” Time and again he tells us, “Intensify your prayers of petition and your mortification for my intentions.”27 Praying for the Father is also an inexhaustible fountain of graces for the whole of the Work and for each one of his children. Omnia bona pariter cum illa:28 numerous good things derive from it. This unanimous prayer keeps us united, like the scales of a pine cone around the one who is at the head of the Work, and united in this way we find ourselves clothed in the fortitude of the others.

Our Founder pointed out another reason which ought to push us to pray unceasingly for the one at the head of the Work: Everything painful that can occur in the Work, reaches me: the worries of each member, noble and pure human suffering, the injustices that some of my children undergo, ill-treatment, slander... Everything comes to me. And it makes us suffer. Help me; since I am helping you. I will never leave you to suffer alone.29 We too want not to leave the Father alone. We would like to say to him, opening our heart in trusting and filial conversation, that he can rest upon our shoulders, because if our strength should fall short, God’s charity does not. United to his intentions allows one to live the Communion of Saints with extraordinary effectiveness. Our prayer makes itself present where it is most needed, and horizons are widened, while unity is ever stronger, and our work always more fruitful; our joy will be greater, and our union with God more intimate.30 May our Mother Holy Mary obtain for us the grace to increase our union with the Father, in a practical and concrete way, in all the incidents of our day: in the easy moments and in difficult ones too, at work and at rest, in sickness and in health, in joys and in sorrows... You’ll see how gentle, how easy that which was going to be unpleasant becomes, if the thought comes to you: “Let’s offer this for the Father’s intentions.” Instead of getting upset, you’ll find yourself smiling. Perhaps you’ll feel annoyed at first, but the smile will come, a smile full of peace and joy. And meanwhile, our Lord will be uniting us in a union of intentions and affection, because there is unity in prayer and sacrifice.31
