# Letter 7

*On the work of St Raphael, for the Christian formation of young people; also known by its incipit Quem per annos, dated 24 October 1942 and sent on 22 November 1966*

> **A translation, and whose.** English by Brian McCarthy for the Lismulin Educational Foundation, for private circulation. It is not the official text: opusdei.org has published no English of this letter. Where the wording carries the argument, the Spanish is the text — `carta-07-19421024.es.md`, the Rialp critical edition.

*Madrid, 24 October 1942*

### 1 {#carta.19421024.1}

For many years now, and in a thousand different ways, we have been carrying out an apostolic work with young people which has opened up a fertile furrow and has been very fruitful in the service of the Church and for the good of souls. Because, as I have often reminded you, my dearest daughters and sons, our work of St Raphael is a haven of peace and magnanimous service, even in the midst of great national and international conflicts.

But our Lord wants us to do more, and there is undoubtedly a great deal to be done. The objective of St Raphael’s work with young people is very ambitious, because its field of action is immense, and allows of “ways of working” that can never be exhausted. And at the same time there are also many individuals hard at work corrupting those who are trying to take their first steps in life.

### 2 {#carta.19421024.2}

Our St Raphael’s work is aimed at *select* young people of all social conditions, students or not, without distinctions of any sort. Don’t be surprised that I use the word “select”, although we are interested in all souls without exception. Precisely because of that, if one wants to reach everybody one has to pay special attention to the better ones. Every day at Mass they are included in my intentions as I pray with outstretched hands over the offerings: *et in electorum tuorum iubeas grege numerari*: grant, Lord, to number us among your *chosen*, among the elect.

*Without forming an association*, the young fellows take part in the activities that are held *in and from* our houses. This allows us to give them a good supernatural and human formation, while bringing them closer to the Work, providing them with the means to form their character, and teaching them to fight and to conquer in the ascetical struggle.

Through this apostolate we offer the basic spirit of the Work to a great number of people. Through the example of our lives and our word we help them improve their personality, to use their freedom well, to deepen their knowledge of the faith, and to acquire the christian concern to serve souls for the Love of God.

### 3 {#carta.19421024.3}

The immediate purpose of St Raphael’s work, therefore, is the integral formation of all those who take part in it. They should be aware that they are participating actively in something very important and which requires an active response, so that afterwards they can become good parents or, if God wills, souls completely “dedicated” to his service. And so they are expected to take it all seriously: it requires a *basic level of commitment* and a sense of responsibility.

If a student, during his university years, behaves like a spoiled teenager, that is, in an irresponsible or infantile fashion, he cannot be expected to become an adult all at once as soon as he finishes his studies, by the mere fact of getting a degree or a professional qualification.

The same is true of boys of girls from any social background. “Truly, an abandoned youth is like uncultivated ground, which only produces thorns”. This is our mission: to form finished men and women, to cultivate that ground and make it fruitful.

### 4 {#carta.19421024.4}

And so, as regards personality development, the first thing we try to instil in the girls and boys of St Raphael is a great sense of responsibility, making them see the serious obligation they have to study or work, and helping them to sanctify themselves in the fulfilment of this fundamental duty. This way their young hearts acquire that modicum of human virtue which is a necessary foundation for its supernatural equivalent.

This also teaches them a very characteristic feature of the Work, namely, a readiness to understand others, to forgive, to live together. This spirit of genuine christian charity helps them to be fraternal in practical ways, both with one another and with everybody else, and to acquire a genuine desire to do apostolate with their friends and companions.

### 5 {#carta.19421024.5}

The first spiritual result of all this, generally right from the start, is to get them to frequent the sacraments. And if the means of St Raphael’s work are properly used, they receive a solid doctrinal formation, they learn to be souls of prayer, to live in the presence of God in the ordinary affairs of their day, to give a christian meaning to their work — intellectual or manual — and to have a spirit of sacrifice.

In a word, they learn to have a life of piety which is strong and deep, to love in a special way the Blessed Trinity, the Blessed Virgin, the Holy Church, the Pope, and the Work; and to show through their behaviour that they are seeking unity of life, fighting to make their actions match their faith, and using their work as a means and occasion of apostolate.

When we see in young people a false spirit of self-sufficiency and a lack of respect for parents and teachers — “geriatrics”, as they say — we begin to appreciate and love St Raphael’s work more. As St John Chrysostom puts it: “Infancy and childhood are followed by adolescence, in which blow those impetuous winds, like in the Aegean, as concupiscence begins to grow.

“It is the age at which correction is least easily borne, not only because passions are stronger, but because sins are less rebuked, because teachers and instructors have disappeared. When the winds are stronger and the pilot is weaker and there is nobody to lend a hand, consider how great must be the shipwreck”.

### 6 {#carta.19421024.6}

I have always reminded you, my children, that our aim is to serve. “In order to be useful, serve”. We need, therefore, an upright lifestyle, a clear understanding of the faith, and the constant and loving practice of our “Norms” as children of God in his Work.

In this way, and only in this way, can all this work we carry out with young people be imbued with a lofty spirit of service. It is a service on a number of grounds: firstly to the individuals themselves; then to the Church, because we are preparing them to be faithful to her; and finally to society, because we are fashioning model citizens, Christians who will act uprightly in their professional and social lives.

I am very conscious of the apostolic desire and the dedication which moves you to look after and form the St Raphael fellows well, and I thank God for it. It is a work that never ceases, which requires a special zeal for souls and love of God, patience, and limitless goodness, in keeping with that maxim of St Paul: *Love is patient and kind*.

### 7 {#carta.19421024.7}

The work of St Raphael is the seed-bed of Opus Dei. It is the ordinary means which God’s grace counts on to foster future vocations, and not to nurture it would be to tempt God and oblige him to grant extraordinary graces.

It is logical that this would be so, because the spiritual and human formation the boys get, at an age when they are already mature enough to be fully conscious of what the christian life entails — without leaving their normal place in the world — is sufficient to enable them to accept the divine call to the Work and to be, from the moment they ask for the Admission, effective instruments under their Directors’ guidance.

There is of course a very close bond between the St Raphael fellows and the Work. In practice they form part of the supernatural family of Opus Dei: they willingly want to receive its warmth, to acquire at least its basic spirit, and to cooperate with the members of the Work in their spiritual enterprise.

### 8 {#carta.19421024.8}

The boys and girls of St Raphael, therefore, should be the foremost object of your apostolic activity. May God our Lord increase your love for this work and your supernatural outlook daily more and more, so that your zeal ranges forever wider without diminishing its intensity, helped along by those very souls you are forming.

Let me remind you now of some words of St Augustine: “Your faith should see everything in relation to God. Love God above all things, raise yourselves towards God, and everybody you can, drag them towards God. Son, wife or slave, drag them towards God. If he is a stranger, draw him towards God. Your enemy, push him towards God. Drag him, drag him towards God; because, if it is towards God that you bring him, he will not be your enemy for long”.

According to Cassian, “there are three classes of vocation. To explain these classes of vocation clearly, we will say that the first comes from God, the second through a man, and the third by necessity. The second class of vocation, as we have said, is obtained through a man: when through the example or exhortation of some saints, we are enkindled in the desire for salvation”.

### 9 {#carta.19421024.9}

Winning new vocations is the best proof of love for souls. I have always told you that each one of us, after entrusting everything to our Lord, ought to bring at least two vocations each year, carrying out the divine command *compelle intrare: compel them to come in*, which implies an invitation, a help to make up one’s mind, but never any sort of coercion.

Since respect for people’s freedom is a principal characteristic of our spirit, the *compelle intrare* you employ to win new apostles is not in any sense a physical push, but an abundance of light and doctrine; the spiritual stimulus of your prayer and work, which is a genuine testimony to that doctrine; all the sacrifices you offer; the smile that comes to your lips because you are children of God: a filiation that fills you with a serene happiness that others see and envy, even though at times you find the going rough. To all this add your courteousness and your attractive human qualities, and there we have the content of the *compelle intrare*.

Don’t be surprised, even though in our case we’re not religious nor do we observe common life, that St Augustine would speak like this: “I came to this city to see a friend whom I was hoping to win for God, with the objective of getting him to become a monk in our monastery”.

### 10 {#carta.19421024.10}

Nevertheless, although we desire that our Lord produces many and good vocations, I insist that we seek in the first place the spiritual welfare of all souls, without exception, who come in contact with our apostolate with good dispositions.

And so we don’t let anybody drift away simply because he or she doesn’t appear to be suitable for the Work. We always bear in mind that, although only some will receive this special grace from God, we are interested in all souls.

It shouldn’t surprise you that some will drift away, in spite of the preliminary work of selection and friendship. We can’t expect everybody to understand us: *Not all men can receive this saying, but only those to whom it is given*. When this happens, don’t get discouraged: keep on working, with the same effort and the same happiness as always.

### 11 {#carta.19421024.11}

If they go away, it means that they aren’t ready, at least for the time being, to work with the spirit of sacrifice that a genuine christian lifestyle requires; or that our Lord wants them somewhere else. In any event, it will always have done them some good. Be certain that you haven’t been working in vain: *knowing that in the Lord your labour is not in vain*.

Moreover, the work of St Raphael should provide many vocations, Cooperators and helpers for the work of St Gabriel. There are always lots of worthwhile young people, who are held in high esteem by their colleagues, who understand and love the Work and who are not called by God or who don’t have all the conditions to become Numeraries.

There can also be special circumstances which make it clear that it is our Lord’s will that they follow another way. And so, in order to help them effectively, it is very important to get to know them well, through the personal apostolate of friendship and trust, and giving each one as much time as he or she requires. And not infrequently when the time comes to speak to them about vocation it will be necessary to direct them towards the work of St Gabriel.

### 12 {#carta.19421024.12}

You should have as your objective that in their first youth or in full adolescence they would feel moved to follow an ideal: to seek Christ, to find Christ, to know Christ, to follow Christ, to love Christ, to remain with Christ.

One has to wait patiently for divine grace to start working in their souls, that the “hour” of the encounter with the Master comes and that they have the generosity and the sensitivity to follow his voice — *come, follow me —* renouncing many things which are lawful for others.

From the start of the work of St Raphael, with those first fellows I used to bring to my mother’s house, I have been reminded of the hunger for truth of the apostle St John, which is the same hunger that the adolescents and good-living youth of all times have felt on opening their eyes to the reality of the world around them.

### 13 {#carta.19421024.13}

With this desire to learn they joined the wandering circle of the Baptist’s disciples. Jesus passed by, *he looked at Jesus as he walked*, and the Baptist proclaimed, *Behold, the Lamb of God!* Two of the Baptist’s disciples, John and Andrew, heard these words *and they followed Jesus*.

Afterwards our Lord asked them, *What do you want?* And the answer, not altogether logical, *Rabbi, where are you staying?* Jesus said to them: *Come and see*. John and Andrew went, they saw where he was living, and they remained with him the rest of that day.

Their conversation must have been lengthy, and love must have sunk deep into John’s young heart. When he recounts his divine adventure many years later, those pages of the Gospel are as fresh and fragrant as an affectionate diary. *It was about four o’clock in the afternoon*, he writes. He recalls the very moment when Jesus, *seeing that they were following him*, invited them to come with him.

### 14 {#carta.19421024.14}

The work with those young friends who seek to follow Christ is very effective: they move discreetly and naturally among others of their own condition, living our spirit with refinement and love and carrying with them *the aroma of Christ* wherever they go. Their cooperation in the work of St Raphael is a great help to the Numeraries who direct it. In general it is not prudent to speak to them about becoming Numeraries. They themselves will bring it up, if our Lord wants them to.

On the other hand, there are many others who will never be asked to consider the question of vocation or any sort of “dedication”. They can instead be cooperators, or can continue being simply good friends who are very willing to take part in our spiritual and human undertakings.

It is very moving to see how the boys and girls of St Raphael, even those who don’t get the grace of vocation to Opus Dei, have a great affection for the Work. There have been many cases latterly of how they have nobly spoken out to defend it and calmed the waters which some people had muddied with calumny.

They have felt a debt of loyalty and of gratitude for Opus Dei’s maternal care for them, and they have been quick to refute falsehoods because, for them too, Opus Dei is very much theirs, and their conscience hasn’t allowed them to sit back and not put the record straight.

### 15 {#carta.19421024.15}

In the apostolate with young people it is essential to ensure continuity so they don’t lose the constant support it provides them with. When the time comes for them to cease taking part in the work of St Raphael, for example, when they finish their studies or start to work professionally, make sure that they are put in contact immediately with the work of St Gabriel as cooperators.

Often, if they continue receiving our formation for a more prolonged period, the call to a generous “dedication” to the service of God in his Opus Dei will eventually mature in them, if he so wills it, either within marriage or as Numeraries.

Also, my daughters and sons, with the passage time you will see many people who as youngsters are in contact with our apostolate and are afterwards lost sight of, because their job or their family duties take them to far-off parts, and who eventually renew their contact with Opus Dei through the work of St Gabriel; so that the work of the first hour, which at first sight seemed sterile, in the long run is seen to be fruitful.

One can truly apply to our work the words of Holy Scripture: *This water flows toward the eastern region and goes down into the Arabah; and when it enters the stagnant waters of the sea, the water will become fresh. And wherever the river goes every living creature which swarms will live, and there will be very many fish; for this water goes there, that the waters of the sea may become fresh; so everything will live where the river goes. Fishermen will stand beside the sea; from Engedi to Eneglaim it will be a place for the spreading of nets; its fish will be of very many kinds, like the fish of the Great Sea*.

### 16 {#carta.19421024.16}

My daughters and sons, when speaking of the work of St Raphael we shouldn’t conceive it as restricted to apostolate with students. The apostolate of the work of St Raphael is directed not only to students, but also to young people who are not doing third-level studies; this latter work is not carried out in St Raphael centres for Numeraries, but in other Centres.

As you can expect, they all have to acquire the same spirit, and the overall outlines and many other characteristic features will be the same for all. We have already said that the people who take part in this work don’t acquire any bond with Opus Dei; they are friends of Opus Dei with just the necessary basic conditions.

They have been spoken to about the apostolate of the Work and have voluntarily undertaken to be part of it; they have joined us while continuing to be what they were before: ordinary students, office workers or labourers.

That is to say, they don’t acquire any juridical bond — I repeat — with the work, nor with the centres of the Work. That is why the first thing they are told, in the first class they attend, is that we don’t form any sort of association. There is no need to fill in an application form to be part of the apostolic enterprise of Opus Dei.

### 17 {#carta.19421024.17}

It is clear, nevertheless, that those who start receiving our formation will feel a change, an interior shake-up, which will make many of them change their lives; and for all of them, an awakening desire to try to live out the consequences of their faith fully.

They will feel at least genuinely attracted to the spirit of Opus Dei, a human admiration at our exclusively spiritual way of focusing and solving problems which will tend to arouse in them supernatural ideals, a great love for freedom and personal responsibility, and a desire that eventually leads them to be good Christians, and perhaps later on to generous self-giving to God’s service as members of the Work.

### 18 {#carta.19421024.18}

You will understand the work of St Raphael better if you think of it independently of the work of winning apostles, although in reality, the work of St Raphael and this latter always go hand in hand. But one cannot say that one particular boy or girl is simply part of the work of St Raphael and another is a candidate for joining the Work, whether they belong to the work of St Raphael or not: some will receive the grace of vocation and others will not.

If we reflect on those whom we regard as simple friends of the work of St Raphael, we will discover how to develop naturally our apostolate with them: what it is we ought to teach them, what we expect of them and what they themselves expect, and what God asks of them.

The apostolate of the work of St Raphael begins, therefore, with friendship, with some social or professional contact with one of my sons or daughters. Friendship and contact which those of the Work supernaturalize from the very first moment, because it is a work of apostolate; the fellows themselves don’t see every aspect of it from the start, although gradually they do begin to understand and assimilate it without needing to have it spelled out explicitly.

This friendship, this relation with one of you is deepened afterwards, on the one hand, by a growing sense of affection, by more regular contact with the centre of the Work, which they get to know and learn to regard as their own; all this, naturally, linked also to the other friendships they make with the people they meet and get to know in the centre.

On top of that they gradually begin to assimilate the spirit of Opus Dei and to identify with it; and little by little they acquire an affection, a genuine love for the Work and start to consider it their own.

### 19 {#carta.19421024.19}

It is not necessary to wait until one has a deep friendship in order to introduce a boy or girl to the work of St Raphael. But it is essential that you already know each other, either because you both come from the same place and have met previously, because you are related, because you are both studying the same thing, or simply because someone else has introduced you; this initial relationship then allows you to exchange ideas and provides you with an opportunity to explain what we want to say.

It wouldn’t make sense, the first time you meet someone, to start telling him about some residence or corporate apostolate, or some formation classes run by Opus Dei, since more than likely he won’t understand it.

And if he has heard any of those false stories about the Work — those that ill-intentioned or ignorant people circulate — he might possibly get the idea that we want to “rope him in” and could take fright. If we were to speak to someone in this fashion and this were to happen, in most cases it would then be more difficult to win him over later on and make progress with him: if he doesn’t give us a straight ‘No’ or avoids contact with us from the start, either through politeness or lack of personality, he would be deceiving us, we would be deceiving ourselves and we would end up wasting our time.

### 20 {#carta.19421024.20}

This preliminary contact is indispensable to give us a chance to get to know the chap we are dealing with and establish some degree of friendship. Once we see that he has the minimum conditions of selection, he can be spoken to about an ideal that is worth fighting for; which is nothing other than living one’s Catholic faith fully, as well as trying to improve society and to solve all the human problems of this world of ours.

We can tell him that the way to achieve this is to get every individual to improve his or her own behaviour through a re-appraisal of the importance of human virtues, so often neglected; and that, since fighting on one’s own is difficult, we need to support one another to persevere in the struggle. This will give us the necessary feedback, because someone who is unable to understand the Work is of no use for the work of St Raphael.

Afterwards one has to consider especially the candidate’s human qualities, upon which one can always build spiritually, with the help of grace: he should be sincere, generous, industrious, straightforward, discreet, optimistic. It is very difficult for people who have already acquired deeply ingrained habits of idleness to change through classes of formation.

One also has to take into account their intellectual level, their external appearance — which contributes greatly to the human aspect of the Work — their prestige among their friends and colleagues, and their ability to assimilate the ideas they are taught.

Don’t forget that someone who, for example, has acquired a deformed notion of piety, and were to maintain it as an untouchable dogma, would find it difficult to fit in, because he would be trying to defend his own deeply entrenched ideas about apostolate, and the work with others would suffer as a result.

### 21 {#carta.19421024.21}

I have been speaking about selection, and even though I will come back to it again in this letter, to resolve possible scruples and clarify some things for you, I must state here that to want to be select is not a sign of pride: it is not arrogance to want to be better.

Rather, it is a virtue that pleases God: given that we know the base material we are made of; to be better, we must rely always on God’s grace and mercy, and repeat those words of St Paul: *I can do all things in him who strengthens me*.

We have, therefore, the obligation to help those souls become good Catholics, to improve their behaviour, inculcating in them the need for interior life, and inspiring them with the conviction that daily work is the best means to seek christian perfection and to do good to all souls.

### 22 {#carta.19421024.22}

In this way they gradually acquire a sense of direction, helped by personal and voluntary spiritual guidance with a priest friend of the Work and through conversations with a member of the Work, in the practice of interior life.

It is necessary, therefore, not to abandon them; what started out so well and with such good will should be finished off well. We can’t abandon them just because we see that they don’t have a vocation. If we did, it could do a lot of harm to the individuals concerned and to the work of St Raphael in general.

They would feel resentful and bitter; they would speak badly about us — and rightly so — they would create a bad atmosphere and make things difficult for others, and put them on their guard against the Work. We need to have a lot of supernatural vision and a lot of spirit of responsibility, to give them as much or more attention as before, and keep them in contact with the work of St Raphael.

### 23 {#carta.19421024.23}

When someone first starts to come around a house or a Centre, it is very important that the people in charge, having sounded out the person who knows him and looks after him, to decide what elements of formation he should be involved in and the right way to go about it.

In general it is not a good idea for everybody and anybody to attend the classes of formation. In some cases, for lack of time or other circumstances, it is better not to ask them to come. That is, you should have a plan for each one which you try to stick to, helping the individual to fulfil it too.

### 24 {#carta.19421024.24}

Even though we try to avoid moving the Numeraries around, it is inevitable that the demands of their formation and the needs of the Work require them to change their place of residence during the course of their studies (indeed at times more than once), but this shouldn’t cause their St Raphael contacts to be neglected. To avoid this happening, you have to make sure that their friends are *really* part of the work of St Raphael and in contact with not just one individual, but with the Centre itself and with a number of the people of the Centre.

Through the classes, and above all through that daughter or son of mine who looks after them, get them to see that what they are involved in requires a serious commitment on their part; and if they take part in the means of formation, recollections, excursions and the rest, it should be because they want to.

But they should also realise that it is not in any sense a form of children’s entertainment: they can’t let the development of the work of St Raphael become conditioned by their own immaturity, state of mind or other circumstances. The fact of coming to a Centre of Opus Dei requires them to embrace all the apparently small things they are exposed to with a spirit of sacrifice and generosity; and if they are not prepared to do so from the start, it would be better for them to leave, because they would only end up creating problems for everybody.

### 25 {#carta.19421024.25}

This aspect of the apostolate we’re speaking about is very different from that other one which consists in leading souls, with the grace of God, to *dedicate themselves personally* — I don’t use the word “consecrate”, because that word is proper to religious — to the service of God and his Church in the Work of God: this apostolate, in many cases, will be a fruit of the work of St Raphael.

Among the young people who take part in this apostolate, there will be some who are capable of understanding this form of total self-giving. Give them things to do, both material things like small repairs in the house, as well as more important jobs. All this will increase the love that will lead them to vocation and enable them receive the divine call joyfully.

At this stage, my daughters and sons, I think I should mention once and for all that when writing these letters I never try to compose a treatise: I write with all the simplicity and warmth of a father or mother writing to their children. Don’t be surprised, therefore, that in the same letter, with evident lack of order, I deal in different places with different aspects of the same things, and at times I may appear to repeat myself. I will come back to those “repetitions” again in another document, because there is a good reason for them.

### 26 {#carta.19421024.26}

The maintenance of the St Raphael centres ought to be something that falls on the boys or girls themselves and on their parents and families. We have always tried to keep in touch with their parents and have them cooperate; many of them, first of all as friends, and I hope in due course as brothers and sisters.

Therefore, apart from the small or large donations that the boys or their families wish to make to the Director or the Secretary of the centre, in some discreet place you should have a collection box where, without showing off and passing unnoticed, each individual may contribute whatever he can, whatever his spirit of generosity dictates. It is a good idea to get those who are longer in contact to whisper in the ear of the recent arrivals that this is one way — in justice — of responding and showing their gratitude for what they get in the centre of St Raphael.

Looking back over the accounts, it is impressive to see how someone handed over the few pence he meant to spend on the tram: he chose to come walking instead. And let me say that, although it mightn’t seem very much, perhaps that fellow gave more than the one who gave much more simply because he could easily afford it.

### 27 {#carta.19421024.27}

The St Raphael fellows cease to belong to the work of St Raphael, first of all when they write the letter asking to join the Work to dedicate their lives entirely to the service of the Church and of souls; secondly, when with the passage of time they feel they have a vocation, but not with the same sort of self-giving, and ask to join the Work with an identical divine vocation, and give themselves to God in Opus Dei in a different sort of way.

Others cease to belong to the work of St Raphael when they begin to become professionally established and the apostolate with them ought to correspond more to the work of St Gabriel.

Others, finally, when they leave voluntarily, either because it is clear that they never managed to understand and assimilate the spirit of the Work and just don’t fit in; or because circumstances lead them to far-away places where they can only help with their prayer. Always try to maintain cordial relations with them all — directly and personally with the former, and by letter with the latter. Believe me, if you don’t do that, you will lose very valuable collaborators and you will cease doing good to their souls.

### 28 {#carta.19421024.28}

In the whole formative enterprise of the work of St Raphael we count on a great variety of apostolic means and activities. Some of them are already traditional and have proved their effectiveness for the good of souls from the very beginning of our Work: courses of formation, catechism classes and visits to the poor of our Lady, meditations, retreats, and all the liturgical acts of piety celebrated in our halls of residence.

These means are perennial, and whenever we do the work of St Raphael we must use them always and everywhere, for they are characteristic of this apostolate and give life to all the other activities which take place around and about it.

My dearest daughters and sons, you must have great confidence in the supernatural effectiveness of your apostolic work. If you use the traditional means faithfully all will go well. On occasions there may be difficulties of one kind or another, but they will always be solved; they are part of life, and you will overcome them by means of your sacrifice, prayer and cheerfulness.

### 29 {#carta.19421024.29}

The courses of formation are the essential element of the work of St Raphael. With them we nourish continually the boys’ life of piety and help them to complete and deepen their doctrinal formation, giving them — with the “gift of tongues” — the treasures of christian Truth. One begins by explaining the catechism and christian apologetics in a way they can understand, and without telling them that this is what it is, because some of them would find it humiliating. The fact is that many young people, although they are well-disposed and come from christian families, have never received a proper doctrinal formation, either at home or in school, but only a light varnish of “pietism”.

In this context I transcribe some very opportune words of St Thomas Aquinas: “The gratuitous graces are given for the profit of others, as stated above. Now the knowledge one receives from God cannot be turned to another’s profit, except by means of speech. And since the Holy Spirit does not fail in anything that pertains to the profit of the Church, he provides also the members of the Church with speech; to the effect that one not only speaks so as to be understood by different people, which pertains to the gift of tongues, but also speaks with effect, and this pertains to the grace of the word.

“This happens in three ways. First, in order to instruct the intellect, and this is the case when one speaks so as to teach. Secondly, in order to move the affections, so that one willingly hearkens to the word of God. This is the case when one speaks so as to please his hearers, not indeed with a view to his own favour, but in order to draw them to listen to God’s word. Thirdly, in order that men may love that which is signified by the word and desire to fulfil it, and this is the case when one so speaks as to sway his hearers.

“In order to effect this the Holy Spirit makes use of the human tongue as an instrument; but it is He who perfects the work within”.

### 30 {#carta.19421024.30}

Teach them that the Church is a living organism and that they themselves are the Church. Tell them that we are God’s creatures — not animals, but children of God. Make them appreciate that one achieves little with violence, but much more with prayer and study.

In the first Centre I had a placard made which we now have in all the St Raphael centres, with some words of Holy Scripture which remind us that there is a new commandment, the commandment of charity: love one another. Get them to get rid of any sort of distrust; tell them that the truth doesn’t need secrets or secrecy. Let them see that life is short, that there is little time to love.

My sons, you will meet fellows — you, my daughters, will meet young girls — who are capable of all sorts of enthusiasm and sacrifice, but who have some overriding defect that makes it impossible for them, if not to continue in the work of St Raphael, at least to receive the divine calling in any of the forms I have spoken about already.

You have to pray for them, think about ourselves and remember those words of the psalmist: *Afflicted and close to death from my youth up, I suffer your terrors; I am helpless*. If I can apply that to myself, I shouldn’t be surprised that the same can be said of them too.

And one can go to the prayer, and in the presence of God call to mind those fellows, those girls; and our Lord will be pleased to hear us singing softly, silently, the words of that Andalusian song: *That peasant maid / was cast again / like a bell remade*.* And our Lord will listen to us.

### 31 {#carta.19421024.31}

Bear in mind that the Work is not in any sense “transplanted”. Transplants can only be carried out in a carefully controlled environment, whereas Opus Dei is a theological, apostolic and last of all a juridical reality which has to be born in each country — in every race and continent — in the very flesh of the people of that race and continent. If we have the right attitude the Work will always take root very naturally, and it will be totally supernatural: the Work of God.

When I write to you I am often reminded of those words of St Paul to the Ephesians — because I know the clay I am made of, if God were to forsake me — *To me, though I am the very least of all the saints, this grace was given, to preach to the Gentiles the unsearchable riches of Christ and to (enlighten) all men*.

And I add, with all the humiliation of my lack of response, from the bottom of my heart: *For this reason I bow my knees before the Father, from whom every family in heaven and on earth is named*. You are children of God: although you call me Father, you know well that I am nothing: just dust of the earth.

### 32 {#carta.19421024.32}

And St Paul continues: *that according to the riches of his glory he may grant you to be strengthened with might through his Spirit in the inner man, and that Christ may dwell in your hearts through faith; that you, being rooted and grounded in love, may have power to comprehend with all the saints what is the breadth and length and height and depth*.

See how great the life is to which God calls us! We can’t afford to be mediocre! *And to know the love of Christ which surpasses knowledge, that you may be filled with all the fullness of God*.

### 33 {#carta.19421024.33}

We are given these gifts so we can make them known to others. You are well aware that the easiest and most fruitful way to help these young people is by keeping very closely in touch with them, teaching them to make use of the spiritual means to enable them to conquer from the early stages of the ascetical struggle, when their passions begin to awaken and the first signs of rebellion, the first signs of independence, also make their presence felt.

In the words of an early Christian writer, “Recently planted trees, which are not firmly rooted, move under the influence of the blows they receive, and are easily misdirected in any direction; but these young trees, normally twisted by the buffeting of nature itself, can be easily and quickly straightened, in keeping with the purpose of the person tending to them”.

### 34 {#carta.19421024.34}

In the preparatory course one speaks primarily about topics of interior life, stressing always the asceticism of the Work: sanctified and sanctifying work.

Those who have finished the preparatory course move on to the professional courses, which should be distinguished from the doctrinal apostolate the members of the Work ought to carry out in their personal capacity, individually or in public or private associations of a professional nature. The objective of these latter is to provide basic Catholic principles on specific current issues in the religious, social and professional spheres. This is also one of the objectives of the Study Circles of the work of St Gabriel, so that those who attend can pass it on in turn in their own gatherings and environments.

In explaining the different topics, you always have to allow for your listeners’ level of understanding, adapting even the subject-matter to the group you are dealing with: students, employees, farmers, labourers, etc.

It would be better if they don’t take notes in class, nor in the other means of formation; and if they do take them, they should hand them up afterwards. That way they become accustomed to not carrying around notes on spiritual matters or things of conscience; and one avoids the possibility of someone, through lack of awareness, taking something down wrongly and afterwards attributing it to you.

If they are particularly struck by some point and they want to hear more about it, you will very willingly give them whatever guidance you can, perhaps recommending some book for background reading on the subject.

### 35 {#carta.19421024.35}

As part of their apostolic formation, you ought to encourage them to take an active part in professional, artistic, athletic and cultural associations; in doing so, you should always respect their freedom to join or not, and to join whatever ones they prefer. Nevertheless, you do have to give them appropriate guidance about these associations whenever they relate in some way to matters of faith or morals.

There is no objection either to their belonging to religious associations. In this, their objective should be to try to prepare others to run them so that they themselves can remain humbly in the background. You know well, my daughters and sons, what my guiding principle is in this matter. We recommend them to participate in the associations approved by the local bishop, but without making it in any sense obligatory, because in this they are completely free.

The fact of belonging to a religious association or one type of another is not in any way incompatible with taking part in the formative activities of the work of St Raphael. We don’t constitute a group, nor do we create religious associations connected with the Work

### 36 {#carta.19421024.36}

The objective of the professional courses is to help the participants acquire knowledge and improve their capacity for discernment. We’re not trying to arouse an unhealthy sense of questioning everything, of giving scope to the widespread craze of seeing “problems” everywhere or being ideologically avant-garde, but to give them doctrine which is solid, deep and reliable, as well as an apostolic awareness and a deeper interior life. One has to avoid, therefore, both empty dialectic as well as too much over-simplification.

Normally, speaking to them about specialised theological issues is only likely to confuse them, to waste time, as well as possibly putting them in danger of losing their faith, because they are not sufficiently prepared philosophically or theologically to handle things at this level.

We have always been particularly interested in the proper doctrinal formation of our St Raphael fellows. From the moment they first come in contact we try to guide their reading, to recommend some works as being sound and warn them against others, and explain to them briefly but clearly the reasons for the Church’s rights in this matter.

### 37 {#carta.19421024.37}

The professional courses are also a wonderful opportunity to get some older friends involved in this apostolate. They can explain the topics of their speciality clearly and succinctly, and direct the exchange of impressions afterwards efficiently.

I want to remind you here that, as always, the exchanges of impressions are not meant to be a critical analysis of the subject-matter that has been explained, and less still an occasion for heated argument: they are a period of calm study. Their purpose is to clarify people’s real genuine doubts, or to provide explanations or clarifications on points of detail so they can afterwards explain things effectively to others. To achieve this, it is important that the Numeraries and the friends who attend the classes help the person in charge to chair the procedures in an orderly fashion.

Bear in mind that if things look like getting out of control, it is better to ask people to write out what they want to say as briefly and exactly as possible; and it will be explained to them afterwards individually, unless it happens to be of interest to the whole class.

It would be good to get those friends who take part in this work to try to help the best students of the Professional Courses professionally. With their experience and maturity they can guide and advise them effectively.

Very often they will also discover that these young men — professionally competent — end up being good collaborators in their own work; and it will give them an opportunity to keep up contact with them and to prolong, as I have said already, the apostolic contact: *guarding the paths of justice and preserving the way of his saints*.

### 38 {#carta.19421024.38}

To complete this work of formation which you carry out, my daughters and sons, we have the work of the priest. Through frequent preaching, these priest friends of ours — shortly, through the grace of God, it will be priest brothers of yours who will carry out this work — will help you to get the fellows to deepen in the doctrine they get in the Courses of Formation, making it part of their life: generous ascetical struggle and apostolic zeal.

In all the houses where St Raphael’s work is carried out, there should be a weekly meditation for them, normally on Saturdays, in honour of our Lady. They should also have the opportunity of attending monthly recollections and retreats, which are so effective in making progress in the spiritual life.

When inviting them to attend these means of formation, always tell them that they are completely free to come or not, because they are not under any sort of obligation.

When we have priests of our own in the Work, as an essential part of this apostolate, they will carry out an extensive work of personal spiritual direction, thus offering to all the way to carry out that divine exhortation: *And establish the counsel of your own heart (…) to direct your way in truth*.

### 39 {#carta.19421024.39}

With a spirit of sacrifice, the priests will spend many hours looking after all those whom their brothers and the St Raphael fellows themselves bring to our houses: all will go willingly to the priest to open their heart with simplicity and to receive grace from his hands.

Here too let me stress once again that we always respect their freedom to get spiritual direction from any priest they wish. What we offer them are the priests of the Work, in harmony with the other means of formation they receive, but we won’t impose anything on them.

### 40 {#carta.19421024.40}

My beloved daughters and sons, I’m very happy to see you looking after the task of catechesis in the work of St Raphael. It was the first form of apostolate in Opus Dei, and it is logical that this should be so, because all our apostolates are a means of giving doctrine: they are a catechesis. We have to catechise the whole world. I often tell you that the catechism is for children to learn and for both children and adults to practise.

In doing this work we have gone to the poorest quarters, giving preference to those most in need, providing parish priests with this service for the souls entrusted to their care. These priests begin to understand our spirit and to appreciate the Work on seeing the zeal with which you and the St Raphael fellows carry out this apostolate, preparing the children to receive First Holy Communion and teaching the catechism also to adults.

Nevertheless, before inviting the St Raphael fellows to run these classes, they need to be well prepared. They need to have a good grasp themselves of what they are going to teach, both in depth and extension, and they should have sufficient teaching experience to be able to take it on competently. The level of preparation required is in proportion to the condition of their hearers — children or adults — and can never be taken for granted: very often they will learn it directly from you.

### 41 {#carta.19421024.41}

The poor of our Lady. This refined expression of charity began very early on, when the Work was taking its first steps. *Incline your ear to the pauper (…) and answer him peaceably and gently*.

They are visits full of warmth, in which we listen to people affectionately, bringing them words of Christian and fraternal kindness, with a little something to enjoy that they would not normally be able to afford. This expression of spiritual charity also does our St Raphael fellows a lot of good.

Contact with poverty or human weakness is something our Lord likes to make use of to enkindle desires of generosity and divine adventure in souls. At the same time, it has the effect of arousing a deep desire for justice and charity in the hearts of young people.

These unpretentious visits are not trying to remedy any sort of social problem. Explain it like this to the fellows: what we’re trying to do is to offer something a little bit special as a means of comforting someone who is poor, sick, or lonely; to give them a pleasant time, to perhaps offer them some small service, and nothing more.

Once their interior life starts to develop they understand it at once, particularly when they learn that we also do it to honour our Lady. She is mother, Mother of God and our Mother too, and she knows what young hearts try to express through these little acts of love for their more needy brethren.

### 42 {#carta.19421024.42}

It is a great work of charity and justice to strive for the elimination of poverty, illiteracy and ignorance. Nevertheless, charity will be necessary always, because in this world justice can never guarantee man’s happiness completely. Besides, there will always be some who suffer the poverty of loneliness or misunderstanding.

So I emphasise that these visits to our Lady’s poor are especially formative. They teach souls to appreciate the exercise of a living and practical fraternal charity; and, on seeing others who are materially or spiritually in need, they give thanks to God for the goods he has given them.

With these visits we don’t aim either to awaken any form of superficial social concern. It is a question, as I have said, of putting these young people in contact with their neighbour in need. Our St Raphael fellows learn, in a practical way, to recognise Jesus Christ in the poor, the sick, the weak, the lonely, and the suffering, and in children.

*Then the King will say to those at his right hand, Come, O blessed of my Father, inherit the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world; for I was hungry and you gave me food, I was thirsty and you gave me drink, I was a stranger and you welcomed me, I was naked and you clothed me, I was sick and you visited me, I was in prison and you came to me.*

*Then the righteous will answer him, Lord, when did we see thee hungry and feed thee, or thirsty and give thee drink? And when did we see thee a stranger and welcome thee, or naked and clothe thee? And when did we see thee sick or in prison and visit thee? And the King will answer them, Truly, I say to you, as you did it to one of the least of these my brethren, you did it to me*.

### 43 {#carta.19421024.43}

Some forms of charitable work have been so misrepresented and satirised that, for some people, certain acts proper to the spirit of Christianity seem quite archaic. I want you, therefore, to understand very well, and to make others understand, the deep supernatural and human significance of these means, in the manner we have practised them from the beginning.

All these works of mercy — consoling the afflicted, visiting the sick — have been blessed by Jesus Christ himself. It is not right that expressions of genuine Christian charity should be discarded simply because some people have turned them into an ostentatious or frivolous gesture or into a sedative for their feelings of remorse.

You shouldn’t allow routine or laziness to diminish the divine effectiveness of these long-established Christian practices. Always try to breathe new life into them; make it clear that they haven’t gone out of fashion, but are little acts of kindness of great value which ought to lead people to practical conclusions. Teach people, therefore, how marvellously supernatural they are, and how they are a means of winning heaven as well as obtaining happiness on earth.

### 44 {#carta.19421024.44}

Bear in mind that, whatever the circumstances of the country, there will always be lots of scope for practising this affectionate charity. *The poor you always have with you*. Even when society in general is reasonably well off, there will always be at least some needy individuals who are very touched when they receive something a little special which perhaps they ordinarily couldn’t afford, and which is the channel through which they experience a little kindness and fraternal company.

It seems to me that, precisely when circumstances seem to have banished want, poverty or suffering from society, then more than ever there is need for the prompt intervention of Christian charity, ready to identify areas of want in the midst of an apparently universal well-being.

The extension of social remedies against the scourge of suffering or want has made it possible nowadays to achieve humanitarian results which in other times couldn’t even be dreamt of. But, because these social remedies are in another plane, they cannot ever replace the practical supernatural and human kindness of immediate, personal contact with one’s neighbour: with some poor person in the neighbourhood, with some invalid suffering painfully in a huge hospital; or with some other person — rich, perhaps — who needs a period of friendly conversation, Christian solidarity in his loneliness, and a spiritual shelter to remedy his doubts and his scepticism.

This idea may not be understood in places where a materialistic outlook holds sway. That is why I said before that true understanding requires a minimum of interior life, of Christian outlook, of love of God and neighbour.

### 45 {#carta.19421024.45}

Apart from these traditional means, which can never be omitted, you should have many different kinds of cultural, scientific and sporting activities through which large numbers of young people can come in contact with our apostolate. Don’t forget that we are interested in all souls: we have to open out like a fan and bring the light of good doctrine everywhere.

At the beginning the numbers who eventually attend the Courses of Formation may not be very high, because this already presupposes a certain degree of maturity; but if they take part in the other activities associated with the work of St Raphael, you will get the chance to help them round out various aspects of their human, cultural, professional and religious formation. This will help them get to the stage, to some extent, where they can be regarded as *select*.

### 46 {#carta.19421024.46}

There is no limit to the number of activities which can be organised in and from our houses. You’ve already seen this, my daughters and sons, in these last few years, and you will see it even more in the future, as our apostolate, with God’s grace, extends across the world.

I have never wished to tie you down; on the contrary, I have tried to get you to work with great freedom. In your apostolic activity you have to have initiative, within the very broad limits laid down by our spirit, to find in each place, in each environment and in each occasion, the activities that best respond to the circumstances of the young people in contact.

For university students, cultural get-togethers; for other non-intellectual types, a sports club; for young farm workers, practical classes of agriculture, etc. The possibilities are endless, but they all have a common feature: they are lay, secular activities, proper to our specific apostolic way; and they are never ecclesiastical, or official activities of the Church.

We try to provide means which are formative, which the target audience can enjoy and through which we can get to know them better, to put them in contact and to lead them to the warmth of our spirit and the light of the doctrine of Jesus Christ. It is precisely this collection of activities which makes the work of St Raphael perfectly adaptable in all circumstances. We can truly say that our work with young people is a suit made to measure for each occasion and each place.

### 47 {#carta.19421024.47}

The cultural activities, conferences and seminars should all be a forum for good doctrine, a means of spreading very naturally the principles of public ecclesiastical law, of the Church’s social teaching, and the rights and duties of Catholics in public, family and professional life.

One can also have exchanges of impressions about subjects of current interest, but you have to be particularly careful then to follow what I’ve already told you: things are studied, not argued, and the only one who teaches is the one who is properly qualified to do so.

The participants have to study beforehand, in a responsible fashion, without improvising or superficially, the subject-matter that is going to be covered, and they ought to listen attentively to opinions contrary to their own, respecting the freedom of others — in everything that is a matter of opinion — and being very careful to be charitable.

### 48 {#carta.19421024.48}

Another means of formation in some of our residences could be perhaps to have a film club, with good quality films — both morally and artistically — whose members pay a subscription so that the activity is not a financial burden on the house.

Moreover, many of the activities carried out in the ambit of St Raphael’s work ought to contribute towards the upkeep of the Centre: in dealing with the boys don’t neglect the “apostolate of not giving”, because what one doesn’t appreciate what doesn’t have to pay for.

It could also be worthwhile organising literary sessions, concerts and other artistic activities, in order to get to know fellows who are interested in music or who attend music academies, schools of fine art, and so forth. This apostolate is very urgent and extraordinarily effective: *Let this song be a witness for me*, so that art in all its expressions can be a living witness of our Catholic faith, a gentle and powerful influence leading souls to God.

### 49 {#carta.19421024.49}

Excursions too can be very useful as a means of strengthening ties of friendship with the fellows and getting to know them better. It does them good to leave their normal surroundings of study or work for a while from time to time. The occasion can be a visit to some place of historical or cultural interest, or a simple stroll in the countryside, always provided that it is something reasonable, proportionate — we’re not trying to test their physical endurance — and in keeping with their tastes.

A sense of “holy astuteness” will make it easy to find the opportunity then for a more intimate conversation with the fellows, to hold some short devotional practice, and to open to their eyes unsuspected horizons of holiness and apostolate.

During the vacations you can also organise camps, in which the same spirit prevails as in our houses. There you can teach them many small practical ways to be attentive to others, you can help them acquire virtue, and with all naturalness they can participate in some of our Norms of piety.

Where it is customary for students to work during the university holidays, you can also arrange for some individuals or businesses to provide them with summer jobs, through which they can earn some money, at the same time as they continue to benefit from the formation we provide.

### 50 {#carta.19421024.50}

There is no objection to organising work camps during the summer, paying what is necessary to those who need it, because others will offer their services gratuitously. In some cases this can be a way of providing another material instrument for the apostolate — a small house, a mountain refuge, etc. — and moreover carry out social projects, for example, building houses in the countryside or in the city for poor people; helping out in hospitals or schools; or some cultural project like an archaeological dig, or some research project supervised by some friends which the St Raphael fellows can take part in.

We will always find the correct formula. Another idea, for example, that can be used in the work of St Raphael — don’t think it’s stupid — is to collect and restore old furniture: once it is disinfected and tastefully repaired, it can save us money when setting up new houses.

We shouldn’t neglect apostolate with foreign students, which is another expression of the universality of our spirit. We have to be open to everybody, Catholics and non-Catholics, without discrimination of race or language.

Many will come to love the Work, and some will receive the grace of conversion, and even of vocation; and that way you will also make it possible for our apostolate to get to other countries.

### 51 {#carta.19421024.51}

The work of St Raphael is not limited to older students, but extends also to younger ones, those in secondary school, for example. Since they are students, it can be very useful to have career guidance seminars to help them choose a career; or courses on some subject they don’t study at school, or a series of lectures on topical questions, suited to their mentality.

We even have to get to the very youngest fellows, so that they begin to get to know and to practise in some way the basic part of our spirit while they are still small. You have to find a suitable way of keeping them entertained when they come together: some formative game, some practical classes aimed at developing manual skills, etc.

You can organise a club for them, or even set up a scout troop, as long as you have sufficient autonomy in its organisation. That way you can help them to improve, you can speak to them about God and teach them to live as Christians. It is a work of instruction that requires a lot of patience and supernatural spirit, but which is very fruitful.

### 52 {#carta.19421024.52}

With the passage of time many of the children of those friends of ours who wish to be generous in dedicating themselves to the service of God in his Work in the married state, and those of other loyal friends who don’t feel this call, will be brought to the Work by the atmosphere of their families.

It is clear that this task has nothing to do with what religious do in their apostolic schools, and the dioceses through the minor seminaries — which on the other hand I have a great respect for. What we do is a secular undertaking, carried out in society, without taking the boys out of their normal environment. I even recommend to parents not to make it too easy for their children to come to a centre of the Work: without overdoing it, they ought to make things difficult for them.

It is a question really, of looking after these young fellows well so they acquire the spiritual means to help them conquer from the start of the ascetical struggle, when the first rebellions and the first stirrings of passion begin to appear in the soul.

### 53 {#carta.19421024.53}

Our friends have to carry a great part of the weight in these activities which grow up around the work of St Raphael for boys and girls of all conditions and all ages. In this way the Numeraries can concentrate on spiritual formation, giving the St Raphael classes, dealing with the students individually, and providing them with the opportunity of opening their hearts and speaking about their little problems.

Depending on the task involved, some cooperators can help too; and in one way or another the St Raphael fellows themselves who are longer in contact with our work should do the same. Even though they may only be able to help materially, it increases their apostolic concern, their zeal for souls and their sense of responsibility, it binds them closer to the Work, and it makes them see that they don’t come just to improve spiritually themselves: they also come to give themselves by working generously for others.

“The souls of the saints are like the regions of the earth: they communicate mutually what they have received, as in one region are consumed the fruits of another, so that all are united in a single charity”. We have to ensure always that all those who pass through the Preparatory course are given some apostolic responsibility: for example, they can be a useful help in the work with younger fellows.

### 54 {#carta.19421024.54}

I have often told you, my daughters and sons, that there are no holidays in the apostolate: the work of St Raphael is carried out uninterruptedly at every season of the year. As is natural, the work done with students during the holidays has different characteristics from what we do during the school year. But it isn’t suspended.

For young people there is one particular period of the year which can be very dangerous, namely the summer, as it almost inevitably involves less contact with the majority of their companions in the apostolate. So it is important to take all the logical steps to ensure that summer doesn’t imply a total break in their contact with us.

It is essential that the continuity of our relations with them is not broken. You must help them to be faithful to the norms of piety they have begun to make part of their life, and to do some apostolate wherever they are spending the vacation. Help them use their time well by improving their cultural formation, studying some language, and resting too. I have always told the boys that rest is important, and doesn’t mean doing nothing but rather changing one’s activity, and I have even wanted it to be included in the examination of conscience in their monthly day of recollection.

### 55 {#carta.19421024.55}

To make sure they don’t lose touch with the centre they were in contact with during the year, you can organise some excursions as a means of getting to see them; or a number of you can get together, and that way meet somewhere more or less in between. Where there are a number of people spending the summer near one another, each one of them should try to maintain contact with the others. And in any case, it is always possible to write to them regularly, or get their older companions to cooperate with you in writing to them.

How much “letter-writing apostolate” have I done with my St Raphael boys when I didn’t have them nearby! At times, it would be three or four letters in a row before I got an answer. If I couldn’t write to them at length, I would at least send them a couple of lines: something that would awaken them, encourage them, and also remind them of their resolutions.

The Holy Spirit moved the Apostle St John to do the same: *I write to you, young men, because you are strong, and the word of God abides in you, and you have overcome the evil one*.

Also during the summer, so that they improve in our spirit, everybody in contact with the St Raphael centres needs to maintain a minimum plan of life, to do apostolate and look for new people. Apostolate with their friends and companions who are less well “plugged in”, less well prepared, so that afterwards they can bring the more worthwhile ones to the centre. It would be a great pity if, under the pretext of not “complicating their lives”, we were to contribute to their spending the holidays like pagans.

### 56 {#carta.19421024.56}

From what I have been saying you can see that this work of St Raphael is very necessary and merits every sacrifice. And so, with great naturalness we have to approach all young people, through other companions who are already well disposed towards the work, to help them to understand us; because, unfortunately, there are many people bent on spreading among youth what we could describe as “the illness of human hostility”. It is not true that man is moved only by economic factors, which is what is drummed into the hearts of young people nowadays, along with propaganda that destroys their morals.

In the work of St Raphael we have to teach young people that we have to wage a great battle against deprivation, ignorance, illness and suffering. This battle is carried out in broad daylight, because we have no need to operate in the shadows of the night.

That is the moment to whisper, in each one’s ear, that it is true that one carry out a surgical operation with a dirty and rusty scalpel, but that it will be very difficult for it to be successful; and so they, who are God’s instrument, like us, should try to be clean, shining, pure; and should strive to have the science of a surgeon, of a surgeon of souls — the doctrine of Jesus Christ and the practice of piety, which will keep them free from certain moral disabilities.

### 57 {#carta.19421024.57}

Consider how even children, especially nowadays, have thoughts of adventure, action, triumph and passion. But not for adventures of the spirit, which are unknown to them or seem too difficult for them, or (which is worse) they are incapable of discovering.

The general outlook of the civilisation we live in has altered people’s vision of many things which could be otherwise and ought to be oriented differently. The ideals we have to offer the world are: the notion of sanctified and sanctifying work, the supernatural value of work carried out with perfection, and a social contribution which is upright, effective and well-organised.

And we proclaim that no noble human path is unsuited to encountering God, but that in every single one it is possible to find a positive response, provided youth can manage to free itself from the intoxication spread by materialistic solutions to life.

### 58 {#carta.19421024.58}

It is our responsibility to ensure that these young men and women don’t remain trapped in superficial pleasure-seeking, which is always useless and dangerous. At every stage we have to help them develop their capacity to tackle the problems of this world and harness their native power of expression which is so attractive and not at all overbearing, to mobilise a whole untapped generation.

Our objective is to place on the lips of our young people that “startling” supernatural word that moves, that incites to action, that expresses a vitally “committed” attitude, never the grotesque and deadening repetition of words and phrases which cannot come from God.

In the midst of this world-wide cataclysm of hatred and destruction, I tell you once again that we have been called to be sowers of peace and joy. In a very short space of time, if you pray and work with faith and perseverance, we will organise meetings and international gatherings, helped by the St Raphael fellows, with young people from many countries, and we will also be present in undertakings organised by international organisations.

In our residences, or in other activities of the work of St Raphael, we have to organise special courses and cultural activities, particularly during the holidays. Sometimes this will also be a good way of providing a period of formation for the members of the Work. And it will also be an opportunity to carry out an interesting work, namely, the apostolate with foreign students I mentioned earlier.

In this way we will help to create a climate of genuine mutual understanding and co-existence, providing a broad and universal vision which drowns all hatred and bitterness in charity and banishes class struggle, nationalism and discrimination. Dream, and you will fall short.

### 59 {#carta.19421024.59}

To develop the work of St Raphael, it is also a good idea to set up corporate apostolates with young people. Every day they will be more and more varied, just like the whole range of activities associated with the work of St Raphael which I have just been speaking of.

The first task is and always will be the work of St Raphael. We have never started a student residence, for example, without first having begun the work of St Raphael as best we could, even when material resources were almost completely lacking.

The same can be said of any other corporate apostolate: the work of St Raphael has to precede it and accompany it. And so, the boys themselves, and not infrequently their families too, will help, with their prayer and their work, in providing the resources and everything else needed for that undertaking, which they regard as their own. You will see how they always do so willingly and happily; and it will have the effect of bringing them much closer to the Work.

### 60 {#carta.19421024.60}

It is only just, as I have already said, that the boys feel called upon to contribute financially, insofar as they can, to the upkeep of the house. It is the instrument that enables this formative work to take place, of which they are the primary beneficiaries, and represents a considerable effort by us.

Often it is very instructive to give precisely the most troublesome and unruly fellows the job of looking for financial help or other specific tasks. These little obligations tend to give them a sense of discipline, and they respond by improving their character notably, showing that they deserve the confidence that has been placed in them.

We are poor and we will always be so, my dear sons and daughters. I have told you on other occasions that our centres for young people are born with a “congenital defect”, namely, a complete absence of financial resources, together with the need for a dignified oratory, for a domestic staff set up in such a way that it can do its job properly, and for space for an activity that is not financially solvent: that of St Raphael.

We need help, therefore, because we cannot manage on our own. And the fellows respond, contributing to the traditional collections we take up among them; or discreetly leaving an alms, fruit of their spirit of sacrifice, and in a thousand other ways.

### 61 {#carta.19421024.61}

Oftentimes, as I said already, the contribution made by the boys will be quickly seconded by their parents. When they realise how much we are doing for their children, they cooperate generously, they give presents of furniture for the house, or they help to purchase it. In the case of clubs for younger fellows, they themselves and their families should be the ones to provide the premises where they meet and everything needed to run the various activities.

Although sometimes, because of straitened financial circumstances, it may not be possible for someone to make a very large contribution, it is always a first step for mothers, fathers, brothers and sisters to come closer to the Work, and for all of them to benefit afterwards from the supernatural influence of Opus Dei. Through their cooperation God will move them to become our friends — and in some cases even our brothers. They can buy shares, for example, in the properties we set up, and then make them available to us; in return we pay them a reasonable rent, and use the premises as means of apostolate.

### 62 {#carta.19421024.62}

Our corporate apostolates should also get subventions from public and private bodies. These undertakings, which always operate in accordance with the law of the land, provide a great service to society; and so, like all citizens involved in the teaching or caring professions, we have a strict right to receive part of the funds that the State earmarks for these purposes.

But in applying for these subventions we have to make sure that our own autonomy of operation is not eroded. Without that basic freedom we couldn’t do anything. Our friends and cooperators will always be a good means of ensuring we obtain everything we have a right to.

The effectiveness and prestige of what you achieve in the corporate apostolates will also motivate many upright people to help you, even though they may be very far from our Catholic faith. Through contact with your sincere charity they will begin to love the Work and you will carry out an effective task of apostolate *ad fidem* with them.

### 63 {#carta.19421024.63}

I was saying that the work of St Raphael has to precede and accompany any corporate undertaking; that way we ensure we have the right atmosphere from the start. In the case of residences, it will provide a nucleus of well chosen residents who help to set the “tone” of the house, and of the work which is done in the residence and from the residence

These residents, more select than the others, regard themselves as being in their own home, they use their freedom responsibly, they keep the regulations (the family timetable) carefully, and help to create a climate which is fraternal, industrious and cheerful.

If you look after them individually, one by one, letting them see that we are interested in their studies, in their health, in their little concerns, that we share their joys and their sorrows, they will almost always respond generously, and you will do a great good to their souls and to the others living in the house.

### 64 {#carta.19421024.64}

Let me stress here that the residents should feel completely free as regards taking part in the religious formation and acts of piety held in the residence. It is our custom to make a visit to the Blessed Sacrament, and to say the Rosary together — according to the custom of the country — just like christian families do.

We invite them therefore, to join in these customs, but without obliging them, using only the gentle “pressure” of good example. They can also take part, if they want to, in the Mass which is celebrated daily in the oratory.

We respect totally the freedom of the non-Catholic and non-Christian residents. Afterwards, once they see that they are understood and respected and truly loved, it becomes easier for them to become interested in the faith which moves us to live so uprightly and charitably.

If the non-Catholic residents wish (we have to ask our Lord and the Blessed Virgin that they would) we let them take part in the spiritual formation and can give them the necessary instruction to receive Baptism. Through your silent and cheerful effort many of them will receive the sovereign gift of the Catholic faith.

### 65 {#carta.19421024.65}

I am not going to try now to give a list (which on the other hand would be interminable) of the different types of corporate apostolates which, with the passage of time, we will have all over the world. In the educational field alone, at all levels, a huge task awaits us, which we always have to carry out as a professional civil activity.

In the specific field of teaching, the great variety of apostolates in which we always carry out our professional work will also be a means of giving people a solid professional preparation and a good human formation, and of doing a fruitful apostolate, not only with those who come to the centre, but also with people from all social backgrounds.

In the universities, for example, we can run courses of extra-mural studies, always with an apostolic content, aimed at people of the region, intellectual or otherwise, thus providing them with a good dose of Christian formation and good example.

If you are faithful in your daily work, if you try to be holy and zealous, in a short space of time many corporate works of apostolate with young people will become a marvellous reality, a fertile arena for a wide-ranging human and supernatural service to the entire population.

Let me remind you once again that our apostolate with young people will never be restricted to what we do in and from these corporate apostolates. We also have to work in associations, schools, and high-level public and private entities and turn them into effective apostolic instruments: *Wherever the body is, there the eagles will be gathered together*. Wherever anything upright is to be found, there we will go to give it more life.

### 66 {#carta.19421024.66}

I have purposely left it for last to mention the difficulties — apart from the financial ones — that you won’t be spared in this marvellous task. Even good people will be opposed to your work. *The disciples rebuked them*: when children were brought to Jesus the disciples did what they could to prevent them. Our Lord had to insist and say: *Let the children come to me, and do not hinder them*.

Some people will label you from the start with the first thing that occurs to them; that will teach you not to put labels on anybody yourselves. Labels are changed because, with the passage of time, what at first appeared to be useless ends up being a treasure. And perhaps it will happen to you on more than one occasion what happened to me, when all I had was youth and good humour: I prayed, and — I’m not ashamed to admit it — I also wept.

But opposition, borne for the love of God, is always fruitful. *When you prayed with tears, I brought a reminder of your prayer before the Holy One*, said the Archangel Raphael to Tobias. Then the supernatural aspect of our self-giving becomes more apparent, because one experiences in one’s flesh and in one’s soul that oblation which we have made of our lives to the Lord and which rises before him as *a fragrant offering*.

### 67 {#carta.19421024.67}

Difficulties! We will always have them, of one type or another, but they are always overcome; they are part of the background, and you will overcome them through sacrifice, prayer, and cheerfulness. Moreover, don’t forget that we count on the grace of God, on time, on our charity and on our work. Things change, the atmosphere matures; and those who were critical and negative end up praising and supporting.

Don’t get upset if you’re called names: they’re right! Our Lord has said through St Matthew: *One there is who is good*. At times, daughters and sons of my soul, you have to remember that sower *who went out to sow his seed*, and consider that the wanton generosity of casting the pure golden wheat with both hands into the bowels of the earth brings in its wake not a few headaches: rain and drought, sun or fog, cold and heat, all of which can be harmful to the grain.

On other occasions there will be no shortage of occasions to suffer, coming not from without but from within. Like some ill-timed outburst — “putting one’s foot in it”, as they say themselves — from one of those young fellows who come round our centres. It could happen then that the Director doesn’t manage to handle the situation properly: perhaps because he hasn’t been properly trained or lacks experience; or because the job requires a high degree of toil, effort, patience, sacrifice, renunciation and, I have to add, profound humility. You ought to be ready to not to get annoyed at these things and to overlook them with a smile.

I’ve told you already: give them things to do they can feel responsible for; pray for them, treat them affectionately and patiently — with the charity of Christ — and you will see how they change: those who were problematic become, in your hands, *like clay in the potter’s hand*, very faithful instruments.

### 68 {#carta.19421024.68}

My children, when you encounter these difficulties and charity leads you to bear them silently with a cheerful smile, your life is filled with divine wisdom: *Because wisdom opened the mouth of the dumb, and made the tongues of babes speak clearly*. Afterwards you will see how this divine Wisdom, in its own time, opens the mouth of the dumb and bestows eloquence on those who have learned to become like little children.

I know that you are always very conscious that *you are all sons of God, through faith*. What power we have! The power that comes from knowing, and from being, children of God. And if in spite of everything, you feel that you have very little to show for your effort, call to mind those words of Isaiah: *My chosen ones shall not labour in vain*.

Let each of you reflect on those words: *I am under obligation both to Greeks and to barbarians, both to the wise and to the foolish*. We cannot waver, then, in the face of these or other difficulties. It cannot come to pass that, through negligence on our part, someone could say to our Lord: *no one has hired us*.

### 69 {#carta.19421024.69}

With the passage of time the formation classes of St Raphael and other activities will be more numerous than ever, because, as I like to say, our work is a sea without shores. Not infrequently the son of mine who has to give them may be very young, perhaps even younger than his hearers, whom he has to instruct and guide without over-asserting himself.

If problems arise because of someone’s lack of understanding, I would say to that son of mine: *Command and teach these things. Let no one despise your youth, but set the believers an example in speech and conduct, in love, in faith, in purity. Till I come, attend to the public reading of scripture, to preaching, to teaching*.

### 70 {#carta.19421024.70}

My daughters and sons, may all of you, from the youngest to those who are not so young any longer, always be vitally committed to our work of St Raphael. We must look upon it, I insist, with special affection. It must be the apple of our eye.

This concern has to express itself in constant prayer and in deeds. Your apostolic zeal will lead you to go forth to all the cross-roads of the world in search of souls, to communicate Christ’s call to them. “Christ could have drawn the people to himself to hear his preaching just by remaining in the same place, but he didn’t do things that way. He gave us example, so that we too would travel the highways, seeking those who have gone astray like a shepherd seeking the lost sheep, like a physician seeking the ill person”.

You will go in search of them wherever they are to be found, to lead them to the warmth of our spirit, to the divine light of our work of formation, placing them under the patronage of the Archangel St Raphael and the Apostle St John, to set them on fire, to purify them, to fill them with doctrine, with the love of God, and with a noble devotion to our Lady, the Holy Church and the Roman Pontiff.

With a blessing from your Father
