## INTRODUCTION {#inedito.growing-on-the-inside.presentacion}

I can imagine your immense joy when you receive and meditate on these notes taken from our Father’s preaching in the Honduran Consulate during the months of April through August 1937, now seventy years ago. Don Alvaro on several occasions expressed his desire to have them published, but he didn’t have the opportunity to see the project completed. Reading and meditating on these notes will greatly help all of us to set out ever more fully along the paths of prayer, led by the hand of such a good guide as our Father for drawing close to God.

During the Spanish civil war, Christians who remained in the zone controlled by the Church’s enemies suffered a cruel religious persecution. In the first year of the conflict alone 6,500 priests and religious were killed; many laymen suffered the same fate, simply for being Catholics. In this situation, our Father continued to carry out a wide-ranging but clandestine pastoral work, to the extent that circumstances allowed, risking his life when necessary for the good of souls. After a real odyssey, he found refuge in the Honduran Consulate in Madrid, which along with other friendly sites granted asylum to many thousands of people during those months.

The offices of the Consulate occupied two floors of a building on Paseo de la Castellana, no. 51. Our Father lived there with four of his sons and his brother Santiago from mid-March until August 30, 1937. They lodged on the ground floor, accessed directly through the vestibule. In total, almost one hundred people sought refuge in that house.

The living conditions were very trying. Men, women and children were obliged to live together in the confinement of a few square meters. There were no beds; they slept on mattresses spread out at night on the floor. Since only one bathroom was available, it was necessary to wait in line to use it. Only two meals a day were provided, at midday and in the evening; and due to the food shortage, these barely reached subsistence level. Our Father lost sixty-five pounds during those months. Afterwards, when the grandmother saw him she failed to recognize him at first; only later did she realize he was her son, by his voice.

Without a specific task to fill the long, slow hours, most of the refugees didn’t feel up to occupying their time constructively. One of those present writes: “Some spent the time silently mourning their misfortune. Others poured out their troubles, bitterly lamenting family reverses, career or business losses, the uncertain future. Ever present was the fear awakened by past sufferings and persecutions, a fear that painted the world outside our asylum in inhospitable hues.”1

From the first moment of their confinement, the climate our founder created contrasted sharply with the general tone of pessimism. To stay busy during the day, he set up a schedule

During the first weeks, they were assigned a place to sleep in the dining room, along with a number of other people. In that situation, moved by his desire to provide his sons with spiritual nourishment, our Father sometimes gave them a meditation early in the morning in the vestibule, while the others were still resting. Naturally, so as not to disturb anyone, he spoke softly. Nevertheless, some people complained, and the consul asked him to stop. Our Father records this in one of his letters: Can you imagine? They no longer allow my brother to have his morning get-together. They’re afraid of disturbing the neighbors. But I assure you, to employ a nice expression, that although his heart was shouting, he wasn’t shouting out loud. A month later, the consul authorities provided them with a small room. Though only nine or ten square meters in size, it at least offered them a minimum of independence. This improvement occurred during the first half of May, when Blessed Josemaría was sick for a few days. From then on, he began to preach regularly to them again, sometimes in the morning, other times at night, especially before major liturgical feast days. Our Father was also able to celebrate Holy Mass more often. On Sundays and feast days, he celebrated Mass in the vestibule so that others could attend. Isidoro Zorzano obtained the hosts and wine for them in a variety of ways.

This book is based on the hand-written notes of some of our Father’s meditations in the Honduran consulate. Don Alvaro, who was present, recalled that at first no one took notes. But soon they realized that our Father’s preaching would do the others in the Work a great deal of good. So with his permission they began to take notes to pass them on to the others through Isidoro. With his documentation as an Argentine citizen, Isidoro could move about Madrid with a certain facility, though not exempt from danger.

With brief but vivid brushstrokes, our Father sketches the setting of his preaching in the Honduran Consulate. Employing the figurative language that he used in his correspondence during those months to get through the censors, he writes: I spent a pleasant time with the children, talking about all kinds of things. It’s a pity I can’t send you the brief notes that Eduardo took. But I trust that don Angel C. will have already told each of you what you need to know. One of those accompanying our Father, Eduardo Alastrué, had a particularly good memory. As soon as our Father finished speaking, he set down in writing what he had heard, trying to follow as closely as possible our Father’s words and style. Although his notes are not a verbatim account, they are of great value because they offer eye-witness testimony of Blessed Josemaría’s teaching in those trying circumstances. In a letter dated July 1937, our Father writes: Here are some more notes of the talks I’m giving to these fellows. Although often they don’t get what I say right (though sometimes they do), I have them keep on taking notes, since they can always be of some benefit to you.6

Our Father always saw God’s hand in every circumstance, whether favorable or not. He had identified himself so closely with our Lord that he was totally convinced that all things work together for the good of those who love God: 7 omnia in bonum! Reading and meditating on these notes will help us to more fully appreciate our holy founder’s mind and heart, and to learn from his love for God. Certainly, one finds allusions to the specific historical situation of the day, but neither here nor in his correspondence does he ever seem discouraged or pessimistic. Without ignoring either the serious nature of events or the threat to their lives, our Father’s overriding concern was to confirm his children in their faith, encourage their hope, and enkindle them in love for God and zeal for souls. His preaching is so imbued with trust in his Father God that the only explanation can be the fortitude our Lord was granting him. Amid such catastrophic circumstances, which humanly speaking seemed to threaten the very existence of Opus Dei, Blessed Josemaría engendered in the few young men who then followed him the conviction that the Work would come forth strengthened from that crisis. His supernatural vision impelled him, in the midst of such truly dramatic events, to describe the future development of Opus Dei in terms of the tree in the Gospel parable, 8 certain that the Work would spread its branches throughout the world until the end of time, in the service of the Church and all souls. All things would be possible, he assured them, if they remained faithful to their Christian vocation, making an effort to “grow on the inside,” by intensifying their supernatural life.

It is striking to see how clearly, even back then, he describes Opus Dei’s mission in the Church. He had fully incarnated in

his own life the key ideas9 of the spirit of the Work, as he sometimes called them. Concepts such as the Work’s divine origin, the universal call to holiness, professional work as a means for personal sanctification and apostolate, the full secularity of the faithful of Opus Dei, their lay task in the midst of society’s structures—being in the world without being worldly—and many other key aspects of the spirit of Opus Dei are found expressed in these pages with the same words he used years later, right up to the end of his life. As he often said, the supernatural light that God enkindled in his soul on October 2, 1928, grew constantly in intensity and breadth over the years, but the fundamental spirit of the Work was already present from the start.

I ask the Blessed Virgin that by reading and meditating on these notes taken from our Father’s preaching we may all grow interiorly in our Christian life. In particular, may they be a spur for us to overcome with God’s help any obstacles we encounter in our apostolic work, as Blessed Josemaría, our beloved founder, always did.

Your Father,

## THE GLORY OF GOD {#inedito.growing-on-the-inside.1}

April 6, 1937

### 1) {#inedito.growing-on-the-inside.1.1}

Since man’s end is to praise God in this life and enjoy him eternally in the next, death is no more than the “occasion” that allows us to perfectly fulfill the supernatural last end for which we have been created. When we consider things in this way, why should we be attached to life? Why should we be afraid of death? When someone dies, ordinarily everyone is in a hurry to have the cadaver taken away, including his parents, his brothers and sisters, his fiancée, although no one ever wanted to be separated from him; at times people even grow impatient if those who are coming to dispose of the body are delayed. We have to be detached from everything that isn’t permanent. To achieve this, we need to seek out continuous mortification, above all in little things. How mistaken are those who claim they’re ready for great sacrifices, heroic acts, when they can’t conquer themselves in little things. They would gladly allow themselves to be crucified before a huge crowd in the center of Madrid, but are incapable of suffering the least pinprick, the tiniest annoyance.

Notice how even in the natural order, in one’s physical life, it’s necessary to carry out one small act after another in order to achieve lasting results. For example, no one can conceive of an athlete who wants to win a championship and doesn’t train every day. Well, those who want to win the supreme championship need to train much more. We have to be convinced that we need to joyfully deny ourselves in order to gain the imperishable crown. Lord, I seem to be hearing you telling me once again what you told your disciples: If anyone would come after me....10 There’s no other way but to take up the Cross and follow You. But what a pity that after twenty centuries of Christianity, many Christians still don’t understand the value of suffering. They rebel when any suffering comes their way; they want Christ without the Cross. The consequence is that they suffer even more and to no avail, because they aren’t pleasing you, Lord, and derive no supernatural benefit from their suffering. If we’re determined to give God glory in everything we do, death is no more than a “small detail.” Let’s fully accept it then: whenever God wants, wherever God wants, however He wants to send it.

### 2) {#inedito.growing-on-the-inside.1.2}

Ut omnes unum sint.11 May we all be perfectly one. Thus Jesus prays to his Father at the Last Supper. Beforehand he had said: omne regnum divisum contra se desolabitur, et omnis civitas vel domus divisa contra se not stabit;12 every kingdom divided against itself will be destroyed, it will not remain standing. Therefore we need unity of heart, unity of mind and

__________ 10 Mt 16:24 11 Jn 17:21: May they all be one. 12 Mt 12:25: Every kingdom divided against itself is laid waste, and no city or house divided against itself will stand.

unity of will. Here you have sufficient material to fill your half hour of meditation. I’m thinking now of the prayers and offerings that have come from more than one prison cell, more than one battle trench, more than one hospital room, on the last feast of my father and lord, St. Joseph. We should pray a great deal so that all these oblations, pleasing as they are to God our Lord, be maintained by his help. We have an obligation to pray for all those brothers of ours and to mortify ourselves, to give ourselves a hard time (the prayer of the flesh, of the senses), so that we may all be one in heart, mind and will. One in Christ, preserving our own personality.

### 3) {#inedito.growing-on-the-inside.1.3}

And all of this, for what? To give glory to God. It’s very good that you want to stand out and excel in your work; but you need to rectify your intention, channeling those efforts exclusively to God’s glory. Be convinced that everything earthbound comes to an end. After we die, perhaps soon, people will forget us. Why should we seek such fleeting glory? Let’s be concerned only about being a Christian father, a Christian doctor, a Christian architect, a Christian worker. If every country had a group of holy fathers of families, holy doctors, holy architects, holy workers, all the world’s problems would be solved. We end our meditation in a dialogue with our Lady, renewing the specific resolutions we have made today.

## SUPERNATURAL LOVE {#inedito.growing-on-the-inside.2}

April 7, 1937

### 1) {#inedito.growing-on-the-inside.2.1}

When a member of our body is wounded, the eyes, hands and mind strive to remedy the damage. In this supernatural organism of the Work, the same law has to apply. When your brothers, who are my sons, suffer, how can we fail to be moved? If someone is in danger, even if not immediate, how can we be at ease? I suffer when I think of the members of the Work who are separated from us, in the trenches or in prison. And I fully understand St. Paul’s words: “Who is sad, and I am not sad? Who is sick, and I am not sick?” 13 Thus the Apostle to the Gentiles expressed the union in charity that he experienced with his brothers. Ours must be the prayer of the first Christians, even in our gestures, with our hands upraised to heaven! How can I fail to pray for those who are far away, sick or in danger? Lord, pour forth your graces abundantly into their hearts, minds and wills; anoint each of their senses with your merciful love. Along with the supernatural means, we must also enlist our hands and eyes: the human means You ask us never to neglect. Specifically, regarding Ricardo, we’re at peace after doing everything possible, with a peace we owe to You. Now we pray __________ 13 Cf. 2 Cor 11:29

that he can carry on without being harmed. We each prayed for Juan’s arrival here and now he’s with us: may he soon be able to pass over to the zone where Christians aren’t persecuted, without suffering either spiritual or bodily harm. And how peacefully Chiqui bears his sufferings, we are told. He has his interior crosses, but like everyone he also has consolations: those only You can give. I pray for him, and for all who are now having a tough time of it, unbeknownst to us.14 My sons, when the storms of temptation stir up the filth in the depths of our souls, when the three concupiscences of which St. John speaks rise up like three vipers,15 trying to get our mind, will and flesh to rebel, it’s a wonderful help for me to remember the prayers and sacrifices all my children offer each day for me. The prayers and sacrifices we make for our brothers will also be a powerful shield against the devil’s attacks. When the enemy tempts us, offering us the passing pleasure of pride, vanity or the flesh, we must respond to him: what you’re offering me is a lie. Ad maiora natus sum! I was born for greater things.

### 2) {#inedito.growing-on-the-inside.2.2}

Our supernatural love for our brothers takes precedence over the charity we owe the members of our natural family. It takes precedence over it, but doesn’t diminish it: quite the contrary. How can we fail to pray for our relatives and for everyone, asking for a quick end to the war, a quick and definitive defeat of those opposed to God? Not out of pride, not out of human motives, but for You, Lord, and for your Church. May the horrible sacrileges and heinous crimes being committed cease. In the meantime, what reparation am I offering you? For deeds are love and not sweet words. Where are my sacrifices and mortifications? Why is there so little self-sacrifice in that area? I can hear the excuses of our weak human nature: how can I be expected to seek out mortifications, when I have such little freedom here? Nevertheless, I tell you: “where there’s a will, where there’s love, there’s a way.” If I’m moved by love, how can I fail to find opportunities to overcome myself in little things, without anyone noticing? Specifically, today, let’s seek out a small mortification and offer it for those brothers of ours who are now in danger, and for those others who are also in danger, although we haven’t received news of it.

### 3) {#inedito.growing-on-the-inside.2.3}

As a third point in our meditation, let’s consider our duty to defend the vocation of our brothers tooth and nail, using all the means at hand, especially prayer and sacrifice. Today, more than ever, we have to say repeatedly: Blessed be pain; loved be pain; sanctified be pain; glorified be pain. Let me insist: we can’t limit ourselves to asking God to keep them from going astray from the path of the Work in even their slightest thought. We have to help our brothers persevere as best we can, protecting

them by our prayer and sacrifice, and helping them to rectify if they’ve made a mistake. United to our fraternal affection, to our holy “transigence,” there must always be a holy intransigence in whatever refers to our Christian vocation. And along with this holy intransigence, we need a holy coercion and a holy shamelessness.16 Jesus, if we love you with our whole soul, how can we fail to cultivate in your service—in a holy way!—the lack of human respects seen in those who love one another on earth? We finish this time of prayer in a dialogue with the Blessed Virgin, recalling the importance of praying and offering sacrifices for our brothers.

__________ 16 Cf. The Way, 387 Blessed Josemaría often explained that holy coercion is totally incompatible with not respecting others’ freedom, a teaching he often stressed. For example, in one of his Letters he says that the expression compelle intrare (“compel people to come in”), which our Lord uses in the Gospel parable (Lk 14:23), is “an invitation, a help to make up one’s mind. It’s never, even remotely, a matter of coercion.” “It is not a physical push, but an abundance of light, of doctrine; the spiritual stimulus of your prayer and work, which is the true witness of doctrine; the numerous sacrifices you offer; the smile that comes to your lips because you are God’s children.... To all this add your human class and charm, and there we have the content of the compelle intrare” (Letter, 24 October 1942, 9).

## THE COMMUNION OF SAINTS  {#inedito.growing-on-the-inside.3}

April 8, 1937

### 1) {#inedito.growing-on-the-inside.3.1}

Intimately united to Jesus in the Blessed Sacrament whom we have just received, let’s remember in our prayer those brothers of ours who are still waging the battles of this life, and those who have finished their combat and are now being purified in purgatory, as well as those who are already enjoying God in heaven: the Church militant, the Church suffering, and the Church triumphant. Our resolution will be to always keep in mind the consoling doctrine of the Communion of Saints. All our prayers and deeds done in the state of grace gain two effects for us: satisfaction and merit. We can help pay the debt for our sins and merit divine grace. Theology teaches us that we can also apply these two treasures to other souls who are in need of them, since we are united to the entire Mystical Body of Christ. These effects of our prayers and good deeds principally reach those who are united to us ex radice caritatis, by the root of the same Christian vocation and by charity.

__________  Blessed Josemaría preached this meditation after Mass, which during the first days of his stay he celebrated in the consulate’s vestibule. See the introduction to this book.

For example, the merits of every good work I carry out benefits each one of you, who are my sons; your merits in turn benefit and help all your brothers and me. Through the Communion of Saints, we can never feel alone, since spiritual strength is constantly reaching us from prisons and trenches, wherever one of your brothers is present. Considering this reality impels us to thoroughly examine our conduct here where we are confined, as though in a prison. For, amidst our apparent inactivity, we have the possibility to do a great deal of good. We can accompany each of our brothers in danger and protect them. Conversely, each time we overlook a mortification, each time we shorten the prayer, we harm them, we fail to help them to bear their sufferings and reject temptations. Always keep this consideration in mind. May it spur you on in your interior life. Don’t forget that, although we Christians are many in number, we form a single body in Christ, in union with the holy souls in purgatory and those now part of the Church triumphant.

### 2) {#inedito.growing-on-the-inside.3.2}

Just as there are brothers of ours who are suffering on earth, perhaps one of them is now also suffering in purgatory. In general, people don’t like to hear talk about purgatory and hell: they only want to hear about heaven. They aren’t like our first brothers in the faith, whose virtues made them strong and fearless. Rather, they’re like those brightly-colored, rosy-cheeked statues produced nowadays. Well, if these people could hear us now, they’d say we’re mad, absolutely mad. But they’re the ones who are mad enough to try to pass from this life to eternity, from earthly life to heaven, without suffering in the least, without using the supernatural means, including mortification and union with Christ’s Cross.

Worldly people have a false notion of good and bad. ## CONFIDENCE IN GOD {#inedito.growing-on-the-inside.5}

### 1) {#inedito.growing-on-the-inside.5.1}

With the eyes of our body closed, let’s imagine Jesus with the eyes of our soul. Since we’ve just received Him in the Eucharist, we have Him sacramentally present within us. ## CONFUSIONISM {#inedito.growing-on-the-inside.10}

May 15, 1937

### 1) {#inedito.growing-on-the-inside.10.1}

“Confusionism” means a clouding of one’s mind and heart; at times it’s close to a lie, or even the same thing. Confusionism in ideas involves a mixture, more or less obvious, of error and truth; and the truth can only come out losing when mixed with error. Crowned with the prestige of truth, error spreads more easily and gains control over the spirit without meeting any resistance; and the truth is tainted, stained by the contagion of error. In a group of both sick and healthy people, the latter can never come out winning; if anything, the virulence and danger of the sickness will only increase. It’s imperative, then, to purify and clarify ideas in every sector where confusion has entered. The world is now more than ever under attack from this sickness. Confusionism in ideas is seen in the one who tries to live in both the brothel and the church; it is found in the student who frequents Holy Communion but forgets about study and work; and in those “Christian gentlemen” who, when fulfilling their professional duties, leave out Jesus Christ. Don’t we too suffer from this confusionism in ideas, not in what is essential or fundamental, but in small details of

apparently little importance? It’s hard to be specific, but each of us should apply his mind to his own life to see if he finds anything, just as we apply it to studying any other matter. Let each one look closely at his own situation. If at first he finds nothing, let him insist with more determination; and if even then he still finds nothing, let him look for dark corners in his past life. And if he has left that darkness behind, let him give heartfelt thanks to God and ask for his constant light and help.

### 2) {#inedito.growing-on-the-inside.10.2}

Confusionism in our heart. They carry their heart around exposed, as though it were in a shop window, so that everyone may see and possess it. One should tell them: you can’t behave that way! Where will you end up with that silly sentimentality, that foolish and childish poetry that only inspires laughter? Doesn’t your heart belong to Christ? Well, keep it only for Him, lock it firmly with seven locks.58 Or do you think that you’re going to give your heart to God only after others have used it and cast it aside? No, put a firm guard on your heart. It’s so easy for it to get attached to things. Even when it’s subjected, how often it tries to cast off its yoke and latch on to whatever happens to come along. Let’s be vigilant over the movements of our heart to see whether or not they’re in accord with the will of Christ’s heart.

The remedy for all this? ### 1) {#inedito.growing-on-the-inside.18.1}

Charity in little things. How difficult but how important it is to practice charity in small details! Out of respect for those around them, educated people, when they need to refer to something repugnant or disagreeable, employ a euphemism; they don’t call things by their name. ## MILITIA EST VITA {#inedito.growing-on-the-inside.19}

June 21, 1937

Militia est vita hominis super terram,120 man’s life on earth is warfare. Woe to the one who doesn’t struggle! Anyone who doesn’t struggle isn’t alive. ## OBEDIENCE IN DAILY LIFE {#inedito.growing-on-the-inside.20}

June 22, 1937

It’s understandable that during these months of upheaval, when the members of many apostolic organizations have had to scatter and go into hiding, some fundamental norms of their rule have been relaxed. I’m thinking now about how much the virtue of obedience has suffered these days. It’s a virtue which is needed in any organization, and even more so in religious organizations. And for us, who aren’t religious, it’s not only necessary but very necessary. We need to recognize that many things can contribute to weakening the exercise of this virtue. Obedience isn’t limited to the rapid and perfect fulfillment of commands, whether they are important or not; it extends to the countless details of daily life. On the one hand, living together in close quarters breeds a familiarity that can curtail outward respect. Perhaps one’s heart, since its valves aren’t functioning properly, lets off steam at inopportune moments. Thus one’s interior life weakens. The one who should give example, due to his position and authority, fails in general to do so. In such an environment it’s not surprising that, sometimes by sarcastic remarks, other times by subtle

refusals followed by more categorical ones, docility is neglected and eventually left behind. Don’t forget that I’m not referring to anyone personally. I’m speaking about any organization in general. But what would happen if a watch were to allow all its gears and parts to separate, and each began to run on its own? Yes, life is possible only if each part fulfills its proper work, faithfully carrying out its assigned role. Confusion has resulted in death; the blame falls on the one who should have kept the watch case closed but failed to do so. Perhaps the metal protecting it wasn’t strong enough. Perhaps dust was allowed to get in and ruin the parts. Whatever the cause, the result has been disastrous. ## PRAY WITHOUT CEASING {#inedito.growing-on-the-inside.23}

June 24, 1937

The best book for our meditation is the Gospel, the New Testament in its entirety. Let’s open it now and contemplate in the Acts of the Apostles the first Christians’ way of life. The Church was undergoing fierce persecution, as in this country today. Her visible head, Peter, had just been detained. Three squads of soldiers were guarding him in prison, where he was bound by two chains. The guards watched over him without interruption; even as he slept two soldiers kept watch. What could the first Christians do to defend their first Pope? Most of them had no social influence, and those who had any couldn’t use it. But St. Luke doesn’t fail to record of our first brothers and sisters in the faith: oratio autem fiebat sine intermissione. 129 They prayed without ceasing. The entire Church, with arms upraised in prayer, cried out to God. And what were the results? At night, an angel appears in Peter’s cell, awakens him and says: surge, velociter.130 Get up quickly; get dressed and put on your shoes. The chains fell off, the prison doors opened, and the Prince of the Apostles walks away free. __________ 129 Acts 12:5 130 Acts 12:7

This event, which forms the framework for our meditation, clearly shows us how Christian souls, those who love God, should act, and how God responds to those who give themselves to Him and hope in Him. Filled with faith and peace, in their hour of need, those first Christians prayed. At difficult moments, do we have the peace that comes from praying with the intensity and perseverance the situation requires? Haven’t we slackened in our prayer? Now, when so many dangers besiege us, when we’re beset by so many hardships, are we attentive to God all day long, asking Him for what is most in keeping with his glory? When we see our voluntary imprisonment prolonged, do we cry out from the depths of our heart, ‘Lord, whatever you want, I want it too’? Remaining here or leaving our confinement: what’s the difference? We’re your children, who desire only to fulfill your will. Here we are, my God, ready to obey you. Not only the first guard point, the door to this house, but also the second one, the border guarding the two zones, will open wide for us if we insist in our prayer as the first Christians did. Let’s pray with humility, with faith, with perseverance. And we can’t forget the role of the guardian angels, so that God will receive our petitions favorably. How is our friendship with our guardian angel? Have we prayed for the people living alongside us in this house, by going to their guardian angels? This is an essential duty of charity. We ask our guardian angel, who by God’s goodness accompanies us, to free us from the little bonds that still tie us to the world and the flesh: those small marks of self-love, pride, disorder, laziness. We ask him to make us more diligent in fulfilling our duties, more attentive in obeying, more insistent in struggling against our shortcomings. And above all, let’s beseech him to help us abandon all self-complacency and confidence in worldly things. May we turn towards Christ and abandon ourselves completely in his hands, so that we find our peace in the exact fulfillment of his Will. We draw from our prayer the resolution to renew our confidence in our guardian angel and in those of everyone else with whom we’re in contact.

## DEATH, BOTH CERTAIN AND UNCERTAIN {#inedito.growing-on-the-inside.26}

### 1) {#inedito.growing-on-the-inside.26.1}

Death is both certain and uncertain for us. It’s certain that this critical moment will come; but the moment when death will snatch us from this world is uncertain. We know that death will come for us semel,142 only once. Our “composition of place” for this meditation will be to see ourselves as deceased. This will lead us to prayer of petition, to understanding the fleeting nature and insignificance of the things of this world, and to a single-minded desire for what is eternal. Yes, it’s certain that all of us must die and that it will happen only once—when, we don’t know. Whether in a few years or a few days: what’s the difference? The moment will arrive when we become a heap of rotting flesh that all look away from in disgust, even those who loved us most on earth. What good then will be the honors and pleasures that we enjoyed in this world? Since everything comes to an end, the thought of death has to kill in me the three concupiscences: of money, of the flesh and of ambition.143 __________ 142 Cf. Heb 9:27 143 Cf. 1 Jn 2:16

Of what use will money be to me when I die, even if I own all the world’s gold, the most precious jewels, the most splendid palaces? What will all that be worth at the final moment? At best, to buy myself four boards for a coffin to be buried in. How should I view sensuality in the light of death? As nothing: wretchedness, corruption. And the love of creatures? In death this ends as well. Those who loved us most will at first feel sorry for us. Later, they will forget, be consoled, and end up viewing our death as timely. Our family? It’s like a dream from which one awakens to eternal glory or eternal sorrow. Poets keenly felt the fleeting nature of our journey here, comparing its duration to a flash of lightning. ## THE CHURCH, THE BARQUE OF PETER {#inedito.growing-on-the-inside.32}

Our “composition of place” for this meditation will center on the symbolic image of the Church as the barque of Peter. Our petition is for a firm obedience, even in the smallest matters, for everyone in the Work. Although the keystone may regret losing part of its mass, although it suffers on seeing itself wounded by the instruments that polish it, it doesn’t complain. It gives itself, submits itself. Its roughness is gradually removed, and in what before was an unformed mass one can make out the shape of a perfect piece. But that isn’t enough. The keystone still has to be subjected to the hard blows of a heavy hammer, whose head is armed with an array of sharp points. The monotonous hammer blows begin. These are the daily setbacks, which grind down the stone, polishing and smoothing it. The stone continues to submit. But it still needs some final touches. The one in charge, the directing architect, contemplates the hewn and polished product and decides: ‘This isn’t going to be a common stone. A high-relief of some acanthus leaves needs to be carved on this surface; while this other surface needs to be engraved with some other adornments.’ The keystone is again cut by sharp instruments; a chisel and hammer open deep furrows. If only the keystone could complain! But it remains silent and submits. This is perseverance. Nothing can make it change its decision to want to be useful. Finally the desired shape and appearance is attained, thanks to this constancy. When considered apart, on its own, the stone has now taken on a certain beauty. But when fitted into its place in the building under construction, it disappears and goes unnoticed. Nevertheless, the stone lets itself be moved to its place, to the place the one in charge has planned for it, and there it settles. Now it’s in the place where it belongs. Its own beauty contributes to the beauty of the whole edifice; on its own, it passes unnoticed. How hard it is to hide oneself in this way, to renounce standing out, to disappear in the whole. But what use is a keystone out of place or by itself? Perhaps after it settles, it is then plastered over and disappears entirely. It’s the complete renunciation of personal glory. The keystone has to sustain the pressure of all the stones around it; it also sustains other keystones and these in turn support it. As elements in this supernatural edifice that is the Work of God, we have to ask our Lord for the fortitude to be a sure support for those at our side. Has the keystone resigned itself to being forgotten? Doesn’t the idea of standing out, of leaving its place, try to gain a foothold at times? But if it left its place, what could a keystone do except undermine the whole edifice? The foundation stone in a divine building must stay in its place. It’s very aware of unity and doesn’t want to break it, because this would open the way to partial or perhaps even total ruin. God’s, but at least a partial one: one of the walls, a tower…. This would be the accursed work of a gossip, who engenders and fosters discord among his brothers. ## OUR MOTHER, THE BLESSED VIRGIN {#inedito.growing-on-the-inside.44}

August 15, 1937

### 1) {#inedito.growing-on-the-inside.44.1}

Our “composition of place” will consist in imagining the scene transmitted to us by a pious tradition: all the Apostles gathered around our Lady’s holy body. Our petition: a vigorous enkindling of Marian love in the Work. Prayer is a school of wisdom that a soul approaches in order to listen to God. In this school he learns from two books: God and himself. By praying, he gets to know his God and his own wretchedness. To get to know God’s will more clearly, to carry out a more effective apostolate, we must always have recourse to the genuine sources: we must look at how Christ acted, at his Apostles and at the first Christians who followed him, since that is what God expects from us. He wants us to make present in the world once again the virtues and life of the first Christians. Looking at Christ’s first followers, we see how after our Lord’s Ascension and the Holy Spirit’s coming they gathered around our Lady, venerating and loving her. They entrust their concerns to her; they seek her protection when they’re besieged by persecution, setbacks, apparent failure. When their zeal for the salvation of souls seems to wane, she re-enkindles them.

Although we have a universal heart and want to bring all souls to Christ, as Spaniards we can’t forget that scene with St. James, here in our homeland on the banks of the Ebro river. Abba, Pater!, St. James cried out in his need. ‘Mother,’ he shouted as well, and our Mother, who had not yet abandoned this world, heard his cry from Jerusalem. She appears to him, surrounded by angels, and leaves as a memento of her visit a pillar which is still standing today after twenty centuries. How consoled the Apostle must have been! And what a renewed spirit of joy and peace we’ll experience as well if we have recourse to her amid our difficulties! Our prayer must be personal, intimate: a true encounter with God, one that produces “sparks,” like flint struck against stone, sparks that must illumine new paths for us. In examining my conscience, I find that if I continue to fall into the same miseries, if I persevere in evil, it’s because I’ve failed to have recourse to Mary. Therefore, imitating those who saw her face to face, I have to intensify my dealings with her, increase my sense of her presence, so that in all my falls she immediately brings me the joy of reconciliation with God.

### 2) {#inedito.growing-on-the-inside.44.2}

Theology and Mary. What does the Church teach us about our Lady? Theologians fill her with perfections; better put, it is God who fills her with his graces and the theologians acknowledge them. Mary, Mother of God, Co-redemptrix, conceived without sin. “Greater than you, only God,” they conclude. Reading their books, we learn that our Lady is the conduit, the channel who brings us heavenly gifts. Since Christ is the head and we’re his body, our Lady’s role is to be the neck. To live united to her is therefore to live united to God. So today our fervent petition is for an increase in love and devotion to our Lady. Mary, conceived without stain. Our Mother! Mother! This cry purifies our souls. To call on you in this way is a purifying water that cleanses, a sacred fire that consumes anything that stains our lips and our heart. This is the moment to give vent to our affections, to fall on our knees before our Lady, who will lift us up in her arms. We beg her: may I never again separate myself from Christ, since I’ll never separate myself from you! The fruit of our prayer must not remain only with us. Therefore, when I speak with God, with the Blessed Virgin, with the guardian angels, with the saints, I ask that the graces I implore in my beseeching be transmitted to my sons to whom I’m spiritually united. I address myself to you, my Mother, when thinking of them. I know that while they’re yours, they’re safe. I know that if they know you, if they love you, if they belong to you, they’ll be God’s. So may they truly be yours, may they love you, may they never leave you.

### 3) {#inedito.growing-on-the-inside.44.3}

We reach the third point of our meditation: the Work and Mary. How much the Work owes our Lady! Who but you, our Mother, has sustained the Work for all these years? Who has drawn new souls to this path of dedication and apostolate? Who has maintained our faith, when everything outside seemed to be falling apart? And how has the Work corresponded thus far? I can answer wholeheartedly: very well! Three loves have shone forth in Opus Dei since its beginnings, which constitute the joy and peace of her members and unceasingly inspire her supernatural spirit: love for Christ, for the Blessed Virgin and for the Pope.

The Work has corresponded very well! It’s seen in our norms and customs, with so many details that prove our devotion to our Mother: the whole Rosary, the aspiration that closes our family gatherings, the three Hail Mary’s for holy purity.... And so many others: the visits to the poor of our Lady, greeting her image upon entering a room, a Hail Holy Queen on Saturdays. Our personal correspondence must always be just as vigorous and constant. The members of the Work need the strong virtues that solid devotion to our Mother entails. It can never degenerate into routine or into a sugary “piousity.” May our devotion to Mary imitate the strength of the first Christians. On the day of her Assumption, we ask our Mother who is in heaven body and soul, who hears us and sees us: Mother, we ask you for all of your children in the Work, so that we all correspond to your favors, especially the greatest one, that of our specific Christian vocation. Help us in every moral and physical danger. As we recapitulate the points of our prayer, let’s renew our petition and offer God our resolutions.

## PERSEVERANCE {#inedito.growing-on-the-inside.49}

August 29, 1937

### 1) {#inedito.growing-on-the-inside.49.1}

Today’s Gospel recounts that Jesus was going to a city, and a great multitude was accompanying Him. Alongside Him walked his disciples, and at a distance, the rest of the crowd. The Evangelists several times stress this distinction among the Master’s followers.277 Only his disciples share his intimate friendship; the others follow Him only at a certain distance. What a joy to consider that we’re among those disciples who form a group close to Christ, apart from the multitude. To us, as to the first Twelve, as to the first seventy-two, he says: Silly fellow! I am with you until the end of the world. What security and happiness should be ours! In light of this Gospel teaching, what should I ask Jesus for? Perseverance. Perseverance, in spite of everything; perseverance for everyone. I mustn’t separate myself from our Lord’s small group of intimate friends, becoming just one more in the crowd who contemplates Him from afar. No, I want to enjoy his close, continual presence. For I’ve been called not only to live a supernatural life, but also to be an apostle, as our Lord’s

__________ 277 Cf. Lk 7:11

effective instrument in his Work. What happiness is ours, my God! One form of mental prayer is to make acts of thanksgiving. How many acts of thanksgiving we owe to God, for so many reasons! Thank you, Lord, because you are Father. Thank you, because you are Love, and King. Thank you for our Christian vocation, and for having chosen us, for the special affection with which you honor us. Each one of you can add here his own words, going to St. Joseph and to your guardian angel. In my life as a priest, how many souls I’ve guided towards a real friendship with their guardian angel! How many invocations have been directed to St. Raphael the archangel, thanks to the spirit of the Work taking root in souls! And to St. Peter, to St. John and St. Paul, to St. Gabriel and St. Michael. How could they ever leave us alone in our struggle? They’re witnesses to it; they know how the brambles along the way tug at us and at times bloody us. Well, fight, pray an invocation, and onward! If God allows a pothole in the road to trip us up and we fall over, it doesn’t matter. We call on our Mother, our Spes, and then we get up and continue forward. If out of carelessness we’ve fallen behind, we go to our guardian angel. And we regain our place—alongside Christ! Perseverance. That advice of a saint comes to mind: “To begin is for many; to persevere is for few.” Many flowers blossom, but few reach full maturity and yield succulent fruit. I don’t want to be a sterile flower. I want to be filled with fruit. I don’t want to fall behind and vanish among the multitude without ideals; and much less do I want to be among those who see Jesus but don’t look at Him, who hear his words but don’t listen to Him, who are witnesses to a miracle but fail to give themselves. How can I sincerely say that I love Jesus, if I fail to give myself? Yes, I have to give myself, but truly, perseveringly.

### 2) {#inedito.growing-on-the-inside.49.2}

The Gospel adds that Jesus entered the city of Naim and came upon a young man’s funeral. Beside the coffin was his mother, who was crying. We may have found ourselves enclosed, as it were, in a coffin, and Jesus, moved to mercy by the maternal tears of the Church or our Lady, rescued us, restoring us to life. This miracle is repeated in our own day. We too, as our Lord’s disciples, can count on special graces. Just as the first disciples who followed Jesus, and later Peter, raised the dead by their own hands, so we too are instruments for our Lord to give life to souls. In our work of selection, in mobilizing young intellectuals in the apostolate, we will sometimes find smart, generous, well-disposed young people, full of noble sentiments, but who are imprisoned in the coffin of pride, of ambition, of fleshly passions. ## VOCATION {#inedito.growing-on-the-inside.50}

August 30, 1937

### 1) {#inedito.growing-on-the-inside.50.1}

Today’s Gospel refers to the man who finds a treasure hidden in a field, and goes to sell what he has to buy the field and thus gain possession of the treasure.282 On our path in life, we too have stumbled upon an amazing treasure: our vocation as children of God. To gain this treasure, to acquire the happiness of following Christ, we need to renounce everything. The goods that I must sell are my ambitions, my concupiscence, my dreams of earthly happiness, my affections that stem from flesh and blood, which are noble but tie me down, my professional dreams, even though these will often fit in with my apostolic plans. Is this all I need to sell? Even harder, I have to sell my will. Is there still something left to renounce? Yes, my entire ‘I’, my self-complacency. In a word, my self-love. How hard it is to renounce this self-love! How often it robs us of peace and leads us astray. Our dignity, we think, is at stake. We think that something is a matter of justice; we need to do things in a certain way and in no other. But wouldn’t the man in the parable have

__________ 282 Cf. Mt 13:44

been foolish to have scorned the treasure he found? Aren’t we foolish if, by not rejecting our wretched self-love, we abandon the treasure of our dedication to Christ? Let’s tell our Lord, addressing Him with ardent affection, silencing our intellect and allowing our heart to speak: I want to be your slave in your Work. Even if I were not just one man but a hundred men, even if I had a thousand lives, I would dedicate all of them to your service. Even if I were to undergo such a great misfortune as to be left alone, come what may I’ll persevere on this path, with your help. And we will continue giving free reign to our affections: first humility, for having chosen me despite my wretchedness, for entrusting this treasure to me; then thanksgiving, for letting it fall into my hands; then cries of petition that I may correspond, asking for perseverance, for me, for all my brothers.
