## Mother of God and our Mother {#inedito.mother-of-god-and-our-mother}

Iter para tutum!

On the 50th Anniversary of the first Consecration of the Work to the Most Sweet Heart of Mary

Invoke our Lady with this aspiration: Cor Mariae Dulcissimum, iter para tutum! It is a filial cry that came constantly to my heart and to my lips at very specific moments in the history of our Work. Some day, when I am no longer here, you will learn about it... They wanted to break up the blessed unity of the two sections, which would have meant tearing my soul in two... Not having anyone here on earth to turn to, I turned to our heavenly Mother, so that both sections of the Work might always be like two little donkeys pulling the same divine cart along a safe path, which was gradually being opened up with the gentle ‘violence’ of God’s works... Don’t forget, my children, that the safety of this way also depends on you, and on the effort you put into being faithful, into being saints. 1

A few days before 15 August 1951, our Father announced that he would be in Loreto on the solemnity of the Assumption. He was going to the Holy House to put the Work in our Lady’s hands, and he asked his children to accompany him with their prayers and mortification, repeating the aspiration, Cor Mariae Dulcissimum, iter para tutum! He consecrated the Work to the Most Sweet Heart of Mary during the Mass, and returned to Rome certain that he had been listened to. As it is now many years since that date, the soul is filled with acts of thanksgiving and of petitions to our Mother, so that she continues protecting and blessing us as she has always done. Don Alvaro often reminded us how, from the beginning, St Josemaría worked, thinking about his daughters and sons of all times. Moved by God, his gaze projected beyond the horizon of the particular historical circumstances in which he was living. Covering the future, he had in mind those who would come to Opus Dei with the passage of centuries. And he entrusted everyone, men and women, to the Most Sweet Heart of Mary at the Shrine of Loreto. The history of the Church and the altogether shorter history of the Work – a history of the mercies of God – has taught us to distinguish the transitory from the permanent. Contradictions pass away, and those who promoted them, well-intentioned or not, pass into oblivion. But God’s plans are always confirmed. And since that 15 August 1951, Providence has established that the children of God in his Work, are lodged, with a special privilege, within the Most Sweet Heart of Mary. In a get-together in 1955, our Father said: The Work has the aroma of a ripe field, a complete thing, despite twenty-seven years being nothing for a moral entity, and less for a family which the Lord has wanted to promote and has to last while there are men on earth, to serve the Church, to extend the kingdom of Christ, for the good of souls, doing something pleasant for humanity, leading it to God.2 Opus Dei continues being young, and long is the journey still to be travelled; we sometimes advance along flat and wellworn paths; others along stony mountain paths, requiring more of an effort to traverse. But our Lady, Regina Operis Dei, will always come to our side protecting us and opening up a way for us. We have wanted to recognize the anthology of phrases of our Father where there clearly appears the Blessed Virgin Mary’s protection for the Church, the Work and each one of us. These teachings of his will serve as food for our prayer and acts of thanksgiving, and as a stimulus to pray with even more confidence in our Lady: Cor Mariae Dulcissimum, iter para tutum, iter serva tutum!

Mother of God and our Mother

Among the prayers which our Father learned from the lips of the Grandparents, there appears this filial offering to the Blessed Virign Mary: My Lady, my Mother! Oh my Lady, Oh my Mother! I offer myself entirely to you. And to prove my filial devotion, I consecrate to you this day my eyes, my ears, my tongue, my heart... In a word, all of my being. Since I am already all yours, O Mother of Goodness, guard me and defend me as something belonging to you. He took every step of his life – the important ones and the ones of less transcendence – holding our Lady’s hand, just like a small son who only feels secure when his mother holds him. Marian filiation has been deeply rooted in the life of the Work since the 2nd of October 1928. During the summer of 1951 our Father lived through a period of great disquiet. I felt a tremendous anxiety in my soul, he wrote a few months later, because our Lord gave me an intuition that something very serious was being hatched against the Work.3 And his reaction could not be other than what he had always done: to turn to the Most Blessed Virgin Mary. When I consecrated the Work to the Most Sweet Heart of Mary – he related in a meditation a few years later – in the little house of Nazareth venerated in Loreto, I felt myself to be very much your Father. I went there in the name of all my daughters and sons, in difficult times in the history of our Opus Dei, in proof of devotion, love and confidence in our Mother the Blessed Virgin.4

It is only right that we should thank the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit for the road we have already covered, offering our thanksgiving through the Virgin Mary: Cor Maríae dulcíssimum, iter para tutum! She is the Mother of God, the Mother of men, the Mother of Opus Dei, the Mother of each one of us.5

Have you seen how mothers act? Each one of you recall your own, I will think of mine. There comes to mind the scene of a young little child escaping from the arms of his mother and, on his little legs which can barely support him, seeks out an adventure. And if his mother is not careful, he moves out onto the street and mixes not only with pedestrians but among vehicles. But the mother comes out running after him, gathers him in, embraces him, and presses him to her heart. Children of my soul, this is the sure refuge which we have in our Mother of Heaven! I say this now for you and for myself: when the imagination turns a little mad, when sensuality attempts to drag us down, when pride threatens to darken our imagination … Come, my Mother, our Mother, take us in your arms and press us to your Heart.6

Father, why is the Virgin Mary so loved in Opus Dei? Because she is the Mother of God and our Mother. It does not concern me to repeat once more (…) that for this interior struggle, which no one knows other than when we talk in confidence to one who can listen to us or in Confession, for this struggle there is also a password: let each one prepare his own. I have the custom of changing it every day, but during this last period, I have not changed it. I am going to tell you what it is. Many times a day, dozens, many dozens, I call on the Mother of God and I say to her: Mother, my Mother, My Mother !!! And my soul is filled with consolation, with happiness. And it seems to me that the gates of hell will not prevail. 7

Humanly speaking, when someone is wished happy birthday, that person wants to reciprocate. It seems daring for us to speak of reciprocating on the part of the Blessed Virgin, on the part of one who is so generous, who is the Mother of God, the conduit for all the waters of grace that make the field of our life and apostolate fertile. But it would be a lack of naturalness for us not to be daring like this. Mother of ours, today we hope for your gifts. Today we will feel the gentleness of your hands, the warmth of your Most Sweet Heart, your protection for the whole Work. And despite the realization that we haven’t been good sons, we dare to say: Monstra te esse matrem! Show us that you are our Mother. Make us be good sons. You who are the Mother of Fair Love, Seat of Wisdom, Handmaid of the Lord: Sancta María, fílios tuos ádiuva!8

What are we before God? Nothing! And nevertheless, he is in our soul and we are his children, and children of his very Mother. Have recourse to our Lady. I remember my mother’s lessons, her caresses, the prayers she got me to repeat, her constant concern for me. These memories lead me to consider the role of the Mother of God and our Mother. Mary will give to our life, as she did with hers, the divine significance that draws God’s loving gaze and heaven’s grace.9

I, who have instructed my children in the path of spiritual childhood, without obliging them to follow it, can assure you, from what I know about souls, that with the passage of the years they will find shelter on that path, and they won’t find another, because it is the only safe one. And so the soul sees itself as a child which has to be cleaned, fed and led by the hand, needing its Heavenly Mother and its Father God to embrace it and tell it: you are weakness itself, but with us you are strength; you are dullness itself, but with us you are wisdom; you are a piece of mortal flesh, but with us you are immortality. And so it finds peace, even though all its wretchedness – and that which, by God’s mercy, it has not experienced – looms like a great enemy, inside and outside. It understands that all goodness is on loan, and it feels the need to pray: oportet semper orare et non deficere: to pray always and not lose heart (Luke 17:1), all the

I have always recommended you to say aspirations, and right now I am constantly repeating one which, without great supernatural awareness but with a lot of devotion, I used to say when I was small: Sweet Heart of Mary, be my salvation.10

I usually abandon myself, I try to make myself small and place myself in the arms of our Lady. And I tell our Lord: Jesus make a bit of space for me! Let’s see if both of us fit in the arms of your Mother! And that’s enough. But you, continue along your way: mine doesn’t have to be yours. All of us in the Work follow a general path, which is identical. But within this track which is so wide, long live freedom!11

I want to tell you, that, if this characteristic of carrying on a continuous and trusting relationship with our Mother is so essential to our spirit, then as time goes on, we have more reason than ever to do so. Because it becomes evident to us that she intervenes in our favour, as by a miracle, through ordinary Providence. It is something wonderful, something that seems incredible, and yet it is true (…) I had a little statue of the Blessed Virgin which the communists stole from me during the war in Spain. I called it Our Lady of the Kisses. In the first residence which we had, I never went in or out without going to the Director’s room, where the image was, so I could give it a kiss. I don’t think I ever did so mechanically. My kisses were human kisses, the kisses of a son who was afraid… But since I’ve said so often that I’m not afraid of anyone or anything, let’s not say I was afraid. They were kisses from a son who felt far too young and who sought from our Lady all her tenderness and her affection. All the strength I needed, I sought from God through the Blessed Virgin. Mary’s divine Motherhood: her special Motherhood towards the Work, and her very special Motherhood towards each of God’s children in Opus Dei. Grátias tibi, Deus, grátias tibi: vera et una Trínitas, una et summa Déitas, sancta et una Únitas.12

Supplicant All-Powerful

The faithful of all times have shown their faith in the powerful mediation of the Virgin Mary in very many ways: Mater Dei, Mater Creatoris, Virgo Potens, Auxilium Christianorum... Our Father often involved her like this: Sancta Maria, Refugium nostrum et Virtus. The constellation of Marian shrines which the faithful have disseminated through the globe is a sign of the continuous recourse of the Church to the intercession of Our Lady. On his return from that journey to Loreto, our Father related how much effort he had to make to be recollected during the Holy Mass. Simple people, who went in their hundreds on such a great feast-day, kissed without reserve the walls of the House of Nazareth, and even the cloths on which he was celebrating the Holy Sacrifice. With that gesture, they trusted their intentions, large and small, to the All-powerful Supplication. That of our Father’s was an enormous weight which he carried in his heart. And that was the only place where the burden could be lightened.

Above the altar, on what acts as a reredos, are seen those moving words: Hic Verbum caro factum est. How I would like to go back to Loreto! I go to Loreto now in my desire, looking to find a little corner in that holy house and to say and contemplate that Hic Verbum caro factum est, because Jesus who takes on our flesh, and who is perféctus Deus, cannot fail to move the hearts of all Christians. I am convinced, I am moved. I wanted to say to you a few, but very clear things: and one of these is, that to reach the Blessed Trinity, I passed through Mary, and through Mary got to Jesus, perfectus Deus, perfectus homo. 13

My children, invoke our Lady with all your hearts, trustingly. She has been our greatest protector, our greatest recourse, ever since the second of October 1928, and even before then.14

Before, I didn’t ask. I lived in this way because I saw it was better to abandon oneself trustingly in God. Doing so, in those first moments, was good because thus it was clear that everything came from him. But now I see that I should ask, and I understand better the force of those words of our Lord: Ask, and it will be given you; seek, and you will find; knock, and it will be opened to you (Luke 11:9). I am convinced that we need to pray a lot, and I want to place my prayer in our Lady’s hand, a thousand times blessed.15

I raise my heart to God, and I ask him through the intercession of the Blessed Virgin – who is in the Church and yet above the Church, who is between Christ and the Church, protecting us and reigning over us, the Mother of all mankind, as she is of God,

I raise my heart to ask her to give us a lot of tact and much discretion, and great piety, because we will need it.16

We have suffered in those moments when we were more in need of prayer and affection than criticism. But the grace of God has never been lacking nor the protection of his blessed Mother and our Mother, for us to understand that it is the fatherly providence where we find the strength which makes us mature. In my country they pinch the first flowering of figs so that they sweeten and ripen more quickly. God our Lord, to make us more effective, has blessed us with the Cross.17

There is no storm which can drown the heart of the Virgin Mother of God. When the storm arrives, each one of us fights and, to be certain, we seek sure refuge in the Most Sweet Heart of Mary. She, the Blessed Virgin, is our security, she is the Mother of Fair Love, the Seat of Wisdom, the Mediatrix of all graces, which will lead us by the hand to her Son, Jesus.18

I am going to tell you something which God our Lord wants you to know. We children of God in Opus Dei, adeamus cum fiducia – we must go with a lot of faith – ad thronum gloriae, to the throne of glory, to the Blessed Virgin, Mother of God and our Mother, whom we invoke so often as Sedes Sapientiae, ut misericordiam consequamur, so that we may obtain mercy. Let us go through the Most Sweet Heart of Mary to the Most Sacred and Merciful Heart of Jesus, and ask him, through his mercy, to show his power in the Church and fill us with strength to continue along our way, drawing many souls to him. Adeamus cum fiducia ad thronum gloriae, ut misericordiam consequamur (cf Heb 4:16). May you keep it very much in mind right now, and later on too. I would say it is God’s wish: that we put our personal interior life into the words I have just told you. At times you will hear them with no noise at all, in the intimacy of your soul, when you least expect it. Adeamus cum fiducia: Go, I’ll say again, with confidence to the most Sweet Heart of Mary, who is our Mother and Mother of Jesus. And with her, who is Mediatrix of all graces, to the most Sacred and Merciful Heart of Jesus Christ. With confidence too, and offering him reparation for so many offences.19

Father, why has the Lord wanted to make Mary the Mediatrix of all graces? Because she was so humble. The Magnificat says so: He looks on his servant in her lowliness; henceforth all ages will call me blessed… So that all of us may call his Mother, Blessed, God has said: Nothing passes from Heaven to humanity except through the hands of my Mother. The Virgin Mary is the aqueduct of divine grace. Do you know what aqueduct means? No? It is a channel through which water flows. Our Mother is the aqueduct

God’s grace is abundant, and our Lady is omnipotent in her intercession. When Mary asks God for something, her prayer is always heard. That’s why she is said to be “almighty in supplication”. Well, our great Lady and Mother, the Virgin Mary, who has always protected Opus Dei, will do so until the end. If we don’t leave her, she won’t leave us.21

May your prayer reach God through the most sweet Heart of our Mother, our Blessed Lady. Get her to intercede, as she did at the wedding feast at Cana: When the wine failed, she said to Jesus, They have no wine (John 2:3). You will see how this flood of muddy water that threatens to swamp everything, will turn into Christ’s wine: nourishment – doctrine – to comfort us in our weakness; and strength, to set our souls on fire with faith, hope, love, and union. 22

My children: when you are sad and when you are cheerful; when your wretchedness is more or less apparent, and when it weighs more, always turn to Mary, because she never abandons us, on this path of service to her Son Jesus in the midst of the world.23

Our Marian devotion

You well know, my dearest daughters and sons, that our Mother is Queen of Opus Dei. To increase in you the love for our Lady, I have told you that I, who cannot suggest myself as a model in anything, would like you to imitate me in the love that I have for our Mother Holy Mary. 24

The goal is a high one. There are many details of refinement which filial affection suggested to the heart of our Father: praises and requests; flattering comments and petitions; from the untiring entreaty when he received an inkling of the divine call – Domina, ut videam!, Domina, ut sit! – to his final loving glance at the image of Our Lady of Guadalupe, in the room where he used to work.

All the pictures and statues, all the names, all the advocations that Christians give to Holy Mary seem marvellous to me. But I have a special debt to Our Lady of Loreto.25

Be pious, my dear children. From the moment you get up in the morning, lovingly say to our Lady the prayers I’m sure your mothers taught you. I like to say them slowly: Blessed be your purity… My Lady, my Mother… They are marvellous prayers. Say them as you are getting ready, until the moment comes to receive our Lord Jesus Christ, a prisoner of love in the tabernacle: imprisoned because he loves us. By loving the Blessed Virgin you will learn to be contemplatives, and to act naturally and simply, without doing strange things. Be pious, and everything in your life will go well. Do not invent sorrows. You will always be joyful and contented.26

Seek God hidden in the depths of your pure heart, in the depths of your being when you are faithful, and never lose that intimacy. And if you ever find that you don’t know how to speak or what to say, and you dare not seek the Child again within your own heart, turn to Mary, tota pulchra, our Lady, our wonderful Mother, and say: the Lord wanted you, with your own hands, to take care of God; teach me to get to know your Son!27

Do you want to cast an eye over your own behaviour? In Opus Dei, along with divine filiation, we are deeply conscious of our filiation to Mary. How well do you look after the Marian Norms and Customs for me? How well do you try to fulfil the delightful details of love that each one enshrines? How hard do you try, how much filial affection do you put into them? How much do you try to please her who is the channel of all the graces coming from her Son to succour your brothers and sisters, the Work, the Holy Church, and the entire human race? 28

In Opus Dei we have constantly shown our love for our Lady, by putting up millions of pictures and statues of her around the world, and fostering practices of Marian devotion in every continent: in Europe, in Asia, in Africa, in the Americas, in Australasia; leading young people in that direction, with freedom. Without freedom, no. But this is natural: how can we fail to love the Mother of God? She is our Mother. What’s more, we need her! I need her. Just as a small child, when it’s afraid of the dark, cries “Mummy!”, that’s how I often have to shout in my heart, not aloud: “Mother! Mummy, don’t leave me.” Interior life is that: naturalness, simplicity. I can’t live in any other way: I have to live as a human being does. And before God, who is eternal, I am a small child who is worth nothing. 29

Then you know what you have to do: to be holy, as your heavenly father is holy. This is a clear, obvious call. We have the protection of the Mother of God. Every night and every morning, I like to repeat and not in a low voice but loudly: sub tuum presidium confugimus, Sancta Dei Genitrix. And as I am a priest, I say to her:

Mother, lead me to celebrate the Mass digne, pie ac devote, dignified, pious and with devotion, so that, in persona Christi, I renew the divine sacrifice of Calvary and consecrate the Body and the Blood of my Lord Jesus. 30

In the Mass, I am surrounded by Angels, who are present at the Holy Sacrifice, praising the Trinity and adoring the Body, the Blood, the Soul and the Divinity of Jesus Christ. And I know that, in some way, the Blessed Virgin also takes part, through the intimate union she has with the Blessed Trinity, as Daughter, Mother and Spouse of God – and because she is the Mother of Jesus. Each one of us carries the blood of our parents. Jesus Christ, conceived in the womb of Mary most Holy by the work of the Holy Spirit, only has the blood of his Mother. This is the Blood which is offered to the Eternal Father in the Holy Sacrifice of the Mass.31

We are carrying out a great campaign to defend the recital of the Rosary throughout the world. Our Lady will be very content, because it is the devil who wishes to take away this marvellous devotion from the Christian people. That is why the devil does not like it to be prayed, and tries his best to uproot it from customs. The Rosary, is moreover, a prayer at the reach of all backgrounds, because it is very easy to close one’s eyes a little, imagine the scene of the mystery, so say a few little words of affection, and then to repeat the Our Fathers, Hail Marys and Glory be’s, and the Litany: so many invocations which are burning praise of the Blessed Virgin, signs of affection. 32

De Maria numquam satis, the saying goes: when it comes to Mary, to loving and venerating her, one can never say enough.33

Under the mantle of Our Lady

In May 1956, our Father gave a son of his the job of preparing a painting which would have as its theme our Lady protecting Opus Dei. After various attempts, the project that went ahead was an image of Regina Operis Dei, which represents the Mother of God holding her Son in her left hand; with the right, she holds out the very edge of her cape. Under the mantle of the Virgin, our Father wanted a milestone with the seal of the Work; and beside it, some small pebbles that had been thrown at the milestone which was chipped off in some parts, with some of its pieces on the floor... Opus Dei is symbolised in this way under the mantle of our Lady who protected us, some twenty-six years ago, from those strong and hidden attacks from outside, while Opus Dei, firm, compact and secure, was strengthened and spread, and she has cared for us always, with her affection, with her smile and with her intercession. How many times they have tried to ill-treat the Work! But since you are our Mother, they have never succeeded. If we are faithful, they will never achieve anything.34

In memory of the consecration of Opus Dei to our Blessed Mother, and so that there may always be souls who seek her protection, it is my wish that, in both Sections of the Work, many times a day, there should arise from our hearts the cry: Cor Mariae Dulcissimum, iter para tutum! Most Sweet Heart of Mary, prepare a safe way!35

Our Opus Dei was born and has developed under our Lady’s mantle. She has been the good Mother to us, consoling us, smiling at us and encouraging us in all the difficult moments of the blessed struggle to bring forth this army of apostles in the world.36

Under the mantle of our Holy Mother Mary, who has always managed to protect you in the midst of the waves, under the protective mantle of our heavenly Mother, you who are a little yeast or leaven will ferment the dough of mankind. Then you will feel the same yearning that brought me to write: Omnes, everyone, for not a single soul should be lost, omnes cum Petro ad Iesum per Mariam!37

We should be full of confidence as far as the future is concerned, trusting in the maternal, loving intercession of our Lady: Recordáre, Virgo Mater Dei, dum stéteris in conspéctu Dómini, ut lóquaris pro nobis bona. A trusting and tender recourse to our Mother is a principal part of our spirit. Mary has given us tangible proof of her special favour; she has always smiled on us. When we realize how small and weak we are, and how often we go wrong, we will seek all the strength we need from God through our filial devotion to Mary.38

My dearest daughters and sons, when contemplating now the great extension of our Work, this batch of marvellous bread is already fragrant with the good aroma of Christ (2 Cor 2:15) throughout the world, these thousands of souls are thinking only of giving glory to God and being in the service of the Holy Church, I constantly raise my heart to Heaven in acts of thanksgiving; with this beautiful reality – lay people, in so many different situations, men and women, priests – with an enchanting expansion, there must be moments of affliction because we are starting – twenty five years is very little – we have to lower our heads lovingly, directing ourselves to God, and give him thanks: gratias tibi, Deus, gratias tibi. And turning too to Our Mother in Heaven, who has been present on every occasion, from the first moment: gratias tibi, Sancta Maria Mater.39
