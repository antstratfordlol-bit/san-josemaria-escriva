# Preface {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.presentacion}

From the beginning of the Work, together with other means of formation which are traditional in the Church, our beloved Father used a specific way of transmitting Christian doctrine and the spirit of Opus Dei: the get-togethers. At those family gatherings people asked him questions and Blessed Josemaría would remind them of the replies, both ever new and ever old, contained in the doctrine of Jesus Christ, while adapting them to the needs of his listeners with the gift of tongues. Many of us had the good fortune to take part in those gettogethers personally, and lovingly preserve the memory of them as a rich treasure. Others have seen the films which were made of them thanks to Don Alvaro’s filial affection. Those films enable us all to be present, in a way, at our Father’s get-togethers. Blessed Josemaría learnt his simple and attractive way of transmitting the Christian faith and the spirit of the Work from the Gospel. When commenting on the beginning of the Acts of the Apostles, he would often point out how our Lord, in the weeks that he remained on earth after his resurrection, spent time with his

disciples, instructing them and speaking to them about the kingdom of God.1 They spoke about all sorts of things, our Founder would add, about whatever they asked him: they had a get-together. Here we are imitating him in some way, because you and I are disciples of the Lord, and we want to exchange ideas: we are in a gettogether.2 Our get-togethers have a Gospel-like flavour, our Father continued; they are a wonderful way of speaking about the doctrine of Jesus Christ and about how to practise it, in a family atmosphere. As you see, I am not exaggerating when I say that the Work is one big catechesis. That’s exactly what Opus Dei is.3

Part of our Father’s teachings during his long journey through Spain and Portugal in 1972 appeared in the books entitled Dos meses de catequesis (“Two Months of Catechesis”). Some of the questions and answers from those meetings, on various matters such as the faith, the sacraments, Christian life, apostolate, the priesthood, and the family, were collected together at the end of those volumes. In response to repeated requests from my daughters and sons, we have brought together in this book the section on marriage and the family already published in those two volumes, enriching it with other questions and answers on the same subject drawn from our Father’s catechesis during his trips to Latin America in 1974 and 1975. They will be of benefit to us all, helping us to meditate again on our Father’s teachings and intensify our apostolate in all the environments of this world of ours, which we must bring to God. Although this selection is so copious, it cannot be called an anthology of texts on marriage and the family. Many other aspects of our Father’s teachings, expressed in his Letters and Instructions, meditations, homilies, etc., are not covered here. In bringing these passages together in this book the aim has been to nourish the

1 Cf. Acts 1:2-3. 2 From our Father in Dos meses de catequesis (sv) vol. 2, p. 651. 3 Ibid., vol. 1, p. 137.

spiritual life of everyone, and especially of my Supernumerary daughters and sons. Reading and meditating on these words will help them to sanctify themselves in their family life, while receiving ever new lights to tackle and answer many questions related to their vocation as fathers and mothers of Christian families. Because of the nature of this collection, a certain amount of repetition is inevitable. But even in these cases we can read the words of our Founder and appreciate the novelty they contain: by adapting his answers to the specific circumstances of the person asking the question, our Father is able to transmit different aspects or to fill out what had just been hinted at on other occasions, thus giving a more complete reply. Through this repetition, he also engraved upon people’s memories the key ideas which should govern marriage and the family – institutions of enormous and permanent importance for the future of the Church and civil society. I ask the Blessed Virgin Mary as Regina familiæ, Queen of the Family, that the reading and meditation of these pages may help the faithful of the Prelature to carry out an even more incisive and effective apostolate in the field of the family, which is so vast; so that in many homes the light of Christian doctrine and the spirit of Opus Dei may be lit and may shine, and they thus become bright and cheerful homes, as our beloved and holy Founder desired.

Your Father + Javier

Rome, 27 November 1996 Anniversary of the death of the Grandfather

# Christian marriage {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.1}

Christian marriage is both a contract and a sacrament: the union of one man with one woman, for ever, and a source of grace instituted by Jesus Christ. I speak about the Sacrament of Marriage with emotion because I remember the love of my parents. How well they responded – in all the circumstances of their lives, and there were some quite difficult moments – encouraging one another, bringing us up in a Christian way, accepting the will of God with the piety God’s grace inspired in them. When human love is noble and good, it deserves that I, as a priest, should bless it with both hands. Guadalaviar (Valencia), 18 November 1972

## Preparing for marriage {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.1.1}

Father, my fiancée and I would like to ask you for a present, to speak to us about engagement and marriage. Yes, my children, human love is a splendid adventure. I know this from God’s love, which is much greater, but is compatible with human love; with holy human love, like yours.

I tell you: love one another, talk and listen to one another, get to know one another. Respect one another, as if each were a treasure belonging to the other. Do not forget that God our Lord is present, that he sees you, that he hears you (...) As you have a great love for the person whom you have chosen to be the mother of your children, may you never be ashamed of that love. Respect her. That doesn’t mean love her less; you will love her all the more. And then the Lord, quite soon, will bless your marriage, and will make it bright, cheerful and happy, and it will be a love that will reach up to heaven. Altoclaro (Caracas), 11 February 1975

## A great sacrament {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.1.2}

Father, please speak to us about vocation within marriage and about the calling that our Lord makes to each one of us. My children, marriage is a sacrament. St Paul tells us it is a great sacrament. I love this sacrament as much as the other six, with all my heart as a priest, and with all my heart as a man (…) Marriage is between one man and one woman, and is forever. Anything else is not love: it is concupiscence. In marriage your bodies and souls, your whole beings, are united. Husband and wife become, in St Paul’s forceful phrase, one single flesh; so they must not then separate and go off and give themselves to dogs. I’ve said enough! Altoclaro (Caracas), 11 February 1975

As Christians, you are all priests. And so are your wives. It’s St Peter who says this, not I: Vos autem genus electum, regale sacerdotium, gens sancta, populus acquisitionis … (1 Pet 2:9), “But you are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a

holy nation...” and you need to present offerings to him: you must offer him your personal Mass, your life. I am not asking you to live like friars. I do not live like a friar: I am not one. God has not given me that vocation. Neither has he given it to you: you are married men, ordinary folk… That’s why I’m not asking you to live like friars, but like ordinary Christians. But honest Christians, who tell our Lord: “I’m finding this or that a bit difficult…” I find things difficult too! Did you imagine that I don’t? There wouldn’t be much to it if I didn’t find things hard! All the years I have lived – not so many, but not so few either – wouldn’t have done me any good… So, as Christians, you are priests, with a royal priesthood. You do not have the ministerial priesthood, because you have not received the sacrament of Holy Orders. Our Lord has called you along another path, which is also divine. Yes, Marriage is a sacrament – it couldn’t be more divine – instituted by Jesus Christ, just like the Sacrament of Holy Orders! And it is a sacrament that St Paul calls a great sacrament, sacramentum magnum (Eph 5:32)! Therefore, you have a royal priesthood, and what’s more, you are participating in a great sacrament. Alameda (Santiago de Chile), 1 July 1974

## For those recently married {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.1.3}

What advice would you give to a recently married couple? First, love one another a lot, in accordance with God’s law. Then, do not be afraid of life. Love all of each other’s defects, as long as they are not an offence against God. And then, try not to neglect yourself, because you do not belong to yourself. You will have been told (and you know it full well) that you belong to your husband, and he belongs to you.

Don’t let anyone steal him from you! He is a soul who ought to go to heaven together with you, and, as well as that, together with you he should give a Chilean quality (that is, a Christian quality), and also human grace, to all the children God sends you. Pray a bit together. Not much, but a little every day. When you forget, he can remind you; and when he forgets, you remind him. Never reproach him, don’t go mortifying him with your grumbles. Tabancura (Santiago de Chile), 7 July 1974

## A programme for marriage {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.1.4}

As for those of you who are married, I congratulate you. But I tell you, don’t let your love wither; try to stay young forever, to keep yourselves wholly for one another; get to the point where you love one another so much that you love your spouses’ defects, provided they don’t offend God. Never complain about each other. If you do complain, it’s a sign you don’t love one another enough, because you will always have defects. I have them, despite my age, and I keep on struggling against them. Do the same yourselves. I’ll tell you once again: love your husbands’ defects, if they don’t offend God. And if they do, then with your affection, little by little, you’ll be able to help them to change. You will be filled with happiness and encouragement. They will learn to love your defects too, and will treat you very tenderly, gladly sacrificing themselves for you. When you love that way, you will have learnt what real love is. Guadalaviar (Valencia), 18 November 1972

## Two obligations that are but one {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.1.5}

Father, how can we make dedication to our family compatible with dedication to God? My son, you won’t be pleasing God if you don’t give

yourself to your family. You must love your family. Are you single, married, a widower, or what? Married, Father. Wonderful! Love your wife very much, with all your heart; try to raise your children well; try to work hard for their sake, to please God and to do good to your country. If you act that way, you will deserve to be called a loyal man and a Christian. There’s no conflict between those two duties, because they come together as one, just as the different strands of a cord, when woven together, form a strong rope. Tajamar (Madrid), 28 October 1972

## A clean love {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.1.6}

Father, purity is seen as a virtue for young people, young men and women who are unmarried. Could you speak to us a little and, if you can, a lot, about how to live this virtue well as adults and specifically as married people? My daughter, those who are married must practise chastity in marriage; so they have a duty to love one another – the wife loving her husband, and the husband his wife – in accordance with the natural law and with God’s law; and continue being chaste, while loving each other a lot. Those who are not married must abstain totally from sexual things. Is this clear? And those who are married may only have relations with their own spouse; that is what a clean life means. But, just like everyone else, they cannot let themselves be carried away by any desires or thoughts that are outside the holy path of the Sacrament of Matrimony. A man and a woman may love each other with a clean love only if they are on the path towards marriage, and then, after they marry, until death; she being faithful to her husband and he being faithful to his wife. Altoclaro (Caracas), 11 February 1975

## To improve married life {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.1.7}

How can we improve our married life? If you are a good Christian, you will treat your wife with great affection. If you are a good Christian, you won’t put obstacles to life. You’ll work hard to support your family and raise your children well, trying to contribute actively to their Christian formation. You won’t neglect this duty, with the excuse that your wife is already taking care of it, or that you’ve put your children in a good school. Besides, who can trust schools nowadays? You need to follow each step of those children whom God has given you, and you will come to understand that the best and most important business in your life is to form your children well. It’s not enough to bring them into the world; animals do that much. You must pass on to them your spirituality, your Christian yearnings and ideals, your love of God, your devotion to the Blessed Virgin Mary. My daughters, you know that your love for your husband and for each child is part of your love for God. So is the care you have put into raising your children, that is, in bringing them up in a Christian way. I was tempted to say “creating” them,1 as if you shared even more actively in the power of God, who creates things out of nothing. Do you understand me? So, each time a child of yours makes progress, give a loving cry of thanks to God. If one of them falls behind a bit

– I don’t want to be specific – give another cry: “Lord, help me to help this one move forward!” And you help him with affection, with the almost infinite kindliness mothers have in their hearts, getting your husband to help you in this task too. After all, when all is said and done, you are the ones in charge at home! Guadalaviar (Valencia), 18 November 1972

1 A play in Spanish on the words “criar,” to raise, and “crear,” to create (Translator).

## Peace and good humour {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.1.8}

Father, how can we ensure peace in our families? I usually say that if homes are at peace, it’s because the women are in charge – and even more so when the husband thinks he’s the one in charge. But don’t turn into tyrants, trying to monopolize your children’s love. You would be making a serious mistake, and you would end up by being unhappy. You should lavish love on your husbands and, along with your own affection, give them the children’s affection too, so that they capture their father’s heart, and oblige him to help you bring them up. Otherwise, when base passions begin to stir, the boys think that piety and clean living are just for women, and that’s not true. They are also for men, for very manly men. That’s why I always teach you that you women should be the ones in charge at home, but with your husband’s agreement: you know very well how to convince him ! Enxomil (Portugal), 31 October 1972

I like to say that, in a family, the mother is the Minister of Home Affairs and Minister of Finance, but that you should leave the position of Minister of Foreign Affairs to the father, and never contradict him in front of the children. When children see dad and mum in agreement, when they see that you are united, how happy it makes them! How delighted they are! How well they understand that it is God who makes a home! Perhaps they don’t realize it at that moment, but they’ll understand it later. Gaztelueta (Bilbao), 12 October 1972

## Don’t argue in front of the children {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.1.9}

Why do you say that you bless human love? … and I cannot but bless that human love, which our Lord

has asked me personally to give up. But I love it in others, in the love my parents had, in your love, in that of spouses for each other. So, love each other truly! And, as I always advise you, husband and wife, few quarrels. It’s better not to put your happiness at risk. You wives give in a little. He will also give in. What’s more, never ever argue in front of the children; because children notice everything, and immediately make judgements. They don’t know that St Paul wrote qui iudicat Dominus est (1 Cor 4:4), that it is for the Lord to judge. So they set themselves up as masters, even though they may be just three or four years old, and think: “Mummy is bad”, or “Daddy is bad”. They get into a terrible muddle, poor things! Don’t provoke that tragedy in your children’s hearts. Wait a while, be patient; you can quarrel later, when the child is asleep. But not much, and knowing that you are wrong. You’ll have got over your bad temper, and the one of you who thinks they’re right should say to the other: “Forgive me, because I really am impatient, but I love you with all my heart...” and you give each other a big hug, and make peace, a really delicious peace. Coliseo Theatre (Buenos Aires), 23 June 1974

## Controlling one’s temper {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.1.10}

How can we stay cheerful? We have to pocket our temper and, for love for Christ, smile and make life pleasant for those around us. A moment ago I seemed to be saying just the contrary, because I asked someone: “Who is going to make me a saint? Prester John of the Indies, who never existed, or if he did exist, lived very far away? No! It is you who have to sanctify me, those I have around me.” But, husbands, don’t start mortifying your wives, nor wives your husbands, or I’ll kill you...

Therefore, put up with things yourself, rather than making your wife do so. As you are human, occasionally you will argue; but only a little. And afterwards both of you have to recognize that you are to blame, and say to one another: “Forgive me!” And then give each other a big hug... and on you go! But try not to get into an argument again for a long time. And never argue in front of the children, be they young or old. Even though they’re very young, children notice everything. I usually say that I never saw my parents squabble. They loved each other a great deal, so they must have quarrelled sometimes. With my sister Carmen, especially towards the end of her life, we used to reflect upon our childhood. We never ever saw them argue! How come? They often met with affliction in life, and faced setbacks… Didn’t they ever argue? They never did so in front of us, and that was not hypocrisy. It is worth arguing, so as to make peace afterwards. But let it be a sincere, affectionate peace. Love one another a lot. The love of Christian spouses – above all if they are children of God in Opus Dei – is like wine, which gets better as the years pass, and gains in value… Well, your love is much more important than the best wine in the world. It is a splendid treasure that our Lord has willed to bestow on you. Keep it safe. Don’t throw it away! Guard it! Sumaré (São Paulo), 4 June 1974

## The secret of happiness {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.1.11}

Sometimes we meet people whose marriages are breaking up. What can we do for them, Father? The first thing is to learn from others’ misfortune. The second is to help, with your prayer and your example, to get them back together again. If they are your friends, you will

need to tell them, confidentially, certain things that may seem unpleasant, but which are the greatest service you can render them. And pray, pray! As Christians, we have that great means, that “direct line” to God and to the Mother of God, which brings splendid results, because we are always heard in heaven. Zurbarán (Madrid), 27 October 1972

## Half angel, half woman {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.1.12}

It’s you women that are to blame. Happiness depends on you. You are half angels; but the other half, sometimes... I would like the other half to be woman. Angel and woman. As angels, you bring your husband to God, you make him more devout, a better Christian, and you sweeten his life. As women, you’re humanly smart and you don’t make his life a misery... Win him through his heart, and a little bit through his stomach… And then, don’t go on and on about things. If you see that he calls your mother “mother-in-law”, well, try ingeniously to get him to call her “Mum”. And that’s it, problem solved! See: you can achieve anything! With a couple of affectionate caresses you get what you want! (...) You women, since you know how to give way, to excuse and forgive, and not add fuel to the fire, you can get your way in everything. The majority of family problems are due to you. Either because you have, as the Italians say, a caratteraccio, a wicked temper. Or because you’re slovenly. My goodness, how can you expect your husband to behave if you don’t look after your appearance…? A little bit of know-how! Your husband loves to see you looking pretty, for his sake! And it’s your duty. You are his! That way he will keep himself strong and clean for your sake, because he is yours.

(…) My daughters, don’t blame anyone: not the times we live in, nor gossip, nor bad propaganda, nor the woman next door... You are the ones to blame, for neglecting yourselves. Be at the battlefront, at the battlefront of affection, of love, with all your weapons. You’ll tell me: “He’s mine now, I’ve won him, that’s it.” Not so! Now you have to hold on to him. And he has to hold on to you. And then comes a really, really good marriage, because you love one another a lot. So, regarding that half angel and half woman that you women are, I want to see it in action, with all the supernatural and human cleverness you can summon up, so that your home may be a happy home, an oasis of peace. Coliseo Theatre (Buenos Aires), 23 June 1974

## To keep up and increase one’s love {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.1.13}

What would you recommend the mother of such a large family to do if she wants to look after her children as well as possible, but without neglecting all the many signs of care and affection that are needed to keep the love in her marriage alive and growing? I’d recommend her to thank our Lord, who has shown her his confidence seven times over, and to be ready to receive a further seven joys, if God sends them. It is a blessing from God! How your soul expands, doesn’t it, my daughter? You don’t hold back; you love them all equally. And since you love them equally, you treat them unequally, because you will be inclined to give a little more love to the child that is weakest, that is in greatest need, that is going through a difficult time, or is suffering… Keep it up and, above all, do not neglect your affection for your husband. Look after your appearance, knowing that you do not belong to yourself. Sacred Scripture expresses it in rather crude terms. It states that those who marry are two in one flesh (cf. Mt 19:6). Therefore, you belong to your

husband and your husband to you. Try to stay young and attractive, because “a well-groomed woman keeps her husband away from other doors”.2 It is a matter of justice!

Yes, my daughter, good. And never complain about the children. Receive them for what they are, a proof of confidence from God, who sends you these little ones in order to make your house into a heaven. With their worries, with their little trifles and with their silly mistakes... They’re inevitable, because we are human beings. I always say the same thing. I repeat myself a lot, because truth has only one path. I usually explain to you women – your husbands aren’t listening – that they are the smallest children in the family. So treat your husband as a child: with affection, with care, with pampering and with firmness. Be strong, right? Miralba (Lima), 25 July 1974

## Divorced couples {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.1.14}

Father, today, unfortunately, there are many families who have experienced divorce. What should the attitude of a Catholic be towards these families and their children? In the first place, understanding, my children. We won’t get anywhere by ill-treating people. If they are souls that need help, good counsel, an affectionate word, we are not going to treat them badly. They are sick in spirit, as others are sick in mind or body. First attitude: Not to ill-treat them. Second. If they ask, “What do you think of my situation?”, give them a clear reply: “Well… lamentable! I am sorry to say so, but it is lamentable.” Why lie? But don’t give up hope, because with the grace of God things can be put right. As

feelings are at stake and children are involved, it is difficult. But those situations can often be resolved and, at the end of life, always. Never treat them badly. Is that clear? And as for their children, help them in the measure that you can. Help them not to be ashamed, although the poor things can’t be very happy. It is a tremendous shock, but that’s another reason for us to treat them well, affectionately, with a supernatural attitude, and showing them that we are Christians. So first be human; and then, Christians. First we are human beings and then, with Baptism, comes the grace of being children of God. In life, both those qualities need to be seen in the way we treat people: the human virtues and the supernatural virtues. Give them your affectionate kindliness, be polite and courteous, and afterwards give them the supernatural medicine of your good Christian advice and your good example. Sumaré (São Paulo), 5 June 1974

## The enemy of faithfulness in marriage {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.1.15}

We see daily, with deep sorrow, more and more married couples separating. This includes spouses who seem to be practising Catholics. How can we help people understand that true happiness is connected to faithfulness and to valuing the indissolubility of marriage? I know a set of people who are trying to have a deep relationship with God, and after a certain number of years, they call it Fidelity. And I tell them that they shouldn’t just call it fidelity but felicity, happiness. We all agree on that! It’s the same in marriage. Just now I was blaming the women… and they are so good that they didn’t protest. The truth is that it is not only your fault. Men are also to blame. They can’t protest either. Men and women alike, you seem determined to make yourselves miserable and make a

mountain, a whole mountain range, out of a molehill. Why? Why should a couple begin to argue and quarrel?… There is never a good reason for husband and wife to get into a fight. The enemy of conjugal happiness is pride. Make a firm resolution not to argue in front of the children. Before that, you should make a resolution not to block up the sources of life; let the Lord send you the number of children he chooses, and receive them with love: each of them is a blessing from God. In my country, which is almost yours, they say that each child brings a loaf of bread under its arm; so don’t worry… Don’t argue in front of the children, because the children will side either with Dad or with Mum, and suffer; and so they begin to go wrong. They begin to be suspicious, not only of love, but also of the home; they are already preparing for a bad marriage, for a bad choice of their state in life. You will tell me, “But surely we can argue occasionally?” Yes, you can, and sometimes… it can even be a good thing. It is a sign of love… But only a little…! And just the two of you! Do you want to make a spectacle of yourselves by arguing in front of the children, or friends, or neighbours, or relatives, as in a public square? By coming to blows? No! Say something quietly to the other one, and wait till night-time, – and keep calm! And at night-time… see which of the two of you is bold enough to tell the other they were right. The one who thinks he or she was right, can be certain that they were wrong, because neither of the two is ever right. Say sorry to each other. Give each other a good hug, remembering the first time you embraced… and love one another, for the Lord is pleased with your affection. You will see that there’s nothing to worry about. The wife should look after herself; I have already said it; and the husband, likewise, mustn’t neglect himself. As for him, he mustn’t behave foolishly, acting the farmyard cockerel; as

for her, she mustn’t be a simpleton, but learn to keep hold of what is hers. Is this clear? And if someone hasn’t understood me, it’s because they don’t need to; and if they have understood, it’s because it will do them good. Anhembi Park (São Paulo), 1 June 1974

## As when you were engaged {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.1.16}

My children, you say that you love one another, and it’s true. You told each other so countless times when you were engaged… It’s the Church’s wish that, between one man and one woman, there should first be a period of engagement, so that they can get to know each other better, and so love each other more, and come to the Sacrament of Marriage better prepared. But marriage isn’t just satisfaction for the heart, life and senses. It’s also suffering; it has two sides, back and front, like a medal. Love, my children, is sacrifice. So a married man has to love his wife, and to show her that he does. Don’t be miserly. Throughout your whole lives you have to be a bit like an engaged couple; otherwise things are not going well. Coming home tired, with a long face, is not the way. Your wife needs you to give her a kiss when you get home; not putting it on, but naturally, with affection. Wives shouldn’t take their husbands for granted: you already know that. There’s a Spanish proverb that puts it very clearly: “A well-groomed woman keeps her husband away from other doors.” So make yourselves pretty. Take care of your appearance. Be keen to look good, as you did when you were younger, because you are always young. I give you this advice: when you turn twenty-five, go back to twenty-four, then twenty-three, until you get to twenty; then count back up to twenty-five again. You’ll see how well it works.

Put yourselves out a little. Don’t speak about the disagreeable things of the day, when your husband gets back from his occupations, his work, his business. Sometimes you come out with so many foolish things that you drive him crazy, and obviously the poor fellow won’t want to hurry home, no matter how good he is. He needs to find the house refreshingly cool if he’s hot; warm and welcoming if he’s cold; with an affectionate smile and some extra little touch from you if he’s sad. If one day he comes home very tired and perhaps a bit down because things haven’t gone as he’d hoped, then, since you’ve already caught him by his heart, and caught him well, catch him also by his stomach. Prepare a nice little dinner, the dish he likes best, realizing that he’s exhausted. He has worn himself out working for love of you and your children, for the good of your home, out of love for God. I tell you all these things because, in the final analysis, they are the demands of Christian morals. To fulfil this advice, you’ll have to put yourselves out, and that is a sign of true love. I’ve said so many times that the hardest mortification can often be to smile. Well, then, smile! Club Xénon (Lisbon), 3 November 1972

## The conquest of each day {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.1.17}

It’s not wrong to show your affection for one another, in front of the children: it would be bad if you didn’t show it. Don’t make extraordinary displays of affection in front of the children, out of modesty; but love each other a lot, for our Lord is very pleased when you love one another. And with the passing of the years – at present you are all very young – don’t be afraid: your love won’t diminish; it will get better. It will become even more enthusiastic; it will become once again the love you had when you were engaged. Sumaré (São Paulo), 4 June 1974

## Work in the home {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.1.18}

Father I want to tell you I have twelve children and… How many? Twelve. How wonderful! You see? Another proof that motherhood makes women beautiful... ... and therefore I have some work on my hands... Thank God for it! Organize yourself. Get hold of your older girls, and get them to help you with the little ones... In the Work we learn that work is a means of sanctification. I would like to ask you how to sanctify the work the children make for me? The work the children make for you – doesn’t it seem sanctifiable to you? How much patience you need, how much marvellous heroism! All that you do, do it for them. They deserve it. That is already something holy. But if, as well as doing it for them, you think of the Blessed Virgin, and how she would do for the Child Jesus the same tasks as you are doing… Take a good look at the way you are sanctifying your married life and your relationship with your children; I repeat, you can make them divine: it is no longer just a human task. I see that very clearly. Centro de Estudos (São Paulo), 4 June 1974

## A marvellous profession {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.1.19}

Why do you say that the work a housewife does really is a profession? I'd like you to explain this a bit. Of course it is, my daughter! A very specialized profession! Because you have to know something of dietetics, to give your family suitable nourishment, so they can grow up strong, without getting… fat and chubby. You need art, so that with a few items of clothing you make yourselves look

elegant. And there’s your feminine ability to make some little corner of your house attractive, so that when your husband gets home tired from work, he can recover his strength. So, science, art and elegance. Besides, nowadays you do everything by pressing buttons. You have machines for everything, and almost anyone can afford them. You even know about engineering. Do you really think it’s not much of a profession? Why, you’re walking encyclopaedias! And then there’s psychology as well. Yes, you are real “psychologists”! Coliseo Theatre (Buenos Aires), 23 June 1974

## The value of work in the home {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.1.20}

Father, the work of a woman in the home generally goes unnoticed. How can we explain to our friends the value of this work? I always say that housework is professional work which is becoming more scientific every day. A woman who looks after her home properly is a scientist and an artist; and also a “psychologist”, because when her husband gets home tired, she has prepared a comfortable corner for him, with his slippers. And a nice little meal with a dish that he likes, and an affectionate look; and she does not weigh him down with problems, about how this has happened, and then that… She doesn’t tell him those things, doesn’t mortify him; she leaves disagreeable things for another moment. You women know a lot. You are what matters the most. In Opus Dei, for example, all the apostolates would collapse if my daughters did not look after the Administration in this scientific way, with their supernatural focus on things, their cheerfulness, their commitment and artistry, knowing they are serving God, and that God looks upon them with delight, loves them dearly. Therefore I am very sad if in some homes people have to eat by “stabbing”: stabbing at cans with a tin opener. Centro de Estudos (São Paulo), 25 May 1974

## Work outside the home {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.1.21}

What do you have to say about married women who, besides their family and home life, have another professional life outside their home? Many married women need to have a professional life. But that professional job should not take them away from their love and dedication to their home: bringing the grace of a smile and the grace of God as they do, taking loving care of their husband, and raising their children to be healthy, right minded, aiming heavenwards... My daughter, you need a double portion of grace from God: for your life as a mother and wife, and for your professional life. Club Xénon (Lisbon), 5 November 1972

Each day, more and more of us mothers are combining our profession as homemakers with another profession. How can we strike a proper balance in this situation? If you’re concerned about it, I’m sure you’ve found the right balance. First of all, think of your husband and children, and how to make your home pleasant and well-looked after, and then consider your professional work, which is also an obligation, and that way you will sanctify yourself in both jobs. Apparently, more and more men have two, three or four jobs. They learn how to handle this. You, as a Christian mother with so many wonderful insights given you by God’s grace and your own talent, will be able to solve the problem very well. Brafa (Barcelona), 22 November 1972

## Two compatible occupations {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.1.22}

I have eight children between the ages of three and sixteen. As you can imagine I also have to work outside to help my husband support them.

But others couldn’t imagine it, if they saw you looking so pretty, when you have eight children. Thank you Father. It’s always the case, always… Go on with your question. When people hear that I work outside they think that I do so to escape housework. How can I explain to them that housework is a lovely thing, and so is having a large family? And that the two tasks are compatible. You make them compatible. Today, in life, nearly everybody multi-tasks. I multi-task and do not earn a penny, not a cent (…). And I tell you that you are right, that the two are perfectly compatible. Furthermore, one is a help for the other. For while you are working at one job, you are resting from the other. This is the spirit of Opus Dei. In 1946, when I arrived in Rome, I became friends with Cardinal Pizzardo – may he rest in peace. He was a welcoming man, and very affectionate… He would say to me: “I have learnt this from Opus Dei, that to rest, one only has to change what one is doing.” You’re doing just that. So tell people that this is what you are doing, and that it does you a lot of good; that I am doing it and it does me a lot of good; and that I recommend it. Tabancura (Santiago de Chile), 7 July 1974

## A love that is always young {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.1.23}

Father, I’ve been married for twenty-three years and I have five children... How can I maintain and increase the enthusiasm in my marriage? You will be a perennial, constant lover. Each day you should aim to win over your husband, and he should do the same for you (...)

My daughter, you will achieve this if you look at your husband as what he is: a large part of your heart, all your heart! If you know that he is yours and you are his. If you remember that you have a duty to make him happy, to share in his joys and in his sorrows, in his sickness and in his health… You know more than anyone in the world, because love is most wise. When your husband comes home from work, from his professional occupation, don’t let him find you annoyed. Tidy yourself up, make yourself pretty, and when the years go by, do up the “façade” a little bit more, as we do with houses. He will be so grateful to you, so very grateful. When difficulties have come up at work, he has often thought about God and about you. And he has said to himself: “I am going back home. It’ll be great. There I will find a haven of peace, of joy, of affection and of beauty,” because in his eyes there is no-one in the world prettier than you. But let it be true, eh? Don’t mortify him. Be clever. And… one more thing, for you alone. No one else will hear. It’s true I have got this microphone on. They have hung this on me and keep pulling on the line, but they won’t catch on, nor will your husband, if he’s here. You have won him through his heart, and you have him well caught. You will make him fall a little more in love each day, and he you; and you will also win him over a bit by his stomach. Don’t neglect the cooking, you mothers! You need to keep the house nice, but also the cooking… nice meals! It’s not a matter of making him fat. Not at all! Because you don’t want him to grow fat. On the contrary, you look after him with such affection, with that charm of yours. The day he comes in tired – and you know it, you foresee it – you remember that dish he likes, and think: “I’ll make it for him!” And you don’t tell him in advance. You make it a surprise.

And he looks at you with such a loving look ... And that’s it! That’s it! It’s your fault, when things go wrong. They are little boys! I say to all of you: the youngest child you have is your husband… But, of course, you treat him as a grown man…! Very wrong! Treat him affectionately. Understand him, find excuses for him, forgive him: spoil him! And you in turn will be spoilt, forgiven and understood. Don’t invent tragedies. It’s your fault, when things go wrong! The men won’t hear this either, and you women told me the other day that you wouldn’t take offence if I told you the truth. When things go wrong in the home, it’s Mummy’s fault because she’s just being… silly! Anhembi Park (São Paulo), 1 June 1974

## A matter of justice {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.1.24}

Father, is looking after one’s appearance important in a woman? Certainly! It’s a matter… of justice. Look, my daughters, you cannot let yourselves go. Love your husbands a lot, but don’t take them for granted. Keep looking pretty, well dressed, and attractive. Is that clear? Make yourselves elegant. Sometimes, when your husband comes home, you drive him crazy with stupidities, talking about how this has gone wrong, and that has gone wrong… Look at him affectionately, give him a smile which, sometimes, can be a mortification for you, because you are tired and it costs you a lot of effort. But do it out of love for him, and also out of Love for God. And your husband should keep young and strong. He has a duty to do so, because he is yours, as you are his. My children, this is elementary common sense! It is a matter of justice: of justice pure and simple. Otherwise countless dangers

come up for both of you, which you have a duty to avoid. Therefore don’t go around badly dressed, or neglect your looks. What’s more, when the years go by, it’s to be expected that the “façades” which are no longer so new, need to be done up a little… You’re not offending God in doing that, on the contrary: among the household expenses, this one, looking after your appearance, is a very important one for peace in the home. But, Father, that’s not the main way to please our husbands.... I know, my daughter. The main thing is your charming behaviour; the way so many mothers of families behave, sacrificing themselves cheerfully, without going on about it; spending themselves without being noticed – like sanctuary lamps before the tabernacle – out of gratitude to the Lord and for love of their home, their husband and their children. On the other hand, those who make sacrifices but go around telling everyone about it, just put people off. “I don’t go to the theatre... I didn’t wear this dress or the other one... I didn’t buy that piece of jewellery... I didn’t....” No, my daughters! People like that don’t please God, nor me either; they don’t make their husband love them more, nor increase peace in their home. Club Xénon (Lisbon), 4 November 1972

## My husband doesn’t practise… {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.1.25}

My husband doesn’t practise. He is baptized but does not want anything to do with the Church. And I am not that fantastic either… Sit down, sit down, I can see what you’re getting at. First, love your husband a lot. You have a duty to love him a lot; not only being faithful, but very affectionate. Then, don’t pester him. Is that clear? You women are sometimes far too insistent… Your very affection blinds you. He is good, he’s been baptized; therefore he has the faith. And on top of that

faith – most likely through no fault of his, or very little fault, but a little neglect – life has thrown a lot of things on top of him, and now the faith can’t be seen or felt… But if you, with a clever hand, begin to do a bit of cleaning… That soul is closer to you than others. Pray, invoke St Joseph, who was the head of a family, so that the father of your family, the father of your children, may become – in time and very soon – very attached to St Joseph and may behave very well. Do you know how you will achieve this? The same way as I told that other daughter of mine: striving to get him to go to confession one fine day… Put your whole heart into it, offering to our Lord the small mortifications that crop up in life, each day. Don’t put on a glum face. Don’t go on at your husband… And you will see that between us all we will succeed. Tomorrow at Mass I will dedicate a memento for this intention. But never lose your temper. Are we agreed on this?... I am sure I would be very good friends with your husband if I knew him and talked with him. I’m sure he must have many virtues, many human virtues, and then… there will be something that scandalized the poor man; and something that needs to be pruned or scraped clean. Some small thing! Let us pray for him to the Lord that he may go and let himself to be washed clean a little, like your children when you wash them clean, and you do it with so much love. The priest, in the holy Sacrament of Penance, as he is another Christ, also cleans with great care, he lances the abscesses, he treats them so that they can heal… Your husband will be healed; and you and he will be very happy, my daughter. God bless you! Tabancura (Santiago de Chile), 7 July 1974

## With holy astuteness {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.1.26}

Sometimes we find great difficulties. For example, in doing apostolate with a married couple, our first contact is with the husband, and often he receives everything favourably; but then we encounter obstacles on the part of his wife… Yes, my son. This won’t happen to you; but if a husband does not have a holy cleverness, not only is his wife not happy that he’s leading a clean life, but she becomes envious of his soul. It’s very odd! It also happens the other way round; if we don’t say this, you men would always be blaming the women. No, sir! Sometimes husbands envy the good things their wives have, and become jealous of the Work, of their wife’s vocation. But this is due to a lack of astuteness and cleverness. Let us ask our Lord to give us that astuteness. Ask our Lord to give you the gift of tongues, that is, the ability to pass over the difficulties that you encounter in people, so that you can put into their hearts all the good doctrine you have. And then neither wife nor husband will be able to resist. In any case, you can be well content and grateful to God our Lord, because over and above the grace of the Sacrament of Marriage, you have another divine gift: your vocation to seek holiness in the married state. Vocation does not deprive you of anything: it makes everything greater; it divinizes everything. Sumaré (São Paulo), 4 June 1974

## The first weapon {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.1.27}

Father, how can one’s children be brought up to love God when one’s husband doesn’t have faith? Your husband has faith, I’m sure. Therefore, the first thing to do is to revive your husband’s faith. We always come

back to the same: for us, Christians who aren’t ashamed of being Christian, our first weapon is prayer. Pray very much for your husband. Pray, but love him when you are praying. Then, don’t be overly pious. “If by going to church to pray, a woman burns her stew, she may be half an angel, but she’s half a devil, too.” To me she seems a devil all the way through. Look lovingly on your husband, love him very much. Ask God to help you. Then, little by little, with patience, leave some book around, without telling him to read it... In fact, it’s even better to tell him: “Don’t read this book; it’s for me.” You already know how to do this so well! All of you are marvellous “psychologists”, and besides, you have the strength of God’s grace. What can sometimes hamper things is your behaviour, when you become tedious. Don’t go on at your husbands! Club Xénon (Lisbon), 3 November 1972

## A bright and cheerful home {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.1.28}

Why do you say that our homes have to be bright and cheerful? The fact is that you women are brightness and cheerfulness. I have been complaining that sometimes you aren’t, because although you are very clever, you start acting stupid, you get annoyed over the slightest thing, neglect your way of dressing and appearance; and then, where has the brightness gone, if you look a sight? Of course I’m joking with you, but you’ll realize that I’m right. You see, love isn’t something flitting in the air … (He made a flitting gesture with his hand) … love is something tangible: it is soul, spirit, conversation, character, intelligence... And the body too, my daughters. So you have to look after your body. Knowing,

besides, that if you don’t do so, you are offending your husband; and he you. So, that’s the way to be bright and cheerful, and you will find in your homes all the light and happiness you can want. And your children will grow up paternalistic; and they will think, as I do, “Why shouldn’t I be proud of my father and mother? Of Dad and Mum?” Coliseo Theatre (Buenos Aires), 23 June 1974

## The best advice {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.1.29}

My children, why should I speak to you about a bright and cheerful home when all you need to do is consider your own? You cease to be cheerful when you cease to be Christians; when you cease to practise; when you cease to pray as a family; when you cease to receive the Sacraments frequently… And one or other of you will be complaining inside: “Here he goes again.” Well yes, here I go again, because I love you. Just the same as you tell your children that they should eat when one day they don’t want to, and the next day you say it again... this is good for you too. Mothers are endlessly wise and inventive. They explain that this has iron in it, and that other thing has something else, I don’t know what… and the children take it so as to become strong as iron and steel, with marvellous muscles. I have come to remind you, without inventing anything – because our very God has invented it: it’s a divine invention – that the Sacrament of Penance makes us strong; that the Sacrament of the Eucharist, which is all love, gives us life. Don’t abandon them; receive them frequently, assiduously. Seek God in all things: he is hidden in the most elementary things, in the ordinary things, in daily life. Sometimes we obstinately, and foolishly, think we will find God in rare events, which won’t ever happen, when he is present in our own lives all the time: in the life of each one of

us, in mine, now! Here! Here especially, because he has assured us that where two or three are gathered in his name, he is there in the midst of them. And there are more than two or three of us here, in spite of the marvellous unity which makes it seem like only two or three. I have seen crowds of five, six, or seven thousand people, and it was just the same every time. Sometimes I feel embarrassed, and the thought comes: is it not tempting God, now that when five or six Christians come together they argue and fight? The children of this large family, on the other hand, love each other; and when they bring others that do not belong to the family, this divine madness immediately catches hold of them: a madness of peace, understanding, harmony, love, forgiveness, and loving one another… In Opus Dei – the Holy See recognized this in a solemn document many years ago – we are sowers of peace and joy. That is why your homes are and should be bright and cheerful. If by chance we find an exception, it is because you are not behaving as you should. Therefore examine your conscience, put things right, and get going again! Tabancura (Santiago de Chile), 7 July 1974

# God’s blessing {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.2}

I am very much in favour of large families, but I am also a friend to those families who have fewer children, or none at all, because God doesn’t give them any. I’m not a friend to families who, through selfishness, cut love’s wings and render love barren and unfruitful…! I often say that the last child who comes into the world is the one that gives the most joy. In big, traditional families it is normal for there to be a considerable age difference between the eldest and youngest child. I’m nearly twenty years older than my brother. Mothers, take courage! Your hearts will be filled with so much joy! Besides, there’s another thing I tell mothers: it makes me very happy to say that motherhood beautifies you. There are women who selfishly think… how can I put it?... that they will lose their good looks. Not so. Those of you who have had that gift of maternity many times are much more beautiful. Don’t be afraid! (…) Accept children lovingly; they are always a blessing from God. Anhembi Park (São Paulo), 1 June 1974

## Don’t block up the sources of life {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.2.1}

Father, people around here are marrying much younger, often with the explicit intention of postponing the arrival of children, because they want to complete their studies, or they say they could not support a family, or all sorts of other excuses. Father, what can we do in these situations? You can speak up clearly, and if they get angry, that’s their affair. If you were to see them close to a precipice, ready to jump over the edge, you would warn them even if they got annoyed. Tell them that they can’t behave that way, that it’s a vile, disgraceful thing; it means turning the marriage bed – which for me is an altar – into a lecher’s couch. That it’s very sad! That it’s criminal! And I’d rather not continue… Altoclaro (Caracas), 11 February 1975

## Large families {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.2.2}

Nowadays, when everything is planned down to the last detail, parents who have or want large families are regarded as irresponsible. What would you say to that? Sometimes it’s out of jealousy, since you mothers of large families are wonderful, beautiful: one only has to look at you… I’ve said it everywhere, for I do trot around the world a bit, from time to time. I’m not ashamed to pay you compliments: motherhood makes you beautiful, you are wonderful, and they envy you. Is that clear? And each child that comes along is a blessing from God. It means God is showing how much he trusts you. And each child brings a loaf of bread under its arm, don’t you worry. They will all make their way in the world, wonderfully well! You will feel a holy pride for having been God’s instrument in a task that is so full of love, for you do raise them well, in a Christian manner, don’t you?

Tell those people not to be cowards. It’s selfishness! A bad sort of selfishness. And in order to live that sort of selfish life, they commit real crimes… Tabancura (Santiago de Chile), 2 July 1974

## False fears {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.2.3}

Father, why is there this fear of having a large family? Afraid to have children? No! You should have a lot of love of God, and be very grateful when he sends you those children. Every time a child comes to your family, you have a sign of the Lord’s trust in you. Be happy. In my country they say every child brings a loaf of bread under his arm; and, besides, the last one is the one that will give you the most joy. Beware of the infamous propaganda going around now! Don’t take any notice of any of it. Blocking up the sources of life is a terrible sin: it’s like propagating murder. Even worse! My children, those of you who are united in the Sacrament of Marriage: love each other truly, with everything that flows from that. Be at peace, be calm, ready for all manner of sacrifices, which in any case will turn into joys, for you will not be lacking the Lord’s help to bring up your family as you should. Pozoalbero (Jerez de la Frontera), 12 November 1972

## When it’s time to be rebellious {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.2.4}

Father, I’ve got ten children. When I tell people this, some look horrified, like I’m really weird. What do you think? That God has shown great trust in you, ten times over. Tell this to your wife, on my behalf. That I bless you ten times over, with both my hands, as a priest, because you have not placed obstacles in life’s path, because you have received that life, which is a most wonderful gift, as something coming from God.

Each child brings a loaf of bread under its arm. It won’t go short; don’t worry about the money side: you’ll manage all right. Follow the advice of a doctor, a Christian doctor, for you need to keep up your strength so you can work; and so you can smile, with a little bit of pity, at those who have other views. This may not be the last one, Father. As many as God wants. Well done! Don’t tolerate that infamous, anti-Christian propaganda. They want to treat you like animals! That’s why I tell you to be rebellious, to be rebels. I am a rebel: I don’t want to live as an animal, but as a son of God. And the same goes for you. Tajamar (Madrid), 28 October 1972

## Two ways of blessing human love {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.2.5}

Father, you have always praised large families, and that makes sense to me, since the purpose of marriage is to give oneself and to love. But it does happen that there are married couples who only have very few children or none at all, because God does not give them more. What would you advise such married couples, regarding their longing to love, to give themselves? Look, my daughter, God can bless married couples with many children, with few, or with none. What you can never do is to block up the sources of life. Does God give you many children? Welcome them all. They are a treasure, and the last one is the one that will give you the most joy. Does God give you few children? If it’s just one, you will have to be very careful, because sometimes you pamper an only child too much. Bell-lloc del Plà (Gerona), 24 November 1972

Father, what can we say to married couples who have had no children? It does happen sometimes that our Lord, for whatever reason, to very good married couples, who fulfil their marital duties, who love one another a lot and who should continue loving each other always, does not give children. When this happens, my advice to them is to pray to God humbly for children and to consult a doctor who is a Christian. Not only the wife, but the husband as well, because men always say that the problem isn’t with them… Both should go to the doctor. Club Xénon (Lisbon), 5 November 1972

And what about those of us who have no children, in spite of everything? And what about others, what are they for you? They are your brothers and, as for me, you call me Father, and I have so many children… I’m giving you more and more nephews and nieces! Wonderful nephews and nieces, who need the encouragement of your virtues. They need that smile of yours…! If the Lord doesn’t give you any children, I’ll give you plenty to do, don’t worry. I usually say something, which you’ve probably heard, which is that the Lord – in his Providence – has two ways of blessing married couples: one, by giving them children; and another, at times, because he loves them very much, by not granting them children. I don’t know which of the two is the better blessing; in each case, what is best is what God has given them. I tell those married couples who do not have children to love each other very, very, very much. Human love, within marriage, pleases the Lord greatly. Love one another with all your heart, in keeping with the natural law and with God’s law. Brafa (Barcelona), 22 November 1972

## The role of doctors {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.2.6}

Father, I’m a gynaecologist. What can I tell my students, and the families who ask me about birth control? The answer is No! Is that clear? No! Moreover, tell them not to be afraid of children. I found it very comforting to hear some words from a good Cardinal, who used to work in the Holy Office. There was talk of blocking up the sources of life, of accepting criminal ideas, and this elderly Cardinal – elderly, but a real warrior – stood up and spoke his mind: “I am the twelfth child of a worker from the Trastevere: if my parents had thought like that, the Church would have one Cardinal fewer.” He’s a man of formidable intelligence, who is doing a lot of good for mankind and works wonderfully for the Church. So if his parents had put those theories into practice, mankind and the Church would have lost an outstanding servant. My answer is No. Let people receive children for what they are: God’s blessings, not allowing their love to wither away (…). Those of you who are married, love each other: that way you are pleasing God. Don’t be afraid of anything! And you doctors: give good advice, so that children are born well and grow up healthy! Belagua (Pamplona), 8 October 1972

Father, I am a paediatrician. For some time now, we doctors have been under pressure from various quarters to apply birth control. What do you advise me to tell my patients, colleagues and students regarding this point? You know, as well as I do, that you must say No. You can shout it out loud or say it quietly, but always say no! And to those who advise that, whisper in their ear – so they don’t get

annoyed – that it would have been a pity if their mother had practised birth control. Keep calm! You are doing very well. You, being a man who brings health, must not turn into an assassin, a butcher; and, moreover, a butcher and assassin of souls, not only of bodies. Altoclaro (Caracas), 9 February 1975

I teach medicine, and I’m worried about the spread of birth control programmes amongst my students. I am quite sure that you know all about this subject, both as a Christian and as a doctor. As a doctor you know it much better than I. You’ll also realize that there are many young people present here for whom this topic should not hold much direct interest. I will confine myself to saying that no contraceptive method can be used, as a general rule. In some particular case, with the advice of a Catholic doctor and a priest … a Catholic priest, there is a solution about which I do not wish to speak… You can speak; you do very well to speak about it. Keep it up: have this concern and do what you can, my son. But listen: you should speak clearly and openly with everyone. They mustn’t block up the sources of life. And if not, there is a wonderful system which is abstinence. It is painful, since love can be lost and it’s a shame for a home to lose the warmth of love. It is better for them to put themselves in God’s hands, not to fear those blessings, which are a way of participating in God’s creative power, and to receive children for what they are: gifts from God most high. Tabancura (Santiago de Chile), 7 July 1974

## A diabolical murder {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.2.7}

My children, I want you to be happy. And you will be happy if in your conjugal relations you are not blocking up life’s sources, in accordance with God’s will. I was speaking the other day – and it seems to me I have repeated it two or three times – about a Chinese son of mine who is a medical doctor. His family had to leave their country. Perhaps he will return if things improve a bit: let’s pray. He was working as a doctor in a country in Latin America, and has been with me for two years in Rome. One day he told me: “Father, I’m going to tell you about something that happened to me.” It’s something truly original. A native Indian woman came to him, pressured by the diabolical propaganda that a certain State is waging in Latin American countries, saying that she wanted to have an abortion. This doctor told her very clearly: “You can’t do that: it’s a criminal act, it’s murder!” I totally agree that it’s murder, a diabolical murder, because the poor baby cannot even defend itself… Besides, for me, as one who has the fullness of the faith, it means depriving it of heaven. Without receiving Baptism, where will that poor child end up? Draw all the consequences you want, for you are on sure ground. When the doctor saw he wasn’t get anywhere with this woman, and that she would simply go to another doctor, he had a truly supernatural idea: something like the judgment of Solomon. He asked her: “How many children do you have?” The woman answered that she had six or seven. “And the eldest, how old is he?”

“Nine years old.” “Why don’t you want the one you are expecting?” “Because I can’t feed them all.” Then this son of mine told her: “Well look, as for this one who is going to be born, I won’t do it; but bring me the nine-year-old, and I’ll kill him for you.” The woman raised her hands to her head: “No! No!” The doctor made her reason it out: “But he is the one who eats the most! Whereas the one you’re expecting will hardly cost you anything, because you will be breast-feeding him …” You shouldn’t have to use such arguments with a Christian woman; God’s law should be enough. That is shameful, my daughters! May your friends learn from you, from your good example. Guadalaviar (Valencia), 19 November 1972

## Always a blessing from God {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.2.8}

Father! I have a child who is mentally handicapped: more specifically, he has cerebral palsy. This is why… How old is he? Seven. This is why I am often in contact with parents whose children have similar problems. I’ve realized that we all go through crises of courage, to a greater or lesser degree, and also great anxiety. Father, I’d like you to speak a few words to us so we can carry all this cheerfully. And Father, one more little thing: how can we help the families of these children and above all the teachers, who sometimes have ideas that are somewhat different

from the Catholic religion? You’ve touched on a very interesting topic. In the first place, I want to tell you that God our Lord sends a child with disabilities like that, to families whom he loves a lot; it is not a misfortune. Love that child a lot! Love him! I know you already do. Love him even more! Consider that this is a gift that God has given you, a great gift, a proof of his trust that the Lord has given you, by sending you this child. And then, you know there are associations everywhere to help you care for such children. Do you have such associations here? Yes, Father. Then on you go. Get in touch with associations in other countries, and you yourself try to give a Christian meaning to the lives of these boys and girls, and a Christian understanding to their parents, who don’t always receive that gift from God as what it is: as a blessing! My daughter, there’s something more I have to tell you: congratulations! Yes: congratulations! Tomorrow, in the Holy Mass, I shall pray for all these children, and especially for yours, because your motherly eyes are asking me to do so. Thank you, Father. When you get home, give your son a kiss, and a caress on my behalf. If he is aware of it, so much the better. He is aware, Father. Well, give him a caress, and get him to pray with you and for my intention: “Hail Mary most pure, conceived without sin.” In heaven there will be a great celebration, there will be little silver bells ringing, and gold ones too. Altoclaro (Caracas), 11 February 1975

## Parents’ sorrow {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.2.9}

Father, sometimes the Lord sends parents crosses that are very hard to bear. How can I console them? First, tell them that calling those things crosses is an offence against the Holy Cross. I don’t like you to call things that cause pain “crosses”, because the Cross is the throne where Jesus Christ the Priest triumphed. Those furious, twisted figures of Christ really upset me. The Lord stretched out his arms in the gesture of a priest and, even more than by the nails, he allowed himself to be nailed to the wood of the Cross by Love. He loved us so much, so very much, that he willed to suffer a lot; and since he had all the power to do so, he suffered all that can be suffered, for each one of us. So he does not want those parents to be unhappy. I can understand it. Do you think I don’t cry when those I love die? There are so many I love… Even now, during my stay here, I am beside myself with worry, because I have two children with terminal illnesses. So I do understand those parents. But they shouldn’t call it a cross. Tell them that they will find their child just around the corner, in no time. Life here below is but a bad night in a bad inn! Life is slipping through our fingers. Sometimes it hurts to think like this, but it’s the truth. Tell them they will soon meet the child who has died. Meanwhile, they can invoke him in prayer, to help them in all their needs; but they should stop crying. The first tears after the death of a loved one are a gift from God. We would not be made of flesh and blood if we did not cry. But afterwards we should cease crying; otherwise it goes too far. Viaró (Barcelona), 21 November 1972

# Raising children {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.3}

From the very start, children are relentless witnesses of their parents’ lives. You don’t realize it, but they judge everything, and sometimes they judge you in a bad light. So what happens at home influences your children for good or for evil. Try to help them with your good example, try not to hide your piety, try to behave uprightly: then they will learn, and they will be the crown of your mature years and your old age. You are like an open book to them. Therefore, you need to have an interior life, to strive to be good Christians. If not, what you are trying to do for your children or your friends’ children will be useless. Pozoalbero (Jerez de la Frontera), 12 November 1972

## A responsibility of both father and mother {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.3.1}

Father, I’ve got seven children… Seven children! God has shown his trust in you seven times over!

Father, I am praying for their vocations and I try to raise them up in the spirit of the Work. They have a fair amount of trust in me, but I can see that they are also being influenced by other ideas they see around them, which go against the truth. What means should I use to support and strengthen their formation? First, try to get your husband’s cooperation. You have cooperated to bring them into the world, with that holy love which I bless with all my affection, because the Lord himself has blessed it. Continue loving each other a lot. But your husband mustn’t be passive, leaving the children’s upbringing to you. This is a task that the two of you should do together… Is that clear? Yes, Father. … You should do it together, in agreement with one another, and sometimes disagreeing a bit. But you “call the shots” yourself, OK? Even if I didn’t tell you to, you’d do it anyway… I suppose so, Father. Don’t tell your husband. He already knows well enough that you run the show, but he doesn’t like others to notice it. Thank you, Father. Yes, quite a few husbands are laughing here, and you know what? The same thing happens to them. And then, you’ve set up those wonderful clubs, where you can take your children. You have schools in which you must have a say, because in schools the parents come first, and then the teachers: you need teachers who are trustworthy. You haven’t brought animals into the world, but human beings with bodies and souls. And if you have given them a good-looking body – for you, your children are the best-looking ones in the world, that stands to reason; besides, it’s true, and may the

Be careful with the things they read, even though they carry some ecclesiastical approval! (…) Before buying a book, make enquiries in each case, so that you don’t go home with a load of poison, of filth. Miralba (Lima), 14 July 1974

## Freedom and authority {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.3.2}

How can we combine freedom with the authority needed for there to be a certain level of order in the home? Love your children’s freedom and teach them to use it well. Help them realize that freedom is open to a serious illness: that of not wanting to accept the responsibility that goes with it. Then it’s no longer freedom, but licence. Our parents watched over our freedom with a lot of love. They did not want their children to become victims of bad habits, crazy ideas, disgraceful behaviour, bad friends, or brutal propaganda that would end up turning them into beasts, instead of living as children of God. You parents nowadays – like those who went before you – have a commitment to defend your children’s freedom, as you have put it very well, trying to ensure it does not become licence. Freedom should be accompanied by responsibility. So, in raising your children, you need to ensure that freedom and authority go hand in hand. Husbands too, because they sometimes shirk this duty and leave their children entirely in the mothers’ hands, saying they have too much else to do, as if women weren’t kept busy, very busy, running the home. I always say that the most excellent profession and the one most to be admired is that of mothers of families.

Try to get the children to learn to ask themselves what their actions mean in God’s eyes. Give them supernatural reasons to ponder on, so that they feel responsible. And don’t show mistrust. It’s better to let them deceive you on occasion than to destroy the affection and union they have with you. You need to nurture your children’s freedom according to how old they are. You can’t treat them all the same. Justice demands that you treat unequal children unequally, but in a way that doesn’t provoke jealousy. They are unequal in age, temperament, health, intellectual abilities… Like that, with your help, they will become equal, loving each other a lot, behaving well, having their parents’ virtues, and being good children of Holy Mary. Guadalaviar (Valencia), 17 November 1972

## Freedom and obedience {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.3.3}

Could you talk to us about how to harmonize freedom with obedience? A reasonable person knows that freedom demands personal responsibility. A person who does not want to take responsibility for his or her actions, doesn’t deserve to be free. Do you follow the argument? So don’t forget that freedom is a great gift from God, a gift our Lord won for us on the Cross. Qua libertate, says Saint Paul, Christus nos liberavit (Gal 5:1). He, Jesus Christ, gave it to us. Besides, as soon as even half a dozen people live together, if there is not a basic minimum of rules and timetable, anarchy sets in: there’s no way of working, of improving oneself, of doing anything positive… Moreover, on this earth there’s no one so free that they are not conditioned in some way. Women are conditioned by their love for their husbands, and husbands by their love for their wives; people in the army are

conditioned by the rules of the military… There’s nobody in the world whose freedom is not “conditioned”! If anyone thinks he can live without his freedom being conditioned, he will have to go off to some desert and live on his own… and there too he will find himself conditioned by the environment, and by his passions. God alone is truly free. Bell-lloc del Plà (Gerona), 24 November 1972

## Help your children to feel trusted {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.3.4}

Father, you have often spoken about freedom, and that’s how we want to raise our children. But how can we unite it with authority? By teaching them responsibility along with freedom: making them feel trusted. I’ve got quite a lot of experience. Direct experience, in the first place, and then through my children from all over the world, of every race and tongue. I remember the young people who came around me in the Work’s early years, big fellows, sixteen, eighteen, or nineteen years old. I used to tell them: “I believe whatever each of you tells me, even though a hundred notaries unanimously say the opposite.” What was the result? Out of a hundred, just one would deceive me. The other ninety-nine felt… well… loyal. Do the same with your children. If on some occasion they deceive you, don’t let on that you’ve found them out. Understand them, find excuses for them. Haven’t you and I done the same with our Lord, and we’ve come back? May your children realize that you are their best friend; that no one loves them as much as their father and mother. You will see how proud your children are of that. But don’t expect them to be headto-toe saints. No one is a saint in this life: saints are put on altars after they die, and they had the possibility of going to hell until… until one second before they died. Is this clear? El Prado (Madrid), 18 October 1972

## Affection and fortitude {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.3.5}

When bringing up our children, how can we combine fortitude with affection? You mothers are born teachers. Besides, don’t forget that you have the grace of state. But, mothers, exercise your teaching skills first of all on your husbands. Because they tend to be a bit passive – they can’t hear us! – and leave the children to your care, as if children were not an important business. They go off to their work; you are left at home most of the time. As for the children, they say, it’s your job to bring them up. No, indeed! It’s a job for the two of you!… So both of you need to talk things over, pray for the children, study their characters, and show them what it’s like to live as normal, ordinary Christians without doing anything strange or overly pious (…). And with regard to your children, you are a teacher, a “psychologist”; and your husband is one too. The teaching skill and justice of mothers consists of treating unequal children unequally. If one of your children gets sick, you treat him with more affection, making a fuss of him, while he is sick. But don’t let him take on an attitude of being sick, because that’s not good for him. If he acts spoiled, shake him out of it a little… So you asked me a question, and it turns out that you are a great teacher yourself. And your husband will become another expert teacher too. Tabancura (Santiago de Chile), 5 July 1974

Father, when I talk to our Lord about my six children… What a good conversation! ... How can I combine my affection towards them with the necessary fortitude? To what point should I let myself be guided by my affection?

Affection has no limits. When there is fortitude, affection is also there, since fortitude is a part of love. Agreed? And then, you can’t treat all your children in the same way. Each one is different; each one is a jewel our Lord has placed in your hands, because he has had a lot of trust in your wife and in you. So, cooperate with God, so that each jewel may be lustrous and bright. Retamar (Madrid), 28 October 1972

## Smacking is not the way {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.3.6}

Believe me: the question of freedom depends a lot on parents. I could tell you about some mothers who give smacks all the time! That way they get nowhere; they achieve nothing. It is better to be understanding, though not so understanding that your children do whatever they like. I’ll tell you what one mother did with a child who was really difficult when it came to eating. “You don’t want that? Well, don’t eat it.” Someone else suggested: “Perhaps something different could be made for him…” “Nothing special is to be made for him!” answered the mother, “He can eat the next course!” That child did not eat peppers, because he was silly, until he was fourteen years old, because he had got it into his head that he did not like them. And you should see how he came to like them, once he had actually tasted them!… I repeat: treat them with affection, a lot of affection. You solve nothing with smacks. You need to explain things to them with educational expertise, Christian expertise, so that they come to understand things from an early age, little by little. Belagua (Pamplona), 8 October 1972

## Neither the stick nor the carrot {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.3.7}

Father, when it comes to raising our children, which would you advise, the stick or the carrot? Neither the stick nor the carrot. I advise you to keep calm with your children, and not just smack them for the sake of it. The children get angry, you get all upset, you suffer because you do love them a lot, and on top of all that, you have to calm down again. Be a little patient, reprimand them when you are no longer upset, and between you and them alone. Don’t humiliate them in front of their brothers and sisters. Talk to them, reason a bit with them, so they realize they should behave differently, because then they’re pleasing God. That way you are training them well, and soon enough they will be opening up their own paths in life and being good Christians and good parents too, if God leads them that way. So the first step is to avoid both extremes: neither being too soft nor too harsh. Bell-lloc del Plà (Gerona), 24 November 1972

## Home is a school {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.3.8}

Father, tourism, the consumer society, everything influences our children, particularly in their upbringing, their love of comfort, and their entertainment. What can we do? Keep up those healthy customs that you have always followed in your homes. Make friends, very good friends with your children. With that friendship, plus your authority as parents, give them appropriate advice. Children do listen. Besides, when you were their age you too were rebellious… Let us go along with them as far as a Christian can. You need to have a big heart, and make severity compatible with understanding them at their level. And pray.

And then, don’t go on and on at your children. Don’t give them the kind of liberty that becomes licence, but respect them. Don’t be overly generous with money, because in general you tend to give them too much money. You can give it to them later on, and even more. Let them learn to live a sober life, even a somewhat Spartan life; that is, a Christian life. It’s tough, but we have to be brave: be brave enough to teach them austerity. Otherwise you won’t accomplish anything. Castelldaura (Barcelona), 28 November 1972

## Teach them to be loyal {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.3.9}

Father, what are the main virtues we should teach our children? Loyalty, since nowadays there’s a lot of disloyalty around. Help them to be loyal and sincere, not to be afraid to tell you things. To do that, you must be loyal towards them. Treat them as if they were adults, adapting your approach to their needs, their age and their characters. Be their friend, be kind and honest to them, be sincere and straightforward. Pozoalbero (Jerez de la Frontera), 12 November 1972

## Excessive affection {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.3.10}

Apart from the bad things our children may see in the street, there is something else more subtle but perhaps worse, because it’s less obvious: I refer to comfort-seeking. What advice can you give us to turn our children into battlers? If you overdo affection you make them pretty soft and comfort-loving. When it’s not Dad, it’s Mum. And if not, it’s Grandma. And sometimes, all three, each one separately, and keeping it a secret from the others. And the child, with three such secrets, can lose his soul. Come to an agreement. Don’t be stingy with the children, but bear in mind what each of

them is capable of, what will ensure their peace of mind, their capacity for self-control: and they should never have an abundance of things, until they can earn it themselves. IESE (Barcelona), 27 November 1972

## Learning how to manage money {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.3.11}

Father, we’ve got a lot of oil in Venezuela and… So you’ve got a lot of money. That’s just what I was leading up to. This abundance has given us a lot of material progress, but it has also created, at least for a good part of the population, a sense of ease, of life being comfortable. My question is: How can we raise our children to love hard work in such an environment? I would take them to have a look at some of those districts around the great city of Caracas. I would put my hand over their eyes, and then take it away so they can see those poor shacks, all on top of each other: and you’ll have answered them! They should learn that money has to be used well; they have to learn how to manage it, so that everyone can share in some way in the goods of this earth. Because it’s very easy to say “I’m a very good person”, if one has never been in a situation of real need. A friend of mine, a wealthy man, once told me: “I don’t know if I’m a good man, since I’ve never been in a situation where my wife was sick, while I was jobless and penniless; I’ve never seen my children weak with hunger, when I myself was out of work and broke; I’ve never found myself in the gutter, with no roof over my head… I don’t know if I’m an honest man: what would I have done, if all those things had happened to me?” Look, we must try to ensure that this does not happen to anybody. We must equip people so that, through their work,

they can attain a minimum of well-being, not have to worry about their old age or sickness, they can educate their children, and so many other necessary things. Nothing that happens to others should be indifferent to us and, from our place in society, we ought to foster charity and justice. Altoclaro (Caracas), 9 February 1975

## I’ve killed all the storks {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.3.12}

What about when children start growing up and opening their eyes as to how human life begins? Dad should make friends with his sons. There’s no alternative but to make an effort in this area, since, if Dad has not spoken to the children, a moment comes when they are led by curiosity – understandable on the one hand and unhealthy on the other – to ask others about the origins of life. They ask some cheeky rascal of a friend, and then start looking upon their parents with disgust. If, instead, you have followed your son’s development from infancy and see that the time is right, and after praying for our Lord’s guidance, you explain the origin of life in a noble way, the boy will go and hug his mother because she has been so good, and he will give you a kiss and exclaim, with all his heart, “How good God is! He has made use of my parents, letting them participate in his creative power.” He won’t say it like that, because he doesn’t know how to, but he will feel it. And he will think of your love not as something dirty, but as a holy thing. Enxomil (Oporto), 31 October 1972

And with the girls, you mothers should speak clearly, forthrightly to them. If not they will go to some cheeky shameless girl and from then on, I repeat, they will look upon

you with disgust. Don’t tell them lies; I have killed all the storks. Tell them that God has made use of you to bring them into the world, that they are the fruit of your love, of your dedication, of your sacrifices… That’s why you must make friends with your children, giving them the basis for talking to you trustingly about what concerns them. Do you understand me? Your children’s souls come first; everything else follows. I am not scolding you, my sons, for not looking after your children as you ought…but I hope your wives don’t let you off the hook from now on! Brafa (Barcelona), 22 November 1972

## When Dad is missing {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.3.13}

What would you advise those of us who must bring up our children alone, since their dad is missing, and furthermore we have to form them cheerfully? My daughter, the Lord will give you both a mother’s gentleness and a father’s strength. Your husband will be helping you from heaven; have no doubt about that. Besides, go to St Joseph. I know a young widow who speaks delightfully, and who has a great devotion to St Joseph. Because St Joseph was the master of the house at Nazareth; and the Mother of God, our Mother the ever virgin Mary, would show him every sign of respect. Well, this widow has put an image of St Joseph in a prominent place, and calls him “the man of the house”. Get hold of a little picture or statue of St Joseph and pray to him, saying: “You, who led and guided both Mother and Son so wisely amidst the perils of this world…” You are well acquainted with the life of our Lord. You read the Gospel often, a little every day. Have a lot of

## “Infecting” others with joy {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.3.14}

It’s not that our fathers didn’t teach us, because mine also used to pray with me. Don’t force them to say long prayers: teach them to say short ones, but every day. When they’re very small, you take their hand and help them make the sign of the Cross, with their own little hand. That’s something they never forget. Your gentleness and your piety, and the piety of your husbands, of their parents, remain deep in the children’s souls. And if later on passions come, and start to drag them down, and they go through a bad time in life, in the end the good seed begins to sprout again. Do you see? The devotion you mothers sow in your children’s hearts is never lost! You mothers and you fathers; but since it is a mother who asked the question, I have spoken especially of them. Sumaré (São Paulo), 4 June 1974

## Bedtime prayers {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.5.3}

Your children shouldn’t go to bed like little puppies. I like to put it that way because it’s very clear and I can make myself understood. Little puppies lie down in a corner, and that’s all. Not so with your children. They have to make the sign of the Cross before going to bed, and say some words to the Blessed Virgin Mary and to God our Lord, even when their soul is not totally clean.

But don’t overload them with many prayers when they’re still very young. Teach them just a few, and get them to understand what they are saying. Let them say with you those short, charming, traditional prayers that have been on the lips of Christian mothers for centuries. El Prado (Madrid), 18 October 1972

## Children’s confession {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.5.4}

When bringing up children, what is the most important way to form their consciences? Taking them to confession soon, very soon, as soon as they reach the age of reason. And if you can prepare them yourself, do it; if not, go to a priest you can trust. It’s not true that children are traumatized by it! It’s not true that it does them harm! It did me a lot of good, and my mother took me to confession when I was six years old. She took me by the hand to her own confessor. And I went to confession like we do in my country. There the men go to confession at the front of the confessional. I was so small that I couldn’t be seen… so the priest opened the confessional door and I went a little bit inside. I made my confession and… I was so happy! He gave me a very amusing penance; I’m not going to tell you what it was, because I told it then, and I can still hear my parents’ laughter ringing in my ears; they couldn’t stop laughing. They had to give me a dish to eat which the priest was very fond of. He didn’t even ask me to say a Hail Mary! How kind of him! Miralba (Lima), 14 July 1974

I think that young children, my own anyway, like going to confession a lot and even enjoy it. What can we do to ensure that they don’t lose this?

Tell those people who go around telling lies, that it’s not true that children are afraid of confession! I myself have been a child – you won’t deny that and, inside, I still am one – and I never found confession frightening. It has given me a lot of joy and consolation; it has left my soul clean and taken away my worries. It has always made me feel closer to God. Tajamar (Madrid), 22 October 1972

I teach religion to young girls. What is the best age and the best way to introduce them to the Sacrament of Penance? As soon as possible! I have a lot of experience in this. So I’m speaking because I can speak. And I’m answering you because it’s my duty to do so (…). What a wonderful thing it is! I have seen the same in so many people I have spoken to. What a wonderful thing it is to go to confession! I have heard the confessions of thousands and thousands of children. It’s not a waste of time: one benefits from it, one learns from those souls in which the Holy Spirit is acting. Just like you mothers give your infants your own blood, and then the nectar of your breast, so too the Holy Spirit, inside the souls of those little ones, who perhaps are utterly unaware of it, is acting, acting, acting. And the priest cooperates with him, with the Holy Spirit. And as well as that, there is the grace of the sacrament, which is also the Holy Spirit in action. So, yes indeed, my daughter. Six years old is already a good age for it, and for some children, even earlier. Delay it? I think it’s very wrong! I don’t care who says so! I don’t speak in secret, I’ve never had any secrets, except those of confession. Which are not mine anyway, but Christ’s. So… get them to confession early! Prepare them well. What they need to know in order to go is simple enough. And God bless

you, because you’ve asked me a question which is very opportune right now. Tabancura (Santiago de Chile), 2 July 1974

## Teach them to love our Lady {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.5.5}

Father, how can we foster love for our Lady in our children? You foster it if they see it in you, because you pray devoutly to our Lady; if they see joy in your face, when they are pious; if they notice that you dress modestly, because you know that your Mother, who is also my Mother and Mother of us all, is looking at you from heaven. De Maria numquam satis, the saying goes: when it comes to Mary, to loving and venerating her, one can never say enough. She will bless your children and the homes they may make later on. Belagua (Pamplona), 8 October 1972

## The family Rosary {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.5.6}

Father, what does the Rosary mean for Catholics, and what should we do to benefit more from it? My son, the Rosary is a prayer that is very pleasing to Mary most Holy; it has been part of Catholic piety for centuries. At the same time, it is a meditation on the mysteries of the life of our Lord and his Mother. And so I recommend it with all my heart, as a form of family prayer too, although you shouldn’t force your young children to say it: it’s better for Mum to say three Hail Marys with them on their own. You shouldn’t oblige the older children either: if they want to come with the others, let them; if not, let them be; they’ll come eventually. It has to be something voluntary. Guadalaviar (Valencia), 17 November 1972

How can we teach them to pray the Rosary? I repeat that it is taught more by example than by word. Let them see you practising it and that you pray it devoutly, without routine. If they don’t show up one day, pretend not to notice, and don’t say anything. If it happens again, then Mummy, affectionately, three or four days later, should be the one to tell them: “Look, make this small sacrifice. Besides, you’ll see how it’s not such a great sacrifice for the whole family to get together to greet our Lady, so that she may bless us and help us.” If the children are a little older, never impose your will on them to make them come to family prayers. Perhaps, at the start, they won’t come and pray, but after a couple of months they will, and will say the prayers themselves with great pleasure. Try to instil a life of piety in them from early childhood, but without imposing a burden on them. To tell the truth, I found it hard to pray the Rosary when I was small. But, as you mothers are very bright, you will teach them to say just two or three Hail Marys to begin with, and that’ll be enough. It’s a very good custom! Don’t make them say the Rosary at the age of three. Just teach them those beautiful prayers we have in our country, and which we grown-ups also say. In this way, little by little, they will grow in piety. And when they have begun praying with the whole family, don’t add too many Hail Marys at the end. Pray them yourselves on your own and, afterwards, take each child aside and say to them: Look, it would be good to pray for this intention… and the child, on his own, will begin to pray. Be clever! Catechesis in Spain and Portugal, 1972

## With complete freedom {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.5.7}

What about when the children are older and don’t want to pray with the rest of the family? It’s important not to lose these wonderful traditions of family prayer, but don’t oblige them to pray. Let your children see you keeping up the family prayers lovingly; let them know what time you say the Rosary, and they will end up joining you. But without forcing them! If they leave themselves open – which they will, if you truly strive to be their friend – tell each one, separately: “Look, this custom of ours is centuries old, and ought to be continued because our Lady likes it very much, and God is pleased by it and he blesses us this way. But say it when you want to, feel free.” And they will return. You must of course teach them their obligations: to go to confession once a year; and if one is in mortal sin, the sooner the better. And also to attend Mass on Sundays and holy days of obligation… Don’t oblige them to do anything, but let them see you pray: that’s what I saw my parents do, and it has stayed in my heart. That way, when your children get to my age, they will fondly remember their mother and father, who compelled them only by their example, with a smile, while giving them doctrine when it was called for, without pestering them. Retamar (Madrid), 28 October 1972

## Religious duties {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.5.8}

We are told nowadays, and it’s often repeated to our children, that it’s better to go to Mass any day of the week, when one feels the need, rather than on Sundays and holy days… Who says so? Where?

It’s being said among our youngsters. How should we answer them? Answer them that the Church is a perfect society, and has the right to make laws; she commands us to keep Sunday holy, and, in her motherly goodness, has also given us the possibility of fulfilling our Sunday obligation on Saturday. It’s very good to go to Mass on other days. However, by law, we have to go to Mass every Sunday and holy day of obligation, or on their vigil, as is now laid down. The Church makes laws, which we, the faithful, have a duty to obey. Pozoalbero (Jerez de la Frontera), 12 November 1972

I’d like to know if parents ought to oblige their children to go to Mass, when they don’t want to. When they get older, you should give them a little more freedom, with personal responsibility. Let them know that they will have to answer to God for their actions; they should know that, when this earthly life is over, eternal life begins, and that there is a judgment; and that after judgment comes Hell or Heaven; usually, in the latter case, after passing through Purgatory. You can’t force your older children to fulfil their religious duties. You should not grab them by the arm and tell them: “I’m taking you off to Mass.” Because, even if you take them to church physically, if they don’t want to attend Mass, they won’t have done so. Let them know that they are doing wrong and offending God; and that they offend him gravely if they don’t fulfil their obligations in a grave matter. But as for you, keep calm, and pray. Remember how St Monica prayed for her son Augustine. If you pray for them, after having explained their duties to them, be certain that in the end God will move their

hearts, and the Holy Spirit – whose feast we celebrate today – will draw those souls, those hearts, to contrition and good conduct. Don’t get upset, you’re a Christian mother. Mauá Auditorium (São Paulo), 2 June 1974

## Unafraid of life and unafraid of death {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.5.9}

Father, should we speak to our children about death? In what way and when? Why shouldn’t we speak to children about death? You mothers begin to do so as soon as they have a bit of common sense: Mind that car, it could kill you! Watch that dog, it might bite you! Why shouldn’t you speak to them about death? About death, and later, my daughter, when the time comes, about life! Before any nasty boy or twisted girl gets to tell them about life, you should speak to them: your husband and you. In the presence of God, with such refinement that they will give you a hug and say: “Mum, how good you are. And how good Dad is! And how good God our Lord is, to have given you this power to bring us into the world! How beautiful this is!” But no storks, please! They need the truth, the truth at the right time, heard from the lips of mother or father. Not in primary school, nor in secondary school. The Church never wanted it that way. It’s not good for them! Your children cannot be treated like animals. They’re children of God! And they’re your children also. Each one of them is worth more than the most precious gem; and you have to treat them differently, according to the stage of their mental and physiological development. So be alert. And make them really and truly devout. Tabancura (Santiago de Chile), 5 July 1974

# A problem that won’t go away {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.6}

## We mustn’t exaggerate {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.6.1}

Father, how can we help our adolescent children, when they think they are grown up and don’t accept our advice, or don’t want to agree with what we say? I have the answer to that one straight away. Because you and I were adolescents once and we did the same. Let anyone who never ever protested at that age raise his hand. None of you dares to? Neither do I, of course. So let’s not exaggerate, nor should you worry too much. Instead, have a positive concern for them, pray to our Lord for them, and help them with your good example. “But aren’t you always saying the same thing, Father?” Naturally! This is the Christian solution for everyone: example, piety, and your motherly words. You mothers are excellent psychologists, because you know how to treat your children according to their individual needs. Your injustice is perfectly just, because the children are different, and so you

treat each one differently; you show greater affection, if possible, for the one who is farthest from God. In this way you will draw them to him: with your kindness, with your prayer, and without too much drama, since we were all rebellious at their age and we are also being rebellious right now. Can’t you see that in this world around us people are talking only about sex and violence? There’s a great tendency, because of so much diabolical influence, for people to live like animals. We rebel against this way of viewing life: we are more rebellious than anyone! We don’t want to live like animals, but as God’s friends. Little by little, we grow in interior life, and become beloved children of God, and of his Blessed Mother, who is also our Mother. And so we also protest, and our rebellion is very pleasing to God. So, let your children “protest” a bit, and look after them with real affection, being a real friend of theirs. Don’t worry, they’ll return. Bell-lloc del Plà (Gerona), 24 November 1972

## An enemy: the environment {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.6.2}

Father, we hear a lot about the generation gap and conflicts between parents and children. What advice do you have for us parents to lessen this sort of conflict? My son, this conflict has always existed to a greater or lesser degree. It’s almost natural. So we shouldn’t exaggerate it, although the situation now is really distressing, because there is this diabolical propaganda: yes, diabolical! The devil doesn’t take holidays and he is at work everywhere. Besides, there’s an international conspiracy to promote brutality, violence, sexuality and rebellion. I told you that we too were rebels in times gone by, but then there

wasn’t this unhealthy, filthy, brutal atmosphere. And so your children have some excuse. You parents don’t have so much, because you should put more effort into freeing your family from this diabolical – I said diabolical, and I repeat it: diabolical – atmosphere in the world around us, and try to form them as Christians… Pozoalbero (Jerez de la Frontera), 12 November 1972

## Sometimes, the parents are at fault {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.6.3}

It’s relatively easy for parents to talk to and be friends with their children until they’re ten or twelve years old. But from then on, they become more withdrawn and we find it very difficult to keep up a conversation with them. How can that earlier friendship be kept alive? I think you’re right, but perhaps you’re a bit to blame yourselves, because quite often you try to give them the impression that you never did anything wrong. It’s very good for the boys to know that when Dad was twelve, or fourteen or fifteen, he too did this or that silly thing, but that he then he went to his father – the boy’s grandfather – and opened his heart to him, and his father dispelled his worries and helped him. You can exaggerate a little here; our Lord won’t mind. Afterwards, speak to them one to one, man to man, with a great deal of trust. Devote some time to them, because you fathers can easily neglect this, leaving the task of raising the children to your wives, and that’s absurd. What children want is a father who is loyal, who makes them feel trusted, who sometimes even tells them the worries and disappointments he’s had – though here you have to be prudent: don’t tell them everything, but only what they can take in, without stirring up hatred in their young hearts… If

you treat them like this, you will see how they change and listen to you, since you’ll have become friends. So, not all the blame is the children’s; a good part of it is the dads’ and mums’ fault, for giving the impression that you are perfect when in fact you never have been. Retamar (Madrid), 28 October 1972

It hurts, Father, to see some of our children turning out odd… We shouldn’t make too much of it. It happens with everything. Sometimes we make a great effort at something and then… You shouldn’t forget that your children are free beings and, little by little, become responsible. Have you examined at all how you behave towards them? Mightn’t you be too hard on them? Or, on the contrary, too soft? Do you act in concert with your wife in bringing up the children? In choosing their teachers, have you used a holy cunning? Have you tried to get them to go to a priest, to one of those who spend time in the confessional? Belagua (Pamplona), 8 October 1972

## The best way to educate {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.6.4}

Don’t let them be afraid. Let them know that you too were rebellious at their age… Let’s be honest: anyone, ladies included, who didn’t give their parents a hard time, raise their hand. Who dares to? It’s only to be expected that your children should also make you suffer a bit. So, one day, get hold of the child who is a bit rebellious, and go for a walk together, treat him to something nice, and say to him: “Do you know that when I was your age, I made your grandparents suffer? Look, I pulled this or that trick on them, and they forgave me right away. Now I’m very sorry for having made them suffer: it was a real shame!” Your child will catch on, he will realize that you can understand him, find excuses for

him, and love him, with his defects. Including his defects! He’ll correct himself, little by little. Who could be a better educator than a father or a mother? Your way of educating, if you are good Christians, is wonderful. Treat them as you would have liked to be treated when you were their age. Above all, show them the greatest possible trust. It’s better to be fooled by them occasionally than to make them think you don’t love them enough, that you don’t trust them. Let yourself be fooled once in a while. It’s not the end of the world! Enxomil (Oporto), 31 October 1972

## Unfounded worries {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.6.5}

Sometimes our children cause us considerable worry; we easily get alarmed and don’t know how to guide them… I understand. Let me tell you two stories, one from my father and the other about my mother. I usually don’t talk much about my parents. I feel reluctant, but anyway, just this once… My father told me that on one occasion my grandfather got very upset with him because he had bought an infernal machine: a bicycle. Evidently my father was one of those young fellows who followed the latest fashion… He bought his bike, he was telling me, and it had solid rubber tyres: “Whenever I went over a stone, it bounced up in the air!” Once, when the bike bounced, he fell off and broke his arm. My grandfather was called to the scene of the accident and took care of him, but he gave the bicycle away to someone else and told my father: “I never want to see you on that infernal machine again!” The story about my mother is different. I was fifteen or sixteen when I began to feel inklings of God’s love: to think,

to know, to yearn… But I didn’t say anything to anyone else. Meanwhile my mother saw me growing up. At that age I wasn’t bad looking – who is at sixteen? – and my mother realized that I was beginning to notice girls, or something of the sort. There’s nothing surprising about that, so if you see your sons taking notice of girls, let them be... One day she drew me aside and said: “My son, try to behave properly, and when you are thinking about getting married, seriously thinking about it, look…” And she quoted me a proverb all you mothers know: “Look for a lass, neither so beautiful she bewitches, nor so ugly she horrifies.” My grandfather was mistaken; my mother was mistaken; and maybe you too are mistaken about what your children are up to. But you are right to be concerned, as were my grandfather and my mother, because you love them so much. La Lloma (Valencia), 19 November 1972

## Confidence to speak out {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.6.6}

Father, what advice do you have for parents who feel they have failed in the task of raising their children? I always give the same prescription: first, pray to the Blessed Virgin Mary, make frequent use of the sacraments, try to get your husband to do so too, and help your children with your own good example. And then, have the patience I mentioned before, a lot of patience, the patience of St Monica, St Augustine’s mother… Don’t get angry with your children. If they are very small, don’t slap them; you won’t get anywhere that way. And the older ones? If you’re going to lose your temper, keep quiet, because afterwards you’ll only regret having spoken. When you have calmed down, and have gone to God and prayed, get them on their own and talk to them trustingly and

straightforwardly. Don’t berate them. Remind them of the days when they behaved well. Help them see that their father and you are their best advisers, that they should open their heart, because you will understand them, forgive them, and help them. You will see how well everything turns out, if you don’t lose (or if you regain) your children’s trust. Catechesis in Spain and Portugal, 1972

## Engaging with the children {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.6.7}

Father, as a mother I’d like you to tell us something about dialogue between parents and children. You often complain about your children; but you forget that all of us gave our parents a rough time too. If there is anyone one here who doesn’t agree, let him put up his hand and we’ll ask him a few questions. Sometimes you make far too much fuss. It’s true they are wearing long hair now. A hundred years ago they did the same. In a hundred years’ time they’ll be doing it again. We shouldn’t make too much of it. (…) To sum up: you need to give in a little. Don’t immediately say: “That’s a lack of respect.” No. It’s a sign of love, of trust. And if at times they are somewhat too outspoken, let them be: they’re your children. Point it out to them gently, and make use of that to have deeper conversations. You’ll see that they listen to you. That way you will be treating your children, like your husband, as little creatures to be shaped by their mother’s touch. And Mother is the one who has sensitive hands, and who can give the final touches to the clay. Coliseo Theatre (Buenos Aires), 23 June 1974

## Go out to meet them {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.6.8}

Father, people frequently complain about the way things are going: young people, the university, and so on, in a critical and pessimistic way. What can we do to combat this critical, complaining and pessimistic spirit? I see that you are seeking something positive: that, instead of criticising, people should be offering solutions. Isn’t that it? We know very well that young people have always been a bit rebellious. In my time and in yours, we young people got up to quite a lot of mischief. So let’s not get frightened when youngsters are a bit rebellious. Still less should we be scandalized, you and I, who have been brought together here by a great shared ideal of love of God and love of neighbour. Tell your children this; and get your friends to do the same with their children. Get them to fight, to give positive solutions! Don’t get angry with them; listen to them, talk with them man to man, affectionately and sympathetically. Try to meet them halfway, and you’ll see how well you get along, and how this time of brutality will come to an end. But we all have to make an effort: myself to start with. After invoking Our Lady of Ransom – how I love her! – I feel strengthened, because she will grant me her Son’s grace, to tell you that, if you do what I say, better times will come: for this country, for this city, for your families, and for the whole world. Brafa (Barcelona), 22 November 1972

## Living a Christian life {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.6.9}

Father, what would you say to us mothers who have tried to bring up our children in the faith and with good morals, and then find that when they grow up they no longer follow their parents’

example and advice, or stop practising the faith? Sometimes we feel a bit at a loss and don’t know what to do. My daughter, you have had a good schooling: your parents’ home: I’m sure of that! And there you were taught the truths of the faith, thanks to that old catechism. Later on, you were taken to Catholic teachers. You’re doing the same with your children. But now, as the sea is a bit rough… It will calm down, don’t worry! Even with Jesus in the boat, the boat seemed to be sinking. But Peter’s boat does not sink! I was saying that, now, though the sea is rough, you are doing the same with your children as your parents did with you. First, teach them that good Christian doctrine. Take them to good teachers. And … keep an eye on the teachers. Because it can very easily happen that the teachers wander onto wrong paths, and you have a duty call them to attention and say: “I don’t want that for my children! I have brought them to you with bodies and souls. My children are not animals. My children are children of God, and I want them to have the true faith, the faith of our fathers.” And besides, teach them with your example. And as they get older, give them a bit more rope, a bit of freedom for them to administer. Allow them some responsibility. Try not to let them have too much money. Because you give them some, and so does your husband, and so does granny … and so does that doting aunt, your granny’s sister, and so on… far too much money! It’s better for them to have money when they’ve earned it, right? So keep them short! And don’t get angry with me, you young people! It’s better for you to know what things cost. You will be happier if you spend what is yours, what you have earned with the sweat of your brow. You, the mother who asked me that question, you were

already doing all this. No doubt you were asking the question because you think there are people here who need to hear it again; and it’s good for me to recall it too, because that way I learn. Thank you. Anhembi Park (São Paulo), 1 June 1974

## Helping them gently {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.6.10}

Father, is it good to use the rod with older children? Older? What age do you call older? Well, say twenty to twenty-two. Listen, by then even very Christian families have given them a bit of freedom. Let them use their freedom well, and don’t be a tyrant. Pray for them, entrust them to their Guardian Angel’s care, give them some good advice at the right time, don’t go on at them, and be understanding with them. Also, you can easily approach the Director of a centre of Opus Dei, to discuss the situation of that son or daughter of yours, and ask: “Would there be any young people of his or her age around?” There will be, and they can make friends with your children, real friends, not pretending, but helping them live an upright, joyful, Christian life. You have so many ways available to help them without beating them up…! Catechesis in Spain and Portugal, 1972

Some parents get distressed when their children threaten, violently and insolently, to leave home, because they don’t accept their parents’ way of running things. How can they be helped? You’ve touched on a sore point. This is something happening all over the world. Let’s not forget that we also were a bit rebellious, we had our own rebellions, although

then they weren’t so violent and shamelessly sexual as now. Nevertheless, those children won’t leave if you are clever in the way you handle them. Ask the Mother of God, who is also Sedes Sapientiae, Seat of Wisdom, to grant you the wisdom to instruct your children, to keep them close to you and close to her, without hurting them, ensuring that they retain their freedom while you retain the authority you need. Go about it gently. You mothers have a very clever and holy way of doing this, and a special grace from God: the grace of state. Always act in concert with your husband, placing these things in God’s hands and offering some small sacrifice, and controlling your temper. That way your children will change; don’t worry. But act gently. Tajamar (Madrid), 22 October 1972

## A powerful lever {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.6.11}

Father, what can we do to influence our young children, so as to help them come closer to God instead of following a crazy sort of lifestyle? Be patient. And you, who love them very much, as is clear from your question, continue helping them with your own good example. Then, pray for them, because the prayer of a mother or father, when they remember their children before God, is most powerful. Pray! Don’t be hard on them. Why should you? You are the one who should understand them better than anyone. Have you ever given them an unnecessary slap? No, don’t answer me. If you have done so, without good reason, that’s not good. It’s better to put up with things and wait until the next day, or the day after that. “But by then they’ll have broken two dozen plates more!” So what? At least you won’t have broken the love they have for you. Pray for them. Entrust them to the Blessed Virgin Mary,

be a very good friend to St Joseph, who did a great job as a father, and have a lot of devotion to their Guardian Angels. Tajamar (Madrid), 29 October 1972

## Don’t lose hope {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.6.12}

Father, I have three children in the Work, and a daughter who has no faith and is very insubordinate. What should I do? Please be seated, my daughter. Your daughter does have faith. Is she baptized? Yes, Father. Well then, your daughter has faith. Because faith, hope and charity are three virtues we can’t acquire through our own efforts: the Lord infuses them generously, in Baptism. Therefore, your daughter does have faith. What happens is that, with all the racket going on in the world nowadays, and because of some people’s bad example, your daughter may have gone to someone believing she would find light, and has found darkness; thinking that she would find strength, and has found doubt; thinking that she would find uprightness, and has found a wretched schizophrenic complex. I’m already loving your daughter a lot. I will pray for her. That’s what I want. And pray yourself, for she’ll be all right. OK? Yes, I do pray for her. ## True freedom {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.7.8}

## Drawing them closer to the Work {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.8.2}

How can we get our children to love the Work from an early age…? I’ve already told you. First, by not speaking to them continually about the Work: without wanting to, you could turn them against it. But let them see that you behave well, that you are understanding, kind, and affectionate. Let them see that you are deeply interested in everything about them, that you make allowances for them, and that you help them. They’ll come to know the Work through your example and your smile, even when things go wrong. Little children, I always remind you, are exacting witnesses of their parents’ life. If they see you smile, if they see you bear up with Christian fortitude, if they see your life of piety – which you live unassumingly – you’re already bringing them closer to the Work, because you have brought them closer to God and the Work is his. It’s not mine: I wouldn’t have been able to do anything. I’ve only been a poor instrument. Father, and when the children don’t understand…?

What you must do, my daughter – please forgive me – is not to nag them… Poor kids! They don’t want to understand… They will come to understand you, believe me. If I were your son, on many occasions I’d argue with you. I have to admit that sometimes I argued with my parents, that I gave them a bit of a hard time. There are certain ages that seem specially designed to give others a hard time! And it doesn’t mean they don’t love you. One can’t expect children, when they’re still very young, to be understanding. Don’t speak to them about Opus Dei. Let them be. Pray for them, love them, always have an affectionate smile for them. If you have to correct them – I’ll tell you what you’ve heard me say a hundred times – don’t do it when you’re upset. Do it when you’ve calmed down. And treat them like adults, in accordance with the age they’re at. Your children notice everything, from the time they’re very small. You should always speak to them affectionately, and understand them, and understand that they don’t understand you. You’ll find that, if you pray and act like this, eventually the day will come when they themselves will ask you about Opus Dei. Then you’ll be clever enough to say: “Well, I don’t want to talk about that. What concern is it of yours? Forget it.” And when they insist a second time: “Well, look… why don’t you go and ask so-and-so. He’ll explain it to you better than I could.” Send them to a brother or sister of yours who works with people their age. Stay calm, on the sidelines as it were, but keep praying: that’s the way! Everything will turn out fine. Remember what I’m telling you. I don’t go around looking for miracles, my daughter. I believe in God’s Providence and, because I’ve touched it with my hands, I can

## Don’t pester {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.8.3}

There’s but one thing you have to do… I don’t know if you say this here, to “pester”, because sometimes there are idioms that are only used in one region or country. Do you have that expression: “pestering people”? Well, then, don’t pester them. Pray for them. Let them see your good example. Don’t speak to them too much about the Work. I’m almost asking you for a miracle… I was more contrary than anyone and, if people kept on at me about the same thing all the time, I got fed up with it… I couldn’t stand it. Apply some psychology. Leave them in peace. You can also speak to your Director or to a priest of the Work, and through friends of their own age and situation, they’ll bring them along. You can behave as if you knew nothing. I am not suggesting that you put on an act, nor that you lie: far be it from me to suggest anything of the sort. Don’t dream of lying, but leave them alone, let them be. Pray a lot and, furthermore, help them with your own good example; an example that will never be sanctimonious, if you have the spirit of the Work: a stout-hearted example of Christian virtues practised at difficult moments as well as when things are going well.

Listen, my children, we’re all tempted to depict our life as a tragedy. We only notice the dark areas of the painting... “My Lord, what a disaster!” “Oh my God, what a tragedy!” “Alas, my God…!” But what would a painting be without dark areas, without its shadows, without its dark brush strokes? There would be no painting. It’s good to have shadows too. We should love those brush strokes, and the brush, for it is God’s brush, and the hand behind it: the sovereign, loving, fatherly and motherly hand – as I have just said about myself, with a pardonable “pride” – the hand of God our Lord. Yet there are others who try to block their children’s path. How can we make them understand that it is a gift from God? My daughter, you yourself will have seen – and this makes me very sad – that there are hardly any vocations for the religious life, for seminaries or for religious sisters. I ask God our Lord, and Our Lady of Mercy, for whom I feel a great devotion, to grant us this great mercy: that in this country we may see once again in every home the holy pride of having family members giving themselves to God’s service. So my answer is not going to be selfish. I’m not one to “rope people in”. My job is to close the doors of entry to Opus Dei, and to open the exit doors to leavers. But you can’t get them to leave, not even with a shotgun! Talk to that mother – talk to her first, because you women are the ones who govern in the home, and it’s good for things to be so, for you put all the gentleness and kindness of your womanly heart into it, and your husbands benefit from it – and tell her that God our Lord chooses whom he wishes, and that it’s a sign of his special love for that family. If the vocation is genuine, it always thrives.

Tell her, also, that I understand her very well: she has an obligation to consider things in a practical light, and I’m not annoyed with her. I understand her completely, and I’m on her side. But, after that, tell her that God is more powerful. And that if she had raised her children to be rascals, it would have been difficult for our Lord to find in their souls the soil ready to receive the seed of a vocation: that ninety percent of the vocation of the children is due to their mother and father. She has only herself to blame! Brafa (Barcelona), 23 November 1972

You often say that ninety percent of a vocation is due to the parents… Yes, my daughter, I do. Could you talk to us about that? My parents are here. You have to remember that your parents receive light from God: they have the grace of state to advise you. Second: your parents can look at your life “in the natural way of things”, thinking about you and your earthly life. They don’t offend God by doing that: they please him. But it’s a big step from there to the tyranny of not allowing you the freedom to be formed as you believe you ought to be, seeing besides that you are a daughter who honours them and who lives an upright life. Agreed? Yes, Father. Love your mum and dad a lot. Pay attention to them in everything… Yes, Father. And they’ll realize that there’s a limit to that “everything”. Tabancura (Santiago de Chile), 7 July 1974

## The parents’ romantic novel {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.8.9}

Father, these days you’ve being telling us how we parents compose a novel about our children, and how sometimes we settle for limited ideals, because we find it difficult to change the plot of the story when God asks for more. How can we acquire that detachment from our children and respect our children’s freedom? Look, my children, you parents do very well to look “in the natural way of things”. So, when you look at your children’s future, you have a duty to think of earthly things; and if the children come to you with a novel that’s too supernatural, and you probe it a bit, in agreement with the confessor – the children’s confessor, that is – you do well. You do well because it is your duty to accompany your children along the entire journey of life. And when they choose a path which, at first sight, appears to you to be extraordinarily spiritual and not very practical, you start to waver a little, and… I understand that perfectly. Tobias had to leave home, the younger Tobias – Tobias “Junior” … He had to travel to the house of Gabael to collect money owed to his father, and God our Lord had prepared everything for him: he had even prepared a bride for him, a woman both wealthy and very pretty. But his father wasn’t too sure, and then an individual appeared who turned out to be none other than St Raphael the Archangel, though he didn’t reveal who he was. So he puts his son under St Raphael’s protection, to accompany him during that journey. A father does well to seek that protection! Entrust your sons to St Raphael the Archangel, so that he may lead them, if it’s God’s will, to set up a happy, Christian, home, by marrying a woman who is pretty and good, holy and cheerful and faithful. And if not, you can entrust them also to St John, the adolescent Evangelist, who was the most manly of those

twelve men whom Jesus had at his side; who was the only one who persevered next to the Cross: the others, seized with fear, ran away. And it may be that God wants them to be like that: courageous; and the Cross can be frightening, even to parents. But it won’t frighten you if you entrust your children to St John the Evangelist. Coliseo Theatre (Buenos Aires), 26 June 1974

## Mothers’ jealousy {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.8.10}

Father, I’m a Numerary. Sometimes our parents (mothers especially) become a bit jealous of the Work. What can we do to get them to be happy about our service to God and to others? Go to your mum, and one of those days when it’s the turn for the joyful mysteries of the Rosary – as your mother has great devotion to our Lady – say to her: “Let’s pray.” When you get to the mystery of the Child lost and found in the Temple… she’ll kiss you on both cheeks and say: “My son, go wherever you want, with my blessing, my affection, and my prayers!” And she’ll be very happy when sometimes you aren’t all that near … In these nearly fifty years I have seen some very beautiful things. Mothers who were jealous, and some who had only one son, and who had brought him up in so Christian a fashion that God found the prime matter well disposed for a vocation to Opus Dei, which is not the vocation of a friar or a religious: we’re ordinary folk. It happens: mothers can get jealous. Not all of them; but some of them do get obsessed, and become a bit jealous… But later on (they all tell me this), when they suffer some sorrow, they find that they don’t have just one son to support them, they have lots. And when they have some good news, if they call, those sons are there as well. Tabancura (Santiago de Chile), 6 July 1974

## Respect the children’s freedom {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.8.11}

Look, my children, you mothers are very good, and you fathers are absolutely wonderful. You have a right to look “in the natural way of things”, as they say in my land, when considering the things that refer to your children’s welfare, and God is not annoyed at you because of that. But don’t forget that you could be weaving plans that may never come to pass. No sooner is the child born but mum is already planning to have him married to a particular girl, and they’ll do this or that. Dad thinks about the boy’s career or about getting him involved in the business. Each one composes a novel, a wonderful romantic novel. Afterwards, the boy grows up bright, turns out well, because his parents are good, and then says to them: “That novel of yours doesn’t suit me”. And we have a fine pair of tantrums. That’s not right, my children! You have to respect your children’s freedom. Allow God to work in their souls. You can’t go against that! What do you want? For them to be happy? Really? Well, don’t stand in their way. If you didn’t want your children to be happy, you wouldn’t be good mothers and fathers. (…) I say to you that you don’t have the right to take away your children’s freedom, when they desire something which is good. You can make some sort of suggestion to them, but nothing more. And, most definitely, you mustn’t do anything that would place their souls in danger. Because sometimes you mothers are so motherly that you think your thirty-year old children are still only three, you control them and manage them, you keep them at your beck and call. Poor things! You’ll end up making them unhappy invalids, of no use to anybody. You get upset if they fall in love, upset if they don’t choose the course you wanted for them, and yet again if they choose to dedicate themselves to a

wonderful universal enterprise, in the service of God… Everything upsets you! The fact is that at bottom your affection is a bit selfish. It’s as if you want a doll to play with and amuse yourselves. Forgive me for speaking so crudely, but it’s the truth! In thinking about your children, you’re wanting to enjoy the novel, so that the story invented by your imagination comes true. That’s not right! I’ve told you a number of times about something that happened to me many years ago. There was this lad, a “baby” of twenty-seven. Some baby! He had qualified as an engineer, and knocked on the doors of Opus Dei – which are tightly closed for those who want to get in: you have to push hard to open them – and since he was able to show that he had a vocation, he was allowed in. He went to tell his parents, who lived elsewhere, and when he returned he was in pieces; happier, even more convinced about his vocation, but in pieces. He said to me: “Do you know what my parents did? They went to talk to the Vicar General, and he, on hearing that God has given me a vocation to serve him in the world, without being a religious or a friar, told my parents: ‘Well, you know, when youngsters go to the big cities, that’s the sort of danger they face’…” May the Lord deliver us from Vicar Generals like that! Club Xénon (Lisbon), 4 November 1972

## On leaving the nest {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.8.12}

Father, our daughter has been sixteen years in Opus Dei, and she lives very far away. My wife and I accept that separation for the love of God. But I have friends who find it very difficult to accept their children’s vocation and they rebel against it… What can I tell them so they can accept it joyously? I would say to those friends of yours, affectionately –

because your question is very opportune, God bless you – I would say to them affectionately that they themselves presumably are not still living in their parents’ home, but left it and went, with a good and upright human love, to form another home. It can sometimes happen in families (I’m not saying that it happens in Chile) that there is a great celebration and everybody is very happy when a daughter gets married to a man of disreputable character, completely depraved, who – excuse me for saying this: those who don’t need to hear it, need not listen – is going to prostitute her. And he takes her off to the other side of the world. Everybody is happy: they’ve managed to get her “hitched”. They’re so happy! She’s finally “sorted”. In reality they’ve made a right mess of it, because she’s really just a remnant – the piece left over after the rest of the cloth has been sold. Tabancura (Santiago de Chile), 7 July 1974

## A greater affection {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.8.13}

Father, I’m the mother of a family, a Catholic family. Yesterday was my twenty-ninth wedding anniversary. I was reading in “Conversations” that an essential characteristic of Opus Dei is not to alter a person’s condition in society in any way. So how should we understand that youngsters can leave home at an early age and no longer live with their family, and that God blesses this and so do you? You tell me you’re the mother of a family, my daughter, and I have great regard for mothers of families. I love you a lot. To become the mother of a family, you had to tear yourself away a little from your parents’ home and start another home; and you haven’t stopped loving them for all that. Isn’t that

true? What makes you think that those who choose to serve our Lord in the middle of the world are going to detach themselves from their parents’ love? No way! Quite the opposite: it becomes warmer, they improve it, make it sublime, ennoble it. Don’t worry. Those who come to Opus Dei, whatever their social background, don’t relinquish that true and warm-hearted affection for their families. Rather, it becomes greater, stronger, more supernatural and more human. If some of your loved ones have joined Opus Dei, think about it carefully, and you will see that they love you more and better than before. You can be sure of that! Can’t you see us? Do you not see that everything is charity, everything is understanding, that we’re not concentrating on earthly things, that we know how to get on with everybody, make allowances for everybody, forgive everybody, love everybody? Non verbo neque lingua, sed opere et veritate (1 Jn 3:18) – not in word or speech but in deed and in truth. Anhembi Park (São Paulo), 2 June 1974

## The great rebellion {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.8.14}

Father, how can we be sure that the vocation comes from God, when our sons and daughters are still very young? If God calls them, leave them in peace, let them be serene, and pray for their perseverance. Vocation is never the enthusiasm of a moment: it always involves great sacrifice, selfmastery, and freeing oneself, besides, of all the things we have around us. That indeed is to be… What do you call youngsters, and those who are not so young, who oppose everything systematically? What do you call them around here?

Rebels, Father. Rebels! This is the great rebellion: that of the soul who doesn’t want to be an animal, who wants to have intimate contact with God – deep and total intimacy – and give his heart to Him completely. A vocation deserves a great deal of respect. If God chooses someone from your household for himself, you should be very happy, and very grateful. If some famous person, a head of state, were to invite a son of yours to have him at his side to do great things for his country, you’d be so happy about it... Well, how much happier you should be when God is the one who is calling them! Besides – I’ve said this many times – if the Lord chooses them, it’s because you yourselves have prepared them. If you don’t want God to call your children, bring them up to be wastrels, scoundrels, and thieves. Then God won’t call them. Gaztelueta (Bilbao), 12 October 1972

## Helping the children always {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.8.15}

What should we do to be completely detached from our children? Do you really want to be detached from your children? No! Explain what you mean! Explain yourself, though I understand what you’re trying to say: not to be a hindrance to your children in their journey towards God. Right? Is that what you want to say? Yes, Father. Even then, you mustn’t be detached: there’s so much you can do for them. As long as they’re still not saints – and as long as they’re here on earth they will be sinners, we are all sinners – you mustn’t abandon them: you have to pray and be vigilant for them, even though they may be far away. And

make small sacrifices, invoke the Blessed Virgin Mary, seek God’s presence and live with the joy of a Christian mother, thinking about those children of yours whom God has deigned to choose, as a sign of his special love for your family. Pozoalbero (Jerez de la Frontera), 12 November 1972

How can we help our children who have joined the Work? Don Alvaro is acting like the Holy Spirit for me. He’s whispered in my ear that “a mother’s task never ends”. That is what I tell all you mothers. All of you who have spoken to me privately have heard the same: as long as your children live, you have not finished your role with them. You should keep on helping them. A smile from you, a glance from you, a tear from you – but not many tears: just one, my daughter – and their heart is won over. Everything’s put right! How great is the power of mothers! You should be proud of being a mother. And besides, nature makes you more beautiful. Altoclaro (Caracas), 30 August 1974

## Your task isn’t finished {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.8.16}

Father, we have seven children. Nearly all of them are in the Work… That’s a good start! In each case we’ve been very happy with their decision. How can we keep helping them in their vocation? You haven’t been in any way a hindrance to their vocation. You have facilitated it indirectly, through your good example, along with your husband. And also, by praying for them, you have given them the nourishment of your soul as you did the nourishment of your blood. Just as between the two of you, you brought them to life with God’s help, so also afterwards you have contributed to their wishing

to give themselves to our Lord, for souls, and you should keep on helping them. They have started out, they need to finish. Do you recall how in the Gospel our Lord speaks about the man who began to build but could not finish? (cf. Lk 14:28-30). I say that all the characters in the Gospel laugh on seeing a failure of that kind. Your children won’t fail. With a mum and dad like you, they will not fail. Pray a lot for them. And they, with the same joy that you show, will serve the Lord with joy and with a hidden life. And with great interior fervour. Tabancura (Santiago de Chile), 5 July 1974

## Help them to be saints {#inedito.bright-and-cheerful-homes.8.17}

When a couple has several children in the Work, how can we help them persevere in their vocation? Not only to persevere, but to be saints. You haven’t finished your job. The work of a father and a mother is something extremely important. You partake in the creative power of God and, because of that, human love is something holy, noble, and good: a heartfelt joy which God wants some of us not to have. And we’re happy with that, very happy. But your work as parents doesn’t end there. You have to help them, as long as you live and as long as they live, to be saints, bearing in mind that for as long as we are on earth not one of us will be a saint. All we can do is struggle, struggle, and struggle. Agreed? And so your task isn’t finished, when you’ve given them life, an upbringing, education… No, no; as long as you live, you’ll go on being a place of rest for them, even though they have all the repose they need within themselves. And you are a great spiritual motor that sends them God’s strength to struggle, to conquer, to be saints.
