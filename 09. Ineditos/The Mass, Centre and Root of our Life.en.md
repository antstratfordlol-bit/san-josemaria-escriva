## The Mass, Centre and Root of our Life {#inedito.the-mass-centre-and-root-of-our-life}

THE CENTRE AND ROOT OF OUR LIFE A selection of texts from St Josemaría’s preaching THE CENTRE AND ROOT OF OUR LIFE

Seventy‐five years ago, on 28 March 1925, our Father received the sacrament of Holy Orders in the church of the Seminary of St Charles in Saragossa. From that moment until the end of his life on earth, he celebrated the Eucharistic Sacrifice with a faith and love which were greater each day. His day hinged around this divine, not human, action, where the priestly ministry encounters its fullness, its meaning, its centre and its effectiveness.1 One saw him so rapt in the Lord, Don Alvaro recalled, when he recited the ritual prayers, when he raised the Lord in his hands (…) Our Father was immersed in God, and we have to imitate him. I am not speaking only about historical memories, of things past. I am speaking to draw out consequences of greater love for God, of greater love for the Sacrifice of the Altar, of converting the whole day into a Mass...22

Our Founder spoke and wrote much about the Holy Mass. At times, in get‐togethers, he would complain, while smiling and calling us blockheads, because with our questions we were drawing out of him aspects of his intimacy with God and of his delicacy of a man in love during the Mass. With the occasion of this 75th anniversary, it has seemed fitting to put together some of these teachings, which are scattered throughout the pages of our internal publications and other writings, in order to have them available and to help everyone – priests and lay people – to follow the recommendation of Don Alvaro that we have just read: to live the Holy Sacrifice better, imitating our Father.

1 St Josemaría, Letter 8 August 1956, 18. 2 Blessed Alvaro, Get‐together, 28 March 1976 2

The Sacrifice of Christ and of the Church

I distinguish perfectly the institution of the Blessed Eucharist, which is a moment of the manifestation of divine and human love, from the Sacrifice on the wood of the Cross. In the Last Supper, Jesus Christ could suffer, although he had not yet suffered; on Calvary he is suffering, suffering with the gesture of an Eternal Priest. Jesus is there, fastened with nails, having sanctified the world with his footsteps, dying for love of each one of us: all his blood is the price of our soul, of every soul… He loves us so much – so very much! – that he wanted to suffer everything; and he was able to suffer much, because he was God and he was omnipotent. Christ suffers out of Love! In the Holy Mass, the victim and the one offering are the same, although Christ cannot now suffer — he is Christ glorious — and it is an unbloody sacrifice. Get‐together, 22 May 1970

A Trinitarian action When I am at the altar I act in persona Christi; I am another Christ renewing in an unbloody manner the Sacrifice of Calvary. I have with me all the children of God, the holy People of God, and not just an assembly. When I renew on the altar this outpouring of love, it is the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit, the Blessed Trinity, who bring about this marvel, using the priest as an instrument, as a minister. The cooperation of the Holy Spirit is something definitive, in order to be able to consecrate. Until recently, we priests recited an invocation to the Third Person of the Blessed Trinity before the Lavabo. Do you remember? Veni, Sanctificator, omnipotens aeterne Deus, et benedic hoc sacrificium tuo sancto nomini praeparatum (Come, Sanctifier, almighty eternal God, and bless this sacrifice prepared for your holy name. I continue invoking the Holy Spirit in my heart). Later, a little before Communion, I recite with great fervour this other prayer which the current rite has omitted: Domine lesu Christe, Fili Dei vivi, qui ex voluntate Patris, cooperante Spiritu Sancto, per mortem tuam mundum vivificasti... (Lord Jesus Christ, Son of the living God, who by the will of the Father, with the cooperation of the Holy Spirit, gave life to the world through your death)… Do you see? The Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit are acting divinely in the Sacrifice of the Mass. We offer the Son to the Father, as on Calvary, with the cooperation of the Holy Spirit. Love the Most Holy Trinity, my children! Love this one God who is with us. Love this action of the Holy Spirit, which is at the same time the action of the Father and the Son. Get‐together, 19 March 1971

The three divine Persons act together in the holy sacrifice of the altar. This is why I like to repeat the final words of the Collect, the Secret and Postcommunion: “Through Jesus Christ, your Son, our Lord”, we pray to the Father, “who lives and reigns with you in the unity of the Holy Spirit, God, forever and ever. Amen.” In the Mass, our prayer to God the Father is constant. The priest represents the eternal high Priest, Jesus Christ, who is, at the same time, the victim offered in this sacrifice. And the action of the Holy Spirit in the Mass is truly present, although in a mysterious manner. “By the power of the Holy Spirit”, writes St John Damascene, “the transformation of the bread into the body of Christ takes place” (De fide orthodoxa, 13). (…) The three divine Persons are present in the Sacrifice of the Altar. By the will of the Father, with the cooperation of the Holy Spirit, the Son offers himself in a redemptive sacrifice. We learn how to personalise our relationship with the most Blessed Trinity, one God in three Persons: three divine Persons in the unity of God’s substance, in the unity of his love and of his sanctifying action. (…) The Mass is, I insist, an action of God, of the Trinity. It is not a merely human event. The priest who celebrates fulfils the desire of our Lord, lending his body and his voice to the divine action. He acts, not in his own name, but in persona et in nomine Christi: in the Person of Christ and in his name. Because of the Blessed Trinity’s love for man, the presence of Christ in the Eucharist brings all graces to the Church and to mankind. This is the sacrifice announced by the prophet Malachy: “From the rising of the sun to its setting my name is great among the nations, and a fragrant sacrifice and a pure offering is made to me in all places” (Mal 1:11). It is the sacrifice of Christ, offered to the Father with the cooperation of the Holy Spirit – an offering of infinite value, which perpetuates the work of redemption in us and surpasses the sacrifices of the old law. The holy Mass brings us face to face with one of the central mysteries of our faith, because it is the gift of the Blessed Trinity to the Church. It is because of this that we can consider the Mass as the centre and the source of a Christian’s spiritual life. It is the aim of all the sacraments (Cf. St Thomas, STh III, q. 65, a. 3). The life of grace, into which we are brought by baptism, and which is increased and strengthened by confirmation, grows to its fullness in the Mass. “When we participate in the Eucharist,” writes St Cyril of Jerusalem, “we are made spiritual by the divinising action of the Holy Spirit, who not only makes us share in Christ’s life, as in baptism, but makes us entirely Christ‐like, incorporating us into the fullness of Christ Jesus” (Catecheses, 22, 3). This outpouring of the Holy Spirit unites us to Christ and makes us acknowledge that we are children of God. The Paraclete, who is Love, teaches us to saturate our life with the virtue of charity. Thus consummati in unum: “made one with Christ” (John 17:23), we can be among men what the Eucharist is for us, in the words of St Augustine: “a sign of unity, a bond of love” (In Ioannis Evangelium tractatus 26, 13). (…) To correspond to such great love, we must give ourselves completely, in body and in soul. We hear God, we talk to him, we see him, we taste him. And when words are not enough, we sing, urging our tongue – Pange, lingua! – to proclaim in the presence of all mankind the greatness of the Lord. Christ is passing by, 85‐87

The priest, Ipse Christus What is the identity of the priest? That of Christ. All of us Christians can and should be not just other Christs, alter Christus, but Christ himself: ipse Christus! But in the priest this happens in a direct way, by virtue of the sacrament. To accomplish so great a work — the work of redemption — Christ is always present in his Church, especially in her liturgical celebrations. He is present in the Sacrifice of the Mass, not only in the person of his minister, “the same now offering through the ministry of priests, who formerly offered himself on the Cross, but especially under the eucharistic species” (Second Vatican Council, Const. Sacrosanctum Concilium, 7). The sacrament of Orders, in effect, equips the priest to lend Our Lord his voice, his hands, his whole being. It is Jesus Christ who, in the Holy Mass, through the words of the consecration, changes the substance of the bread and wine into his Body, his Soul, his Blood and his Divinity. This is the source of the priest’s incomparable dignity. It is a greatness which is on loan: it is completely compatible with my own littleness. I pray to God our Lord to give all of us priests the grace to perform holy things in a holy way, to reflect in every aspect of our lives the wonders of the greatness of God. “Those of us who celebrate the mysteries of the Passion of Our Lord must imitate what we perform. And then the host will take our place before God because we render ourselves hosts” (St Gregory the Great, Dialog. 4, 59). If you ever come across a priest who apparently does not seem to be following the teaching of the Gospel — do not judge him, let God judge him — bear in mind that if he celebrates Mass validly, with the intention of consecrating, Our Lord will still come down into his hands, however unworthy they are. Where could you find greater selfsurrender and annihilation? Here it is greater than in Bethlehem or on Calvary. Why? Because Jesus’ heart, filled with a desire to redeem, does not want anyone to be able to say that he has not been called. He goes out to meet those who do not seek Him. That is Love! There is no other explanation for it. When it comes to speaking of Christ’s Love, we are lost for words. He has so abased himself that he accepts everything; he exposes himself to everything — to sacrilege, to blasphemy and to the cold indifference of so many people — in order to offer even one man the chance of hearing the beating of his Heart in his wounded side. Here we have the priest’s identity: he is a direct and daily instrument of the saving grace which Christ has won for us. If you grasp this, if you meditate on it in the active silence of prayer, how could you ever think of the priesthood in terms of renunciation? It is a gain, an incalculable gain. Our mother Mary, the holiest of creatures — only God is holier — brought Jesus Christ into the world just once; priests bring him on earth, to our soul and body, every day: Christ comes to be our food, to give us life, to be, even now, a pledge of future life. Homily, A Priest Forever, 13 April 1973, In Love with the Church, 38‐39

The priesthood is the greatest thing on earth. It is sufficient just to consider the miracle involved in making Jesus come down to earth every day. Our Heavenly Mother — how much we should love her: greater than her, only God! — made him come down only once: fíat mihi secundum verbum tuum! Let it be done unto me according to your word (Luke 1:38). Love the Mass, my sons, which is the principal purpose of your ordination, where the whole priestly ministry encounters its fullness, its meaning, its centre and its effectiveness. “The priest performs two acts: the principal one has to do with the true Body of Christ; and the secondary one has to do with his Mystical Body. This second act or ministry depends on the former, but not vice versa” (St Thomas, STh., Suppl, q. 36, a. 2 ad 1). We should have a real hunger to celebrate, and to prepare ourselves for it with acts of Faith, Love and Hope: desiderio desideravi hoc pascha manducare vobiscum — I have earnestly desired to eat this Passover with you (Luke 22:15). An ardent desire to unite ourselves with Jesus, Priest and Victim: with these dispositions we will approach the altar every day. Our Lady was conceived immaculate in order to receive our Lord: reflect on it. Haec quotiescumque feceritis, in mei memoriam facietis (Missale Romanum, Canon Missae): As often as you do this, do it in memory of me. The priest is another Christ. Consider, my sons, how we should celebrate; we are carrying out a divine action, and the Angels are present. Letter, 8 August 1956, 17‐18

All of us priests are Christ. I lend Our Lord my voice, my hands, my body, my soul: I give him everything. It is he who says: this is my Body, this is my Blood. He is the one who consecrates. If not, I would not be able to do it. There, the divine Sacrifice of Calvary is renewed in an unbloody manner, in such a way that I am there in persona Christi, taking the place of Christ. The priest as a specific individual, disappears: Father Fred, Father Ted or Josemaría... No, sir! He is Christ. Then I remember that there are many people of all colours, of all languages, of all races, in every corner of the world who, when something costs them an effort because they have to put up with the difficult character of their husband or wife, of their children or their parents, of their colleagues or friends or neighbours, know how to put up with it, to behave in a Christian way, and so that it will be easier to win out, they offer it up to God. There are many – may God bless them!

– who say: I unite myself with the intentions of the Father. And that is like a part of my Mass. I know that many people do this. For this reason not only am I Christ, but I carry this whole load of human sacrifice united to the Sacrifice of Christ. What joy! Get‐together, 10 May 1974

The different parts of the Mass When I arrive at the foot of the altar to start Mass, (…) I feel young, filled with your youth and with God’s eternal youth, and I feel my heart pounding with all the powerful and ardent blood of twenty‐yearolds (…) I go up to the altar with eager longing, and more than placing my hands on it, I embrace it affectionately and I kiss it like someone in love, for that is what I am: in love! I would be crazy if I werenʹt. Get‐together, 17 March 1969

Introibo ad altare Dei: I go up the altar of God, to the God of my youth and my joy. Confiteor: contrition, desires of purification. Kisses of love to the altar, to Christ. The Introit and the Kyries. Glory to God the Father and to God the Son and to God the Holy Spirit. Thank you, Lord, for your great glory! The Epistle and the Gospel, lights from the Holy Spirit which set the soul on fire: with that kissing of the holy book, which is a kiss to the Word of God, to Christ himself, the divine Word. And the Creed: the faith. The offering of the Victim. The lavabo: purification, purification. Suscipe, Sancta Trinitas: receive, Lord, the Sacrifice, in honour of your Son, in honour of the Blessed Virgin and of your Saints, in whose 8 intercession we believe and trust. Orate, fratres: asking for the prayer of all, in communion of intentions. The hymn of the Angels: Sanctus, Sanctus, Sanctus! Letter 8 August 1956, 19

Through the communion of saints, all Christians receive grace from every Mass that is celebrated, regardless of whether there is an attendance of thousands, or whether it is only a boy with his mind on other things who is there to serve. In either case, heaven and earth join with the Angels of the Lord to sing: Sanctus, Sanctus, Sanctus... I adore and praise with the Angels – it is not difficult, because I know that, as I celebrate the holy Mass, they surround me, adoring the Blessed Trinity. And I know that in some way the Blessed Virgin is there, because of her intimate relationship with the most Blessed Trinity and because she is the Mother of Christ, of his flesh and blood – the Mother of Jesus Christ, perfect God and perfect man. Jesus was conceived in the womb of Mary most holy, not through the intervention of man, but by the power of the Holy Spirit alone. In his veins runs the blood of his Mother, the blood that is offered in the sacrifice of redemption, on Calvary and in the Mass. Thus we begin the canon, with the confidence of children of God, calling him our most loving Father: clementissime. We pray for the Church and for all those who are a part of the Church – the pope, our families, our friends and companions. And a Catholic, with his heart open to all men, will pray for all men, because no one can be excluded from his love. We ask God to hear our prayers. We call on the memory of the glorious ever‐Virgin Mary and of a handful of men who were among the first to follow Christ and to die for Him, and we recall our union with them. Christ is passing by, 89‐90

Quam oblationem… Right from the start I have harboured the desire – which is a display of love – that, as soon as it were possible, your sisters would not just prepare the hosts and the wine, the material of the Sacrifice, but even grow the wheat and cultivate the vines to produce it: ut nobis Corpus et Sanguis fiat dilectissimi Filii tui Domini nostri Iesu Christi (Missale Romanum, Canon Missae): so that it may become for us the Body and Blood of your beloved Son our Lord Jesus Christ. The Consecration: This is my Body. My Body, Jesus. Letter 8 August 1956, 19

Quam oblationem... The moment of the consecration draws near. Now, in the Mass, it is Christ who acts again, through the priest: “This is my Body”... “This is the chalice of my Blood.” Jesus is with us! The transubstantiation is a renewal of the miracle of God’s infinite love. When that moment takes place again today, let us tell our Lord, without any need for words, that nothing will be able to separate us from him; that, as he puts himself into our hands, defenceless, under the fragile appearances of bread and wine, he has made us his willing slaves: praesta meae mentí de te vivere, et te illi semper dulce sapere (Adoro te devote), Make me live always through you, and taste the sweetness of your love. Christ is passing by, 90

It does not bother me to tell you alone – as if the others did not hear it – that each day in Holy Mass, without the need for words, I ask our Lord to increase my faith. When I hold him in my hands, I tell him in my heart: I believe that you are here, hidden beneath these sacramental species, with your Body, with your Blood, with your Soul and with your Divinity. And I add: adauge nobis fidem, spem, caritatem! Increase our faith, our hope, our love. You can see how I try to make my faith grow: asking our Lord for it. Do the same yourself. Because one learns to walk by walking, and to have more faith, by striving to have it. Get‐together, 15 April 1973

More prayers, because we human beings almost always feel the need to ask for things – prayers for our deceased brothers and sisters, for ourselves. We have brought all our weaknesses, our lack of faithfulness. The weight is heavy, but he wants to bear it for us and with us. The canon ends with another invocation to the Blessed Trinity: Per Ipsum, et cum Ipso, et in Ipso...: Through Christ, and with Christ, and in Christ, who is all our love, in the unity of the Holy Spirit, all honour and glory is yours, almighty Father, for ever and ever. Christ is passing by, 90

The priest, if he has true priestly spirit, if he is a man with interior life, will never feel himself alone. No one can have a heart as much in love as he does! He is the man of Love, the representative among men of Love made man. He lives through Jesus Christ, for Jesus Christ, with Jesus Christ and in Jesus Christ. It is a divine reality which moves me through and through, when every day, raising and having in my hands the Chalice and the Sacred Host, I repeat slowly, savouring them, the words of the Canon: Per Ipsum, et cum Ipso et in Ipso... I live through him, with him, in him, for him and for all souls. I live by his Love and for his Love, in spite of my personal wretchedness. And in spite of this wretchedness, perhaps because of it, my Love is a love which is renewed each day. Get‐together, 10 April 1969

I have told all my children a thousand times that the Mass is the centre and the root of our interior life: and there is every reason for saying this. Per Ipsum, et cum Ipso, et in Ipso: because he is the Way, the Mediator. In him is everything; apart from him, nothing. In Jesus Christ, and taught by him, we dare to say — audemus dicere — Pater noster. Divine filiation, the foundation of our whole spirituality! The Communion: in order to be immersed in God, identified with Jesus. And to be able to reserve the Blessed Sacrament, to administer it, to adore it. Mane nobiscum, quoniam advesperascit, et inclinata est iam dies. Et intravit cum illis (Luke 24:29) — Stay with us, for it is toward evening and the day is now far spent. So he went in to stay with them. Afterwards, the blessing of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit upon all. When I reached this point in my preaching to priests – when I was young –, I used to get angry with those who were in a hurry to finish. Short Masses! Anyone who doesn’t love a long Mass doesn’t love Jesus Christ. Nevertheless, I advise you, for the sake of charity, not to exceed thirty minutes. That is sufficient time to celebrate calmly and with love. There is no need to rush in reading the prayers. Nor is it a matter either of pronouncing everything emphatically, but rather appropriately, with the right tone of voice, so that what you are saying can be understood, and it is obvious that you understand and that you love. And so, when someone attends Mass with a missal, they can follow us perfectly. Letter 8 August 1956, 19‐20.

Divine work At the age of sixty‐five I have made a marvellous discovery. I love to celebrate the Holy Mass, but yesterday I found it a tremendous effort. How hard it was! I then saw that the Mass is truly Opus Dei, work, as it was hard work for Jesus Christ in his first Mass, the Cross. I saw that the work of the priest, to celebrate Mass, involves the effort of bringing about the Holy Eucharist. He experiences pain, and joy, and tiredness. I felt in my flesh the exhaustion of a divine work. Get‐together, 24 October 1966

For Christ, his first Mass, the Cross, also cost him effort. His most holy Humanity resisted opening his arms on the Cross, with the gesture of an eternal Priest. The celebration of the Holy Sacrifice has never cost me so much effort as it did that day, when I felt that the Mass too is Opus Dei. It gave me great joy, but it also left me exhausted. I am a doctor in Theology, and a member of the Pontifical Roman Academy of Theology…; but one only sees this when God wants to grant it. And then, when he relates it, he says so ashamed that he had not been able to understand it until then. But that’s not important. Have you not heard St Paul say: “I will all the more gladly boast of my weaknesses, that the power of Christ may rest upon me” (2 Cor 12:9)? (...) It is not a matter of feelings, my children. I don’t feel anything: I go against the grain almost always. It is not to feel: it is to live with love and with faith. Get‐together, 9 November 1966

How can we compare the Mass with work? Look, if the Mass is — as it really is — the Sacrifice of Calvary, consider the efforts of Christ until he reaches the Cross: how they fasten him to the wood with nails, how much he suffers! That indeed is truly pain and work. I know priests who have become exhausted after celebrating Holy Mass, as if they had worked long hours in the fields. And they assure us truly that the celebration of Mass is Opus Dei, work of God. My son, make the comparison, for it is a good one: the Mass is a great divine work. Through it, Our Lord continues dispensing the fruits of Redemption to all souls. Get‐together, 4 November 1972 12

Father, do you end up tired after the Mass? On some days, yes, but that is not because I am old; it is because I am Christ and I feel the weight of Cross. Thanks be to God, I don’t lack Cyreneans. I am aware that it is you who carry the Cross; and in this way I can... I never feel alone at the Altar. In addition to not being myself, I go forward with strength, because I am supported by the sacrifices and the victories, and the prayers, of everyone. So we meet each morning at the altar. I have answered you, and in the way you were expecting. It is Christ who gets tired. Get‐together, 10 June 1974

My children, in the Mass I am never alone; I feel very well accompanied. Although only one of your brothers serves, I celebrate Mass with the whole People of God. I would say even more: I am also with those who have not yet come close to Our Lord, with those who are still some distance away and are not yet of his flock; these too I have in my heart. And I feel surrounded by all the birds which fly across the blue sky, some of which even gaze face to face at the sun, like the eagle you have on the shield of Mexico. And surrounded by all the animals on earth: the rational ones, like us humans, although we sometimes lose our reason, and the irrational ones, those which scamper across the surface of the earth or live in its bowels, hidden from the world. That is how I feel when I renew the Holy Sacrifice of the Cross. It is not a renewal of the Last Supper, but the Sacrifice of Calvary! Get‐together, 22 May 1970

The Mass and the interior life

I have always taught you, my beloved daughters and sons, that the root and the centre of your spiritual life is the Holy Sacrifice of the Altar, in which Christ the Priest renews his Sacrifice on Calvary, in adoration, honour, praise and thanksgiving to the Blessed Trinity. In this way, very united to Jesus in the Eucharist, we will attain a constant presence of God in the midst of the ordinary occupations proper to the situation of each one on this earthly pilgrimage, seeking Our Lord at all times and in all things. By having in our soul the same sentiments as Christ on the Cross, we will make of our whole life an unceasing act of reparation, an assiduous petition and a permanent sacrifice for all mankind, because our Lord will give us a supernatural instinct to purify all our actions, to raise them to the order of grace and to turn them into an instrument of apostolate. Only in this way will we be contemplative souls in the middle of the world, as our vocation requires, and we will be truly priestly souls, so that everything in us will be a continuous act of praise to God. Letter 2 February 1945, 11

Priestly soul I would like to tell you that God waits for us in every moment of our life, in our daily work: for each of us in his own. For me too, but no more than for you. Only at the altar, more than for you. But I have to console you with a marvellous truth. I say I am more than you at the altar because I am a priest of Jesus Christ, having received the Holy Sacrament of Orders. But you have a priestly soul. St Peter says so, and it is also sure, general, doctrine of the Doctors of the Church. You all have a priestly soul; you can offer God spiritual hosts, sacrifices; you can unite yourselves to the priest at the altar. As for me, I want the altar only for myself, because there I am not Josemaría; at the altar I am Jesus Christ. Every priest is Jesus Christ. I do not say: this is the body of Jesus Christ, but this is my Body, because I lend the Lord my voice, my mind, and my hands. I am Christ! At that moment, my union with him is greater than yours, but I unite myself with him through you. Get‐together, 27 May 1974 14

All of you, my daughters and sons, have a priestly soul, rooted in the sacramental character of Baptism and Confirmation. A priestly soul, which you put into action not only when you participate in liturgical worship – and above all in the Eucharistic Sacrifice, the root and centre of our interior life – but in all the activities of your life. You exercise this priestly spirit with a fully lay mentality when you offer to God your work, your rest, the joys and setbacks of your day, the holocaust of your bodies given up through the effort of constant service. All of this is a “living sacrifice, holy and acceptable to God; this is your spiritual worship” (Rom 12:1). Letter 6 May 1945, 27

Making our day a Mass All the works of men are done as if on an altar, and each one of you, in that union of contemplative souls that is your day, in some way says ‘his Mass’, which lasts twenty‐four hours, in expectation of the Mass to follow, which will last another twenty‐four hours, and so on until the end of our lives. Meditation, 19 March 1968, In dialogue with the Lord, 86

We must live especially the spirit of making the Mass the centre of our interior life, in such a way that we know how to be with Christ, to keep him company throughout the day, closely united to his sacrifice. All our work has this meaning. And this will lead us throughout the day to tell Our Lord that we offer ourselves to God the Father, through Him, with Him, and in Him, uniting ourselves to all his intentions, in the name of all creatures. If we live like this, our whole day will be a Mass, from the moment we wake up until we go to bed. And then, how easy it will be to bear the blows! We will be filled with joy and serenity all the time, and it will be much easier to remedy things which the Lord permits, and to be very effective. Brief Circle, 27 May 1962

On the stone of a large window which used to be in the Villa Vecchia in Rome, and which now surrounds the door of a room which we call the Coso (…), I had engraved those marvellous words of St Peter: Vos autem genus electum, regale sacerdotium, gens sancta, populus acquisitionis... 15

Here is the basis for the whole theology of the royal priesthood which all of us Christians have, and which is different from the ministerial priesthood, proper to those of us who have received the Sacrament of Orders. My children, you can, then, offer the Mass in the morning and at night, under the protection of our Lady — sub tuum praesidium — and always make of your day a Mass. A Mass which is at odds with disloyalty, with not guarding your sight, with omitting the prayer, with neglecting the presence of God, with not being mortified and penitent. Get‐together, 12 February 1975

To celebrate and participate better in the Holy Mass

My child, think now about the Holy Mass: about how we should celebrate it or attend it. Consider that the angels are present. Remember that you are carrying out or taking part in something divine. See how on the altar Christ once again offers himself for you and for me. And you will feel a great desire to imitate his humility, his self‐abasement in the Host; and you will be filled with acts of thanksgiving, of adoration, of desires of making reparation, and of petitions. And you will offer yourself, with your arms outstretched, like another Christ, ipse Christus, ready to be nailed to the sweet wood, for love of souls. Meditation, 14 April 1960

A very personal encounter Within a few minutes, with this son who accompanies me, I will go to celebrate Holy Mass: to have a very personal encounter with the Love of my soul. And this son of mine will remind me — when he responds with the words of the liturgy — that I will be going up to the altar of God, who gives joy to my youth. Because I am young, and I will always be so, since my youth is that of God, who is eternal. With this love I will never be able to feel old. I will then kiss the altar: with kisses of love. When you come to tell Our Lord, perhaps without the sound of words: Lord, I love you, I believe that you are here; in those moments, also praise the Most Blessed Trinity: the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit, and invoke Mary and Joseph because (...) in one way or another, they are present in the Tabernacle, as they were in Bethlehem and in Nazareth. Unite the Eucharistic presence of Jesus to the Blessed Trinity (…) and remember the trinity on earth: the Mother of Christ and that most perfect man, St Joseph. Without words, but with the beating of your heart. Do not forget it! In front of the Tabernacle, 6 June 1974
